Rivers, Doabs & Fortress Outposts

The geography of Punjab is defined by interfluvial tracts known as Doabs (literally "two waters") coined systematically by Emperor Akbar from the names of bordering rivers, built upon ancient riverine crossings.

Bist-Jalandhar Doab Beas & Sutlej

Bist Doab

The fertile tract situated between the Beas and the Sutlej. Historic anchor points include Jalandhar, Sultanpur Lodhi (major Lodhi commercial and spiritual hub), Kapurthala, and Hoshiarpur.

Key Sites: Dholbaha, Ropar apex, Sultanpur Lodhi
Bari Doab (Majha) Beas & Ravi

Bari Doab

Situated between the Beas and the Ravi. Constitutes the core historical region of Majha. Center of early urbanization, Harappa, Lahore, Dipalpur, and Amritsar.

Key Sites: Harappa, Lahore, Dipalpur garrison
Rechna Doab Ravi & Chenab

Rechna Doab

Tract enclosed by the Ravi and the Chenab. Encompasses the ancient capital of King Menander I (Sagala / Sialkot), Gujranwala, and Faisalabad.

Key Sites: Sagala (Sialkot), Sheikhupura, Tulamba
Jech (Chaj) Doab Chenab & Jhelum

Jech Doab

The zone between the Chenab and the Jhelum. Location of the epic clash between Porus and Alexander the Great, Gujrat, Bhera, and Shahpur.

Key Sites: Hydaspes battleground, Jalalpur, Bhera
Sindh Sagar Doab Jhelum & Indus

Sindh Sagar Doab

The vast tract between the Jhelum and the mighty Indus (Sindhu). Encompasses the Potohar Plateau, Salt Range, Taxila, and the later Rohtas Fort.

Key Sites: Taxila, Katas Raj, Salt Range, Mankera
Cis-Sutlej / Malwa Sutlej to Ghaggar/Yamuna

Sirhind / Malwa Plains

Lies south of the Sutlej river. Governed as Sarkar Sirhind during the Sultanate and Mughal eras. Major frontier strongpoints at Sirhind, Bhatinda, and Bhatner.

Key Sites: Sirhind, Bhatinda Fort, Sunam, Rohira

🏰 Citadel Network: Key Medieval Fortresses of Punjab

Sirhind Fort & Sarkar

The strategic military hinge connecting Punjab to Delhi. Bahlul Khan Lodhi established his initial power base as governor of Sirhind before ascending the throne of Delhi in 1451 CE.

Bhatinda Fort (Qila Mubarak)

Constructed in Kushan/Gupta brickwork style; famous as the prison of Razia Sultana in 1240 CE during the rebel governor Altunia's revolt. A bastion against desert and riverine raiders.

Dipalpur & Multan Citadels

The supreme defensive march against the Mongol Chagatai invasions. Maintained by Ghiyas-ud-din Balban, Prince Muhammad (who died fighting Mongols here in 1285), and Ghazi Malik (Ghiyasuddin Tughluq).

    Punjab History Civil Services Quick Digest

    Exam-essential high yield notes: Prehistory to Lodhis (1526 CE)

    1. Ropar Stratigraphy & Independent India

    Excavated by Dr. Y.D. Sharma (1952–53). Key sequence: Harappan → PGW → NBPW → Kushan → Gupta → Medieval. Yielded a unique oval pit grave with a human buried along with a dog.

    2. Sanghol (Uchha Pind) Stupa Cache

    In 1985, 117 carved Mathura red sandstone railing pillars (yakshis, salabhanjikas, lotus medallions) were uncovered stacked neatly near the wheel-spoke Buddhist stupa foundation (1st–2nd c. CE).

    3. Mongol March Defense: The Punjab Warden Line

    The Delhi Sultanate maintained a triple-tier defense line anchored at Multan, Dipalpur, and Lahore. Balban stationed his eldest son Prince Muhammad (martyred near Lahore in 1285). Later, Ghazi Malik (Ghiyasuddin Tughluq) earned the title of 29-time victor against Mongols from his Dipalpur headquarters.

    4. Sirhind's Rise & The Lodhi Foundation

    Bahlul Khan Lodhi was the governor of Sirhind and Lahore under the weak Sayyids. In 1451 CE, utilizing his Afghan tribal confederacy, he marched from Punjab to seize Delhi, founding the First Afghan Dynasty (Lodhis).

    5. 1526 Panipat Prelude in Punjab

    Daulat Khan Lodhi, governor of Lahore, along with Alam Khan Lodhi, invited Babur to unseat Ibrahim Lodhi. Babur took Lahore and Sialkot (1524), neutralized Daulat Khan at Malot fort in Hoshiarpur (1526), and advanced directly to Panipat.
