1. Ancient Names of Punjab & River Nomenclature Nomenclature

Different Names of Punjab Through the Ages

  • Etymology: Derived from the Persian (Farsi) language: Panj (Five) + Aab (Land of Waters).
  • Sapta Sindhu: "Land of Seven Rivers" during the Vedic Age, centered around the Indus River and its tributaries; considered the "Cradle of Early Indian Civilization & Culture".
  • Panchanada: Used in the Mahabharata and Ramayana, referring to the "Five Rivers" region.
  • Trigarta Kingdom: (800–400 BCE) Ruled by Katoch Kings; means "the land of the three rivers" (Sutlej, Ravi, and Beas).
  • Tak Pradesh: Jalandhar Doab region was called Tak Pradesh because it was ruled by the Tak Tribe in ancient times.
  • Brahmavarta: In the ancient Vedic text Manusmriti, Punjab was called Brahmavarta, defined as the area between the Saraswati and Drishadvati rivers where the Rishis composed the Vedas.
  • Pentopotamia: Name given by the Greeks (from Penta = five and Potamia = rivers), coined by ancient historians like Herodotus and Arrian during Alexander's invasion.
  • Sekia: Used by Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) in the 7th century CE for the land between the Sindh (Indus) and Beas rivers.
  • Vahika: Used by ancient grammarian Panini in his work Ashtadhyayi for the region between the Indus and Sutlej rivers.

Textual Occurrences of the Word "Punjab" in Books

  • Ibn Battuta's "Rehla": Earliest known documentation of the word "Punjab" (written during the era of Muhammad bin Tughlaq). He also termed Abohar as the "first town of Hindustan".
  • Tariq-e-Sher Shah Suri (1580): Written by Abbas Khan Sarwani; the first Indian book to mention the name "Punjab", describing the fort built by Sher Khan in Punjab.
  • Ain-e-Akbari (by Abul Fazal): In Volume 1, "Punjab" is mentioned as a Suba divided into the Parganas of Lahore and Multan. In Volume 2, Abul Fazal alternatively used the word "Panchanand".
  • Tuzk-i-Jahangiri: Mughal Emperor Jahangir explicitly used the word Panjab in his autobiography.

2. Punjab During the Stone Age & Harappan Civilization Archaeological Framework

Stone Age in Punjab

  • Palaeolithic Age (Early Stone Age): Sites discovered in the valley of River Soan in Punjab (now in Pakistan) and in Indian Punjab at Daulatpur, Dher Majra, Hoshiarpur, Dhang, Nalagarh, Pirthan, Khokra-ka-Choa, Rampura, Sirsa, Bhud, and Dholbaha. Riwat (Pakistan) is a well-known Upper Palaeolithic site dating back ~45,000 years ago.
  • Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age): No site on the Indian side of Punjab has been discovered so far. One Mesolithic site, Mahiwala 1, has been discovered in the Thal Desert of Punjab in Pakistan.
  • Neolithic Age (New Stone Age): Human settlements developed after 4000 BCE. In Bahawalpur district, settlements appeared between 3800 to 3200 BCE. In 1963, polished stones related to Neolithic culture were found at Ror and Dibbar in the Beas-Banganga valley.

Proto-Historic & Harappan Milestones

  • ASI Foundation: In 1861, the British created the Archaeological Survey of India with Alexander Cunningham as its first Director General.
  • Harappa Discovery: In 1921 AD, Daya Ram Sahni excavated Harappa in Montgomery district in West Punjab (Pakistan).
  • Outermost Sites: Manda, Ropar, and Chandigarh. Urban Harappan settlements were centered in the southern parts and the sub-Himalayan belt. Proto-historic settlements cultivating wheat and barley were found near Chandigarh.
  • Meluhha (Mesopotamian Inscriptions): Mesopotamians called the Indus Valley Civilization "Meluhha". Sargon of Akkad (~2300 BC) recorded ships arriving from Meluhha (IVC), Magan (Oman), and Dilmun (Bahrain).

3. History of Punjabi Language & Punjab in the Vedic Age Vedic Literature

Evolution of the Punjabi Language

  • Linguistic Family: Originates from the Indo-European language family (which includes Persian and Latin).
  • Direct Ancestry: Evolved from Sauraseni Apabhramsa (Vedic Sanskrit → Classical Sanskrit → Pali → Sauraseni Apabhramsa → Punjabi).
  • Independent Emergence: Emerged as an independent tongue around the 11th century CE, with early written evidence from Naths and Jogis.
  • Father of Punjabi Literature: Popularized in the 12th century by Fariduddin Ganj Shakar (Baba Farid), revered as the first major Punjabi poet.

Punjab During the Vedic Period

  • Aryan Settlement: Aryans settled in Punjab before 3500 BCE; the Rigveda was composed on the banks of Punjab's rivers.
  • Mata Kaushalya's Birthplace: Guru Gobind Singh Ji in Dasam Granth connected Ghuram (Patiala district) with the birth site of Mata Kaushalya, mother of Lord Rama.
  • Madra Kingdom: Prominent epic kingdom centered in Punjab with its capital at Sagala (modern Sialkot). King Shalya (brother of Madri, uncle to Pandavas) commanded the Kaurava forces in the Kurukshetra War.
  • Battle of Ten Kings (Dasrajan Yudh): Recorded in the 7th Mandala of Rigveda; fought on the banks of River Parushni (Ravi). King Sudas of the Bharata tribe defeated a confederacy of 10 kings.
  • Panini: The great Sanskrit grammarian was born in Shalatura (near modern-day Attock/Lahore) in the Gandhara kingdom at the confluence of the Indus and Kabul rivers.

4. Persian, Greek & Mauryan Dominance Imperial Clashes

Achaemenid Persian Empire (550 – 330 BCE)

Cyrus the Great invaded Punjab c. 550 BC. King Gustasp finally defeated Punjab and Sindh in 516 BC. His son Darius I annexed Punjab into the Persian Empire, organizing it into Satrapies (Sindh and Punjab formed the 20th Satrapy, the richest and most populous of the empire). Persian rule introduced the Aramaic Semitic script, directly nurturing the indigenous Kharosthi script in northwestern India. His successor Xerxes was halted from expanding due to wars with Greece.

Invasion of Alexander the Great (326 BCE)

Alexander captured Bukhara (350 BC) and Balkh (330 BC). In 326 BC, he defeated King Ambi of Taxila. He subsequently fought King Porus (ruler of the Paurava kingdom between Hydaspes/Jhelum and Acesines/Chenab) in the epic Battle of the Hydaspes at Nikaia. He founded the city of Bucephala on the Jhelum in memory of his warhorse. His soldiers mutinied at River Hyphasis (Beas), forcing his retreat. He nominated his commander Seleucus, appointed General Eudamas as head of his army and chief ruler of Punjab, and made Python ruler of Sindh (ruled until 316 BC). Alexander died in Babylon; his body was taken to Memphis and buried at Alexandria. Ancient Greek accounts of Punjab are recorded in Strabo's Geographica (Book 15) and Arrian's Anabasis of Alexander.

Mauryan Empire (322 – 185 BCE)

Vishakhadatta's Mudrarakshasa records that Chandragupta Maurya first took Punjab by defeating the Nanda forces. He allied with Himalayan King Parvatka (identified as Porus in the Jain text Parishishtaparvan). In 305 BCE, Chandragupta defeated Seleucus Nicator, securing Afghanistan and Baluchistan in exchange for 500 war elephants. In 303 BCE, Seleucus cemented peace by giving his daughter Helena in marriage and dispatching ambassador Megasthenes (author of Indica) to Pataliputra. Chanakya taught economics and authored Arthashastra at Takshashila University (Taxila, Rawalpindi). Under Mauryan administration, modern Punjab lay within the Gandhara province (capital Taxila). Bindusara hosted Greek ambassador Daimachus. Ashoka served as Governor of Gandhara, introduced the Brahmi Script to Punjab, and erected the great Buddhist stupa (known as SGL 5) at Uccha Pind (Sanghol, Fatehgarh Sahib). Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) was extensively utilized.

5. Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Kushanas, Guptas, Vardhanas & Huns Dynastic Succession

Indo-Greeks, Shakas & Parthians

  • Indo-Greeks (Bactrians): King Demetrius I conquered parts of Punjab. King Menander (Milinda) was converted to Buddhism by monk Nagasena at Sagala (Sialkot). Menander was the first Indo-Greek king to issue gold coins in India. Around 160 BCE, Taxila was made the capital of Punjab, later shifted to Sagala.
  • Shakas (Scythians): Northern Punjab was one of the five seats of Shaka power, with Taxila as its capital. Prominent Shaka ruler: King Moga (Maues).
  • Parthians: Displaced the Shakas. Famous king: Gondophares. Excavations at Sunet (Ludhiana) and Rohira yielded coins/moulds belonging to the Yaudheya tribe.
  • Sungas: Pushyamitra Sunga captured parts of Punjab from the Mauryas.

Kushanas, Guptas, Huns & Harsha

  • Kushanas (Yuezhi): Founded by Heraios; expanded by Kujula Kadphises. Fourth Emperor Kanishka established winter capital at Purushapura (Peshawar) and summer capital at Kapisa (Bagram). Founded Saka Era (78 AD). Held 4th Buddhist Council in Taxila (Mahayana split). Chinapatti (Amritsar) was the winter residence of Kanishka's Chinese hostages (contained 10 monasteries). "Little Kushans" ruled Punjab 270–350 CE from Purushapura.
  • Guptas: Samudragupta annexed Punjab. Discovery of Red Polished Pottery at Sunet (Ludhiana) confirms Gupta dominion.
  • Hephthalites (White Huns): Took western Punjab and Gandhara (460–470 CE). Ruled aggressively under Toramana and his son Mihirakula (ruled 515–550 CE with capital at Sakala/Sialkot).
  • Vardhana Dynasty: Founded at Thaneswar (Kurukshetra) c. 500 CE by Pushyabhuti. Hiuen Tsang visited Jalandhar (Trigarta) in 635 AD under Buddhist King Wuddhi (Udito); recorded 50 viharas and 200 monks. Visited Nagardhana vihara where scholar Chandavarman lived. Identified Sirhind as capital of Shatadru.

6. Early Medieval Invasions & The Delhi Sultanate (8th – 15th Century) Pre-Mughal Eras

Early Muslim Invasions & Hindu Shahis

  • 712 AD: Muhammad Bin Qasim (Umayyad commander) defeated Raja Dahir of Sindh — 1st Muslim conquest.
  • Hindu Shahi Dynasty: Ruled Punjab and Kabul (964–1001 AD). King Jayapala had capital at Bathinda (Tabar-e-Hind / "Gateway to India"). Lost the 1st Battle of Waihind (1001 AD) to Mahmud Ghazni.
  • Mahmud Ghazni (1000–1027 AD): Invaded India 17 times (Somnath invaded 1025). Invaded Multan (1004–05), defeating Fateh Daud. Appointed converted Sukhpal (Nawasa Shah), who apostatized and was crushed in 1008. In 1008, Mahmud launched the 2nd Battle of Waihind near Peshawar, defeating Anandapala's united confederacy of Ujjain, Gwalior, Kalinjar, Kannauj, Delhi, and Ajmer. Contemporaries: Alberuni (Tarikh-i-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind) and Firdausi (Shah Nama).
  • Muhammad Ghori: Captured Multan (1175), Peshawar (1179), Sindh (1182), Sialkot (1185), Lahore (1186), Tabarhinda/Bathinda (1191). Defeated at 1st Battle of Tarain (1191); defeated Prithviraj Chauhan at 2nd Battle of Tarain (1192). Battle of Chandawar (1194) against Jayachandra. Slaves: Qutub-ud-din Aibak, Tajuddin Yalduz (Ghazni), Nasiruddin Qubacha (Multan/Uchch), Bakhtiyar Khilji (Bengal).

Delhi Sultanate Era

  • Slave / Mamluk Dynasty (1206–1290): Aibak crowned at Lahore (1206); died playing Chaugan at Lahore (Nov 1210) and buried there. Capital shifted to Delhi by Iltutmish. 1221: Genghis Khan reached the Indus. Razia Sultana married Malik Ikhtiar-ud-din Altunia (Governor of Bathinda); both defeated and killed near Kaithal (1240) by Bahram Shah. Balban poisoned Sirhind Governor Sher Khan, replacing him with Prince Sultan (martyred fighting Mongols, 1285).
  • Tughlaqs: Founder Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) was Governor of Punjab; administration dominated by Multanis. 1327: Mongol General Tarmashirin Khan invaded. Firoz Shah Tughlaq founded the Punjab Canal Irrigation System; built palace on Saraswati; founded Firozpur, Hisar, and Firozabad.
  • 1398 Invasion: Timur crossed the Indus, marched through Lahore, Ambala, Rupnagar, Hoshiarpur, and Kangra. Appointed Khizr Khan over Punjab.
  • Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451): Founded by Khizr Khan. Mubarak Shah (1421), Muhammad Shah, Ala-ud-din Alam Shah (deposed 1451 by Bahlol Lodhi).

7. Lodhi Administration & The Mughal Era in Punjab Mughal Sovereignty

The Lodhi Rule in Punjab (1451–1526)

  • Punjab divided into 2 Subas: Lahore and Multan; directly controlled Parganas: Sirhind, Sultanpur, and Dipalpur.
  • Bahlol Lodhi appointed Tatar Khan Lodhi as Nizam of Punjab, and his son Sikandar Lodhi (Nizam Khan) as Nizam of Sirhind.
  • Sheikh Yusuf appointed over Multan. When Shah Hussain attacked Multan, Bahlol dispatched Barbak Shah and Tatar Khan, but both were routed. Multan fell away, inciting Tatar Khan to rebel. Sikandar Lodhi defeated Tatar Khan and installed Tatar's son Daulat Khan Lodhi.
  • Sikandar Lodhi died 1517 AD; succeeded by Ibrahim Lodhi. In 1519, Babur launched raids on Bajaur, Bhera, and Sialkot.

Punjab Under the Mughal Empire

  • Babur: Captured Bhera (1519), Sialkot/Sayyidpur (1520), Lahore (1524), defeated Ibrahim Lodhi at 1st Panipat (1526). Until 1712, Punjab was treated as one of the 3 capital centers (with Agra and Delhi).
  • Humayun & Suri: Defeated by Sher Shah Suri (1540); fled to Amarkot (Rana Prasad Rao's refuge; Hamida Banu gave birth to Akbar on Oct 15, 1542). Regained empire by defeating Sikandar Suri at Battle of Sirhind (June 22, 1555) with Bairam Khan.
  • Akbar: Crowned at Kalanaur (Gurdaspur) on Feb 14, 1556 after Humayun fell down library stairs. Bairam Khan administered Sirhind/Rupnagar. Suba Lahore and Suba Multan established. Capital shifted to Lahore (1585–1598).
  • Jahangir: Shaikh Farid Bukhari (Murtaza Khan) was Governor. Built Shalimar Bagh, Begum Shahi Mosque, Anarkali Tomb, Khawabgah, and Moti Masjid at Lahore.
  • Shah Jahan: Ali Mardan Khan and later Prince Dara Shikoh (1639) were Governors. Built Sheesh Mahal in Lahore Fort, Wazir Khan Mosque, Aam Khas Bagh (Fatehgarh Sahib), and Hasli Canal (Upper Bari Doab Canal) from Madhopur to Lahore planned by Ali Mardan Khan and built by Mullah Ala'ul-Mul Tuni. Founded Phagwara. Portuguese missionary Sebastian Manrique visited Lahore in 1641.
  • Aurangzeb: Commissioned the Badshahi Mosque in Lahore. Facing Sikh resistance, Mughal hold weakened, leading to Sikh Misls and Maharaja Ranjit Singh's Sarkar-e-Khalsa (Provinces: Lahore, Multan, Peshawar, Kashmir 1799–1849).

8. Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469 – 1539 CE) 1st Sikh Guru

Chronological Lifeline & Family

  • Birth: April 15, 1469 (1526 Bikrami Samvat) at Village Rai Bhoe ki Talwandi (Nankana Sahib, Sheikhupura, Pakistan).
  • Guruship Span: 1469 – 1539 (70 years). Merged with eternity: September 22, 1539 at Kartarpur.
  • Parents: Father: Mehta Kalu (Patwari); Mother: Mata Tripta Ji. Elder sister: Bebe Nanaki (5 years older, married to Jai Ram of Sultanpur Lodhi; Daulat Khan Lodhi was Nizam).
  • Marriage: Married at age 15 to Bibi Sulakhani d/o Mul Raj of Batala in 1487 AD. Two sons: Baba Sri Chand (Chandar Dev) and Lakhmi Das Ji. "Babe Da Viah" festival began in 1917 after priest Kesra Singh organized an Akhand Path at Gurdwara Kandh Sahib.
  • Early Education: At age 7, learned Devnagri and Math from Pt. Gopal; Sanskrit from Pt. Brij Lal; Persian (Farsi) from Maulvi Qutab-ud-Din.
  • Enlightenment: In 1499, after 3 days of deep meditation in River Kali Bein, received enlightenment; first words: "Na koi Hindu, Na koi Musalman".

The Four Udasis (Extensive Preaching Tours)

  • First Udasi (1499–1510) (East): With Bhai Mardana. Saidpur/Eminabad (met Bhai Lalo; exposed Malik Bhago) → Tulumba (reformed Sajjan Thug) → Kurukshetra → Panipat → Haridwar → Delhi → Kedarnath → Badrinath → Joshi Math → Gorakhmata (renamed Nanakmata) → Banaras (met Chaturdas) → Gaya → Hajipur → Dhubri (met Shankaradeva) → Kamrup (counseled sorceress Nur Shah) → Guwahati → Shillong → Silhet (met Sheikh Jalaal) → Dhaka → Jagannath Puri (discourses with Chaitanya Mahaprabhu).
  • Second Udasi (1510–1515) (South): With Saido and Goho. Guntur → Kanchipuram → Trichinopoly → Nagapatnam → Rameshwaram → Trivandrum → Ceylon/Sri Lanka (met Jhanda Bedi) → Bidar → Ujjain → Mathura → Takhtupura (Hisar) → Pakpattan (met Sheikh Brahm) → Dipalpur → Sultanpur Lodhi.
  • Third Udasi (1515–1517) (North): With Hasu Lohar and Siha Shimba. Jalandhar → Hoshiarpur → Himachal (met Pir Budhan Shah) → Bilaspur → Mandi → Suket → Kangra → Kulu → Spiti → Kailash → Kashmir → Anant Nagar → Ladakh (Gurdwara Pathar Sahib in Leh; revered by Tibetan Buddhists as Guru Gompka Maharaj) → Kargil → Pehalgam → Mattan (met Brahmdas) → Sialkot → Kartarpur.
  • Fourth Udasi (1517–1521) (West): Dressed as a Muslim Haji with Bhai Mardana. Pakpattan (met Sheikh Brahm/Ibrahim) → Multan (met Sheikh Bahauddin) → Uch → Sukkur → Hinglaj → Mecca (met Rukunudin, assumed 1st martyr in Sikh history) → Medina (debated with Imam Azim Khan at tomb of Hazrat Muhammad) → Baghdad (met Sheikh Behlol) → Persia → Kandahar → Kabul → Peshawar → Hasan Abdal (Gurdwara Panja Sahib, boulder thrown by Shah Wali Qandhari; 1st gurdwara built by Hari Singh Nalwa) → Gujrat → Saidpur (arrested in Babur's jail, mentioned in Baburbani).

Global Titles, Kartarpur & Literary Banis

  • Different Names: Nanak Acharya (Sri Lanka), Nanak Lama (Tibet), Guru Rimpochiya (Sikkim), Nanak Rishi (Nepal), Nanak Peer (Baghdad), Vali Hind (Mecca), Nanak Kadamdar (Russia), Baba Nanak (Iraq), Baba Foosa (China).
  • City Founded: Kartarpur on the right bank of River Ravi (Narowal district, Pakistan). Remained there for 18 years until 22 Sept 1539. Kartarpur corridor connects Darbar Sahib Narowal with Dera Baba Nanak in Gurdaspur, inaugurated Nov 12, 2019 for the 550th birth anniversary.
  • Literary Works: Compiled 976 Shabads in 19 Ragas. Banis: Japji Sahib, Sidh Gosht, Sohilaa, Dakhni Onkar, Asa di Var, Var Majha (invented Majh Raag), Thit, Patti, Bara Mah Tukhari, and Var Malar. Included in Rehras Sahib and Kirtan Sohila.
  • Associated Gurdwaras: Gurdwara Janam Asthan (Nankana Sahib, rebuilt by Ranjit Singh in 19th c., earlier by grandson Baba Dharam Chand); Sultanpur Lodhi (commemorates Modi Khana grain dispensation); Gurdwara Rori Sahib (Eminabad; Bhai Lalo vs Malik Bhago); Gurdwara Sidh Math (Kurukshetra); Gurdwara Nanak Wara (Haridwar; exposed watering sun fallacy); Gurdwara Majnu ka Tilla (Delhi; Sikandar Lodhi freed prisoners); Gurdwara Nanakmata (Uttarakhand; Siddh discourses).

9. Sri Guru Angad Dev Ji (1504 – 1552 CE) 2nd Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Family & Accession

  • Birth: March 31, 1504 at Harike or Matte-di Sarai (Sarai Naga). Born as Bhai Lehna Ji.
  • Guruship: 1539 – 1552 (13 years). Merged with eternity: March 29, 1552.
  • Parents: Father: Bhai Pheru Mall; Mother: Mata Ramo Ji (also named Sabhrai, Mansa Devi, Daya Kaur).
  • Family: Wife: Bibi Khivi Ji (the only woman whose name is explicitly mentioned in Guru Granth Sahib). Children: Dassu Ji, Dattu Ji, Bibi Amro, and Bibi Alakhni.
  • Meeting Guru Nanak: Heard about Guru Nanak from follower Bhai Jodha. Bhai Lehna was previously a priest at a temple dedicated to Goddess Durga at Khadur Sahib. Selected for Gurgaddi on Sept 7, 1539; named "Angad" (part of body).

Works, Innovations & Associated Gurdwaras

  • Script & Texts: Standardized and improved the old Punjabi script into Gurmukhi; authored the Balbodh script primer. Introduced regular recitation of "Nam".
  • Janam Sakhi: Compiled the Janam Sakhi of Guru Nanak Dev Ji with the help of Bhai Bala Ji, scribed by Bhai Paira Mokha at Gurdwara Sri Tapiana Sahib. Composed 62 Shabads/Shaloks.
  • Langar & Mal Akhara: Systematized Langar, with Mata Khivi acting as manager serving nutritious meals (kheer). Introduced Mal Akhara for physical fitness, wrestling, and health. Explicitly opposed Udasi asceticism.
  • Towns: Founder of Khadur Sahib (Tarn Taran). Laid the foundation of Goindwal Sahib in 1546, charging Guru Amar Das to complete it.
  • Associated Gurdwaras: Gurdwara Mai Bharai Sahib (paternal aunt's home where he worshipped in solitude); Gurdwara Sri Tapiana Sahib; Gurdwara Tap Asthan; Gurdwara Mall Akhara Sahib; Bhai Bala Ji Memorial (in parikrama of Tapiana Sahib where Guru Angad performed his last rites).

10. Sri Guru Amar Das Ji (1479 – 1574 CE) 3rd Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Family & Accession

  • Birth: May 5, 1479 at Village Basarke. Merged with eternity: September 1, 1574.
  • Guruship: 1552 – 1574 (22 years). Acceded to Guruship at the age of 73.
  • Parents: Father: Tej Bhan; Mother: Bakht Kaur Ji (also called Lakhmi Devi or Sulakhani).
  • Family: Wife: Bibi Mansa Devi Ji. Sons: Mohan & Mohri; Daughters: Bibi Dani & Bibi Bhani Ji. Contacted Guru Angad via Bibi Amro (Guru Angad's daughter).

Reforms, Akbar's Visit & Literary Contributions

  • Baoli at Goindwal: Completed the sacred Baoli at Goindwal Sahib in 1559 with 84 steps.
  • Manji System: Established 22 Manjis (preaching districts) across the Sikh community. Two Manjis were operated by women: Bibi Matho and Bibi Sachan Sach (Sakhi of Matho Murari).
  • Social Reforms: Strictly condemned and abolished Caste discrimination, Untouchability, Sati Pratha, and Parda/Burqa. Separated Sikhs permanently from the Udasi sect. Strictly enforced "Pehlan Pangat, Phir Sangat".
  • Emperor Akbar's Visit: Akbar visited Goindwal in 1567 after the Battle of Chittorgarh, ate common Langar in the Pangat, and granted land to Bibi Bhani. Guru Amar Das persuaded Akbar to waive the pilgrim toll tax for non-Muslims crossing Ganga and Yamuna, and to remit land revenues for peasants facing hardship.
  • Compositions: Composed 907 Shabads in 17 Ragas, including Anand Sahib. Part of Rehras Sahib. Prophesied about grandson Arjan: "Dohita Bani Ka Bohita" (My grandson shall be a ship carrying mankind across the ocean of life).

11. Sri Guru Ram Das Ji (1534 – 1581 CE) 4th Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Family & Accession

  • Birth: September 24, 1534 at Chuna Mandi (Lahore, Pakistan). Born as Bhai Jetha.
  • Guruship: 1574 – 1581 (7 years). Merged with eternity: September 1, 1581.
  • Parents: Father: Hari Das; Mother: Daya Kaur (Anup Devi Ji).
  • Family: Wife: Bibi Bhani Ji. Children: Prithi Chand, Baba Mahadev, and Arjan Dev Ji.

Establishment of Amritsar & Works

  • Founding Amritsar: Laid the foundation in 1574 CE (originally called Chak Ramdas, Guru Ka Chak, or Ramdaspur). Obtained grant of site along with 500 bighas of land from Emperor Akbar, paying Rs 700 to the Zamindars of Tung village who owned the land. Invited 52 traders belonging to nearby areas like Patti and Kasur to establish commerce.
  • Five Sarovars: Out of the 5 sacred Sarovars of Amritsar (Amritsar, Santokhsar, Ramsar, Kaulsar, Bibeksar), Guru Ram Das initiated the excavation of Amritsar Sarovar and Santokhsar Sarovar (completed later by Guru Arjan Dev).
  • Masand System & Udasi Truce: Introduced the Masand System to collect community resources and tithes for the construction of Harmandir Sahib. Historic reconciliation between Sikhs and Udasis took place during his reign.
  • Compositions: Composed 679 Shabads in 30 Ragas (out of 31 Ragas in Adi Granth). Composed the Lavan hymns in Suhi Raga (pages 773–774 of Guru Granth Sahib) for solemnizing Anand Karaj. Bani is included in Rehras Sahib and Kirtan Sohila.

12. Sri Guru Arjan Dev Ji (1563 – 1606 CE) 5th Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Family & Martyrdom

  • Birth: April 15, 1563 at Village Goindwal Sahib. Parents: Guru Ram Das & Bibi Bhani. Wife: Bibi Ganga Ji. Son: Guru Hargobind.
  • Guruship: 1581 – 1606 (25 years).
  • Martyrdom: Merged with eternity on May 30, 1606 on the banks of River Ravi (Lahore) under orders of Mughal Emperor Jahangir. First martyr in Sikh history (Shaheedan de Sartaj). Gurdwara Dera Sahib marks the site.

Harmandir Sahib & Towns Founded

  • Sri Harmandir Sahib: Land purchased by Guru Ram Das in 1564 AD from Tung zamindars. Foundation stone laid in 1645 Bikrami Samvat (1588 AD) by Sufi Saint Hazrat Mian Mir of Lahore (direct descendant of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab, Qadiri order). Construction supervised by Guru Arjan Dev assisted by Baba Budha Ji, Bhai Gurdas, and Bhai Sahlo. Completed in 1601 CE. Baba Budha Ji appointed 1st Granthi.
  • Tarn Taran Sahib (1590): Built in village Khara in honour of Guru Ram Das; possesses the largest Sarovar among all Gurdwaras. Established a dedicated colony/home for leprosy patients.
  • Other Towns: Founded Kartarpur (Jalandhar) in 1593, digging the sacred Gangasagar Sarovar. Founded Sri Hargobindpur (1595) on the banks of Beas River on the ruins of Ruhela. Renamed village Bhaini Sandhua Di to Chohla Sahib after residing there. Dug a Baoli at Lahore as a pilgrimage center.

Adi Granth, Musical Inventions & Economics

  • Compilation of Adi Granth: Procured Bani of previous Gurus from Baba Mohan (son of 3rd Guru). Dictated the scripture to Bhai Gurdas Ji on the banks of Ramsar Sarovar. Completed on August 30, 1604; installed in Harmandir Sahib on September 1, 1604.
  • Hymns: Composed 2216 Shabads in 30 Ragas (highest in SGGS). Works: Sukhmani Sahib, Bara Mah Majh, Bavan Akhri, and 6 Vaars.
  • Innovations: Formalized mandatory Daswandh (1/10th income for Langar). Encouraged trans-Indus horse trading. Invented the string musical instrument Saranda in 1576–77 (aged 13 at Goindwal) and played it for Kirtan. Official Darbar Ragis: Bhai Satta and Bhai Balwand.

13. Sri Guru Hargobind Ji (1595 – 1645 CE) 6th Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Family & Concepts

  • Birth: June 19, 1595 at Guru ki Wadali (Amritsar). Parents: Guru Arjan Dev & Bibi Ganga Ji. Merged with eternity: 1645 at Kiratpur. Guruship: 1606–1645 (39 years).
  • Family: Wives: Bibi Damodari Ji (mother of Gurditta Ji, Bibi Veero, Ani Rai); Mahadevi Ji (mother of Suraj Mal, Atal Rai); Bibi Nanaki Ji (mother of Tyag Mal / Guru Tegh Bahadur).
  • Miri and Piri: Introduced Miri (physical/temporal power) and Piri (spiritual power) wearing two swords. First Saint-Soldier in Sikh history; designated by Sikhs as Sache Patshah. Adopted royal regalia: Kalgi and 52 bodyguards. Encouraged offerings of horses and arms.

Forts, Akal Takht, Army & Battles

  • Akal Takht & Forts: Built the 12-foot high Sri Akal Takht in 1609 facing Harmandir Sahib. Constructed Lohgarh Fort at Amritsar and established Bibeksar Sarovar.
  • Army Organization: Divided an army of 500 soldiers into 5 Jathas commanded by Baba Bidhi Chand, Bhai Pairan, Bhai Jetha, Bhai Paira, and Bhai Langha. A separate Pathan contingent was led by Painde Khan.
  • Martial Music: Invented the musical instrument Taus (peacock-shaped). Initiated Dhadi Jathas to recite heroic ballads (Vaars) in Vir-Ras; appointed famous court Dhadhis Bhai Abdullah and Bhai Natha at Akal Takht parikrama. Introduced Dholak beat around Harmandir Sahib.
  • Town & Preaching: Founded Kiratpur Sahib (1635) on land gifted by Raja Kalyan Chand of Kahlur; lived there for his last 10 years. Entrusted preaching to Baba Gurditta who appointed 4 Dhunas: Almast (Gorakhmata & Dhaka), Phul (Malwa), Gonda (Doaba), and Balu (Kashmir, Hazra, Pathankot). Dispatched Bhai Bidhi Chand to Bengal and Bhai Gurdas to Kabul and Banaras. Contemporary Baba Phul established the Phulkian Misl.
  • Mughal Relations & 7 Battles: Imprisoned in Gwalior Jail, later released by Jahangir along with 52 Rajas (Bandi Chhor). Strained ties with Shah Jahan. Battles fought: Battle of Sangrana (1628), Battle of Rohilla (1630), Battle of Amritsar (1634), Battle of Lehra (1634), Battle of Kiratpur (1634), Battle of Kartarpur (1635), and Battle of Phagwara (1635). Established Gurdwara Dera Sahib at Lahore; toured Nankana Sahib, Nanakmata, Takhtupura, and Daroli Bhai (Moga).

14. Sri Guru Har Rai Ji (1630 – 1661 CE) 7th Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Herbal Medicine & Excommunication

  • Birth & Eternity: Born January 31, 1630 at Kiratpur Sahib; merged with eternity October 6, 1661 at Kiratpur Sahib. The only Sikh Guru with identical birth and death places.
  • Guruship: 1645 – 1661 (17 years). Grandson of Guru Hargobind; son of Baba Gurditta & Mata Nihal Kaur (Ananti Ji).
  • Family: Wife: Bibi Krishan Kaur (Sulakhani Ji). Children: Baba Ram Rai and Har Krishan.
  • The Herbal Guru: Operated an Ayurvedic Herbal Medicine Hospital and Research Centre at Kiratpur. Provided rare medicines to cure prince Dara Shikoh (eldest son of Shah Jahan) and provided him shelter in 1658 after Aurangzeb defeated him.
  • Excommunication of Ram Rai: When summoned to Delhi, Guru Ji sent Ram Rai as emissary. To please Aurangzeb, Ram Rai distorted Guru Nanak's hymn from "Mitti Musalman ki Pede Pai Kumhar" to "Mitti Beiman Ki Pede Pai Kumhar". Enraged, Guru Har Rai excommunicated him. Ram Rai was exiled, received a Garhwal jagir from Aurangzeb, and settled in Doon Valley (giving rise to Dehradun around Guru Ram Rai Darbar Sahib).
  • Disciples: Bhagat Gir, Bhai Sangatiya, and Bhai Gonda. Blessed Bhai Phul with a luxurious life.

15. Sri Guru Har Krishan Ji (1656 – 1664 CE) 8th Sikh Guru

The Youngest Guru (Bal Peer) & Delhi Mission

  • Birth: July 7, 1656 at Kiratpur Sahib. Parents: Guru Har Rai & Krishan Kaur (Sulakhani Ji). Elder brother: Ram Rai.
  • Guruship: 1661 – 1664 (3 years). Youngest Guru, acceding at the age of 5. Revered as Bal Peer.
  • Aurangzeb's Summons: Summoned to Delhi regarding Ram Rai's dispute. Stayed at Amber King Raja Jai Singh's Palace (now Gurdwara Bangla Sahib). Raja Jai Singh tested Guruji by asking him to identify the chief Rani sitting among disguised maidens; Guruji recognized her immediately and sat in her lap.
  • Demise: Served smallpox patients in Delhi selflessly. Afflicted by smallpox, uttered "Baba Bakala" three times before passing away at age 8 on March 30, 1664. Associated with the Sakhi of Pandit Lal Chand & the mute water-bearer Chajju Ram who expounded the Bhagavad Gita.

16. Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji (1621 – 1675 CE) 9th Sikh Guru

Lifeline, Education & Finding at Bakala

  • Birth: April 1, 1621 at Amritsar. Parents: Guru Hargobind & Mata Nanaki Ji. Guruship: 1664 – 1675 (11 years). Merged with eternity: December 11, 1675 at Chandni Chowk, Delhi.
  • Family: Married Bibi Gujri Ji in 1632 at Kartarpur. Son: Gobind Rai (born Dec 22, 1666 at Patna).
  • Education: Gurmukhi, Hindi, Sanskrit, and philosophy from Bhai Gurdas; Archery/Horsemanship from Baba Budha Ji; Swordsmanship from Guru Hargobind.
  • "Guru Ladho Re": Trader Makhan Shah Lubana found him at Baba Bakala after offering 2 dinars, publicly shouting "Guru Ladho Re". Scribe Dargah Mall and prominent Sikhs presented the 5-paisa coin and coconut. Gates of Harmandir were closed by Harji Meena (s/o Meharban); Guruji stayed at Tham Sahib / Thara Sahib. Met Lal Chand s/o Bhai Ghuka at Guru Ka Bagh. In Majha, received a mare (Ghodi) from Chaudhary Raghpat Rai at Khemkaran.

Founding Anandpur Sahib, Travels & Supreme Martyrdom

  • Founding Anandpur Sahib: Purchased land of Makhowal village from Queen Jalala Devi (w/o King Deep Chand of Bilaspur); founded Chak Nanki on June 19, 1665 (named after his mother; later Anandpur Sahib).
  • Travels: Visited Nawab Saif-ud-Din at Saifabad (Bahadurgarh Patiala) via Ropar, Banur, Rajpura; traveled to Pehowa, Kaithal, Kurukshetra, Delhi (met Rajput Raja Ram Singh), and through Mathura, Agra, Kanpur, Banaras, Sasaram, Gaya, Patna, Dhaka, Sylhet, and Chittagong. In Dhaka, met Sangats under Mahant Bhai Nathe and Bhai Balki Das (Chief Masand of Bengal). Aided Raja Ram Singh in reconciling with Ahom King Chakradhwaj Singh of Assam. In 1672–73 toured Malwa and Bangar (Mullowal, Sekhon, Bare, Dhilwan, Bhikhi, Maur, Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda, Dhamtan).
  • Martyrdom: Kashmiri Pandits approached him at Anandpur regarding the atrocities and forced conversions of Kashmir Governor Iftikhar Khan. Guruji marched to Delhi. Accompanied by Bhai Dayala Ji, Bhai Mati Das Ji (Dewan), and Bhai Sati Das Ji (Scribe).
  • Executions & Shrines: Martyred at Chandni Chowk, Delhi (Gurdwara Sis Ganj). Bhai Mati Das sawn alive; Bhai Sati Das burned in cotton; Bhai Dayala boiled alive. Guruji's body was cremated by Lakhi Shah Vanjara by setting fire to his own house (Gurdwara Rakab Ganj). The severed sacred head was brought to Anandpur by Bhai Jaita (Guru Gobind Singh embraced him: "Rangreta Guru ka Beta"). Both Sis Ganj and Rakab Ganj were constructed by Baba Baghel Singh. Composed 116 Shabads/Shaloks in 15 Ragas; sole contributor to Jaijawanti Raag (4 Shabads).

17. Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji (1666 – 1708 CE) 10th Sikh Guru

Birth, Family & Upbringing

  • Birth: December 22, 1666 at Patna. Parents: Guru Tegh Bahadur & Mata Gujri. Merged with eternity: October 11, 1708 at Nanded. Guruship: 1675–1708 (33 years). Blessed in infancy by Sayyid Bhikham Shah of Ghuram.
  • Dastarbandhi: Held at Lakhnaur (Ambala) in 1671 AD (ancestral village of Mata Gujri); maternal uncle Mama Mehar Chand tied a green turban. Learned Sanskrit from Pt. Harjas; Persian from Qazi Pir Mohammad; weapons/horsemanship from Rajput Banjar Singh; Gurmukhi from Bhai Sahib Chand and Bhai Sati Das.
  • Marriages & Sons: 1. Mata Jito Ji: Married 1677 at Basantgarh (Anandpur) at age 10. Sons: Jujhar Singh, Zorawar Singh, Fateh Singh.
    2. Mata Sundri Ji: Married 1684 at age 17. Son: Ajit Singh.
    3. Mata Sahib Kaur Ji: Married at age 33 (no biological children); revered as the "Mother of the Khalsa".

Creation of Khalsa Panth & Martial Innovations

  • Khalsa Panth (March 30, 1699): Established on Baisakhi day at Anandpur Sahib. Panj Pyare: 1. Bhai Daya Singh (Daya Ram - Khatri from Lahore)
    2. Bhai Dharam Singh (Dhai Dharam Das - Jat farmer from Delhi)
    3. Bhai Himmat Singh (Himmat Rai - Jhir water-carrier from Jagannath Puri)
    4. Bhai Mokham Singh (Mokham Chand - Dhobi washerman from Dwarka)
    5. Bhai Sahib Singh (Sahib Chand - Nai barber from Bidar).
  • Abolitions & Symbols: Completely abolished the corrupt Masand system. Prepared the victory drum Ranjit Nagara in 1684 (crafted by Diwan Nand Chand at Anandpur). Invented the bowed instrument Dilruba from the Taus to enable cavalry transport.
  • Nirmala Sect: Founded in late 17th century by sending 5 Sikhs (Bir Singh, Ganda Singh, Karam Singh, Ram Singh, and Saina Singh) to Varanasi to learn Sanskrit and classical literature; later initiated with Amrit into the Khalsa.
  • Literary Works: Composed Chandi di Var (first composition, 1684; only one in Punjabi), Jaap Sahib, Akal Ustat, Bachitra Natak, Chaupai Sahib (composed while residing at Gurdwara Bibhour Sahib on Sutlej bank in Nangal), Charitropakhyan, Chobis Avtar, and Zafarnama. Court poet Sainapati wrote Sri Gur Sobha (1701). Five mandatory banis: Japji Sahib, Jaap Sahib, Anand Sahib, Benti Chaupai, and Tav Parsad Savaiye.
  • Damdama Sahib (Guru Ki Kashi): In 1705, stayed at Talwandi Sabo. Recomposed Guru Granth Sahib (1706) adding Guru Tegh Bahadur's Bani with scribe Bhai Mani Singh. Baba Deep Singh wrote additional copies. Mani Singh appointed Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib, and Baba Deep Singh at Damdama Sahib. Dasam Granth contains 1428 pages. Conferred perpetual Guruship on Guru Granth Sahib on October 20, 1708 at Nanded (Guru Gaddi Divas).

19. Adi Granth Sahib & Historical Birs Scriptural Authority

Compilation History & Metrics

  • Compiled by Guru Arjan Dev assisted by Bhai Gurdas and Baba Budha Ji. Collected Pothis in 1603 from Bhai Mohan Ji. Total 36 Hindu-Muslim saints/bards included.
  • Completed August 16, 1604; final compilation August 30, 1604; installed in Harmandir Sahib on September 1, 1604 (Parkash Utsav Day). First Head Granthi: Baba Budha Ji.
  • Total Pages: 1430 pages (landscape orientation). Total lines: 26,852 lines (standard 19 lines per page).
  • Words & Punctuation: 29,445 repeated words. Most repeated word: "Har" (ਹਰਿ) used 9,288 times. No punctuation marks used (no comma, bindi, semicolon, adhak, pair-bindi). Total Shabads: 5894.
  • Ragas: Written in 31 single Ragas (60 total including 29 mixed Ragas). Begins with non-raga section: Japji Sahib (begins with Ik Onkar), followed by Rehras Sahib, ends with Kirtan Sohila. First Raga: Sri Raga; last Raga: Jaijawanti Raga. Raag Gauree contains the largest number of compositions.

Three Historical Recensions (Birs)

  • Kartarpur Wali Bir (Dhirmaliya Di Bir): Original copy. When Guru Hargobind left Kartarpur, Dhir Mall took possession and installed it at Gurdwara Thara Sahib, Kartarpur. Ranjit Singh procured it; later subject to litigation, returned to Dhir Mall's successors.
  • Bhai Banno Wali Bir (Khare Vali Bir): Sent to Lahore with Bhai Banno for binding; Banno prepared another copy inserting a few unauthenticated Shabads of his own choice. Installed at Khara Mangat; currently with descendants at Kanpur City.
  • Damdama Sahib Bir (Guru Granth Sahib): Guru Gobind Singh added the Bani of Guru Tegh Bahadur at Talwandi Sabo with Bhai Mani Singh. Conferred Guruship on October 20, 1708 at Nanded (Guru Gaddi Divas). Did not add his own hymns (compiled separately in Dasam Granth).

21. Important Sikh Historical Texts & Historiography Literary Corpus

Historical Granths & Chronicles

  • Suraj Prakash (Gur Partap Suraj Granth): Written in Brij Bhasha by Kavi Santokh Singh; published in 1843 CE. Comprehensive biographical narrative of all ten Gurus.
  • Sarabloh Granth: Known as 'Scripture of Pure Iron' or Mangla Charan Puran; traditionally attributed to Guru Gobind Singh Ji (though some scholars place its compilation after his demise).
  • Sri Gur Sobha: Written in 1701 by court poet Sainapati; one of the earliest primary poetic accounts of Guru Gobind Singh's life.
  • Gurbilas Patshahi Dasvi: Completed in 1751 by Kuir Singh, who entered the Khalsa fold under the influence of Bhai Mani Singh.

Modern Scholarship & Reference Works

  • Max Arthur Macauliffe: Arrived in Punjab as an ICS officer in Feb 1864; converted to Sikhism in the 1860s. Wrote an English translation of Guru Granth Sahib and authored the monumental six-volume work The Sikh Religion: its Gurus, Sacred Writings and Authors (Oxford University Press, 1909), assisted by scholar Pratap Singh Giani.
  • Giani Gian Singh: Sikh historian who authored Naveen Panth Parkash and Twarikh Guru Khalsa (simplified version of Suraj Parkash; contains detailed account of dispute between Banda Singh Bahadur and Mata Sundari).
  • Guru Shabad Ratnakar Mahan Kosh: First Punjabi language encyclopedia compiled by Bhai Kahn Singh Nabha over 14 years; contains over 70,000 words from Guru Granth Sahib, Dasam Granth, Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, and other texts.

22. Biographical Dossier of Important Guru Period Personalities Personalities

Bhai Gurdas (1551 – 1636 CE)

  • Born at Basarke Gillan (Amritsar); nephew of Guru Amar Das.
  • Scribe of original Adi Granth Sahib at Ramsar (1604).
  • Appointed 1st Jathedar of Sri Akal Takht Sahib (1609). Supervised 4 copyists: Bhai Haria, Bhai Sant Das, Bhai Sukha, Bhai Mansa Ram.
  • Died on August 25, 1636 at Goindwal Sahib.

Baba Budha Ji (1506 – 1631 CE)

  • Born into Randhawa Jat clan at Kathu Nangal (Amritsar).
  • Had the singular distinction of applying Gurgaddi Tilak to 5 Gurus (Guru Angad Dev to Guru Hargobind).
  • Appointed 1st Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib (1604).

Bhai Mani Singh Ji (1644 – 1738 CE)

  • Born at Multan; served Guru Har Rai, Guru Har Krishan, Guru Tegh Bahadur, and Guru Gobind Singh.
  • Scribe of Damdami Bir of SGGS; appointed Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib.
  • Authored famous Vaars: Gyan Ratnavali (Life of Guru Nanak) and Bhagat Ratnavali / Sikhan di Bhagat Mala.
  • Brokered the truce between Tat Khalsa and Bandai Khalsa (1721). Executed joint by joint at Nakhaas Chowk, Lahore in Dec 1738 by Zakariya Khan.

Bhai Jaita Ji (Bhai Jiwan Singh) (1661 – 1704)

  • Born to Sada Nand and Mata Premo at Patna. Severed his father's head to substitute it and carried Guru Tegh Bahadur’s sacred head from Chandni Chowk to Anandpur.
  • Hailed as "Rangreta Guru Ka Beta" by Guru Gobind Singh. Authored the epic Sri Gur Katha. Attained martyrdom at Chamkaur/Sarsa.

Mai Bhago (Mata Bhag Kaur)

  • Born at Jhabal Kalan (Amritsar); married to Nidhan Singh Warraich of Patti.
  • Rallied the 40 deserters (Chali Mukte) back to Guru Gobind Singh; sole surviving warrior of the Battle of Khidrana (Dec 29, 1705).

Bhai Kanhaiya Ji

  • Disciple instructed by Guru Tegh Bahadur; famous for serving water to wounded soldiers without discriminating between Sikhs and Mughals during 2nd siege of Anandpur.
  • Founded the Sewapanthi / Addan Shahi sect. Celebrated in Sindh as Khat Waro Bao / Khaatwala Baba (Khat Wari Darbar at Shikarpur).
  • Punjab Govt declared his birthday (Sept 20) as Manav Sewa Diwas.

Bhai Mardana Ji & Bhai Bala Ji

  • Bhai Mardana: Muslim Mirasi born to Badra & Lakkho at Talwandi. Guru Nanak's lifelong companion on all 4 Udasis; played the Rabab. 3 shlokas dedicated to him in SGGS. Swami Haridas (Tansen’s master) learned classical music from him.
  • Bhai Bala: Sandhu Jat born 1466 at Talwandi; assisted Guru Angad Dev in composing the Bhai Bala Janamsakhi; passed away at Khadur Sahib in 1544.

Bhai Bidhi Chand Chhina

  • Jat Sikh born at Sarhali (Amritsar). Disciple of Guru Arjan Dev & Guru Hargobind. Fought in battles of Amritsar, Lahira, and Kartarpur.
  • Famed for daring rescue of horses Dilbagh and Gulbagh from Lahore Mughal stables. Preached in eastern India; passed away at Deonagar (Ayodhya) in 1638.

Martyrs of Chandni Chowk (1675)

  • Bhai Mati Das: Chhibber Saraswat Mohyal Brahmin from Karyala (Jhelum). Sawn alive vertically from head down.
  • Bhai Sati Das: Younger brother of Mati Das; court scribe; wrapped in cotton wool and burned alive.
  • Bhai Dayala Ji: Boiled alive in a cauldron of water.

Scholarly Texts & Modern Historiography

  • Suraj Prakash (Gur Partap Suraj Granth): Written in Brij Bhasha by Kavi Santokh Singh (published 1843 CE).
  • Sarabloh Granth: Traditionally attributed to Guru Gobind Singh (Manglacharan Puran).
  • Max Arthur Macauliffe: Arrived 1864 as ICS; translated Sikh scriptures; wrote monumental 6-volume The Sikh Religion (1909, assisted by Bhai Pratap Singh Giani).
  • Giani Gian Singh: Wrote Naveen Panth Parkash and Twarikh Guru Khalsa (details Mata Sundri - Banda Singh dispute).
  • Mahan Kosh (Guru Shabad Ratnakar Mahan Kosh): 1st Punjabi encyclopedia compiled by Bhai Kahn Singh Nabha over 14 years (contains 70,000+ words).
  • Gurbilas Patshahi Dasvi (1751): Poetic chronicle of Guru Gobind Singh by Kuir Singh.

23. Sufism: Etymology, Organization & Sub-Continental Foundations Sufi Fundamentals

Core Definitions & Early History

  • Etymology: The word 'Sufi' is derived from 'suf', meaning wool in Arabic. It symbolizes purity, understood as "the one who wears wool on top of purity".
  • Origin & Center: Sufism emerged in the 8th century. Basra in Iraq became the primary center of Sufi activities. Early known Sufis: Rabia al-Adawiya, Al-Junaid, and Bayazid Bastami.
  • Liberal Framework: Sufi saints did not establish a new religion; they prepared a liberal spiritual movement within the framework of Islam.
  • Oldest Sufi in India: Al Hujwiri, who established himself in North India and was buried in Lahore, is regarded as the oldest Sufi in the Indian sub-continent.
  • Arrival in Punjab: Introduced primarily through the migration of Sufi saints from Central Asia, Persia, and the Arab world in the 11th century. Globally, around fourteen Sufi Silsilas exist.

Organizational Structure & Classifications

  • Silsila: Sufis were organized into orders or lineages (Silsilas), each headed by a spiritual guide (pir) called a Khwaja or Sheikh.
  • Khanqah: The hospice or monastic community center where the pir and his disciples lived together.
  • Wali: A nominated successor chosen from among the disciples to carry on the spiritual lineage.
  • Ba-shara: Sufi orders that follow Islamic law (Sharia) strictly.
  • Be-shara: Non-conformist Sufis who do not believe in Sharia; also popularly known as "Mast Kalandars", "Malangs", or "Haidaris".

24. The Chishti Silsila: Pioneers & Legacy in Punjab & Delhi Chishti Order

Origins & Imperial Centers

  • Founders: Global founder: Abu Ishaq Shami. Founded in India by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti.
  • Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti (Khwaja Gharib Nawaz): Born in Sistan (modern Iran) in 1142. Disciple of Khwaja Usman Harooni. Settled in Ajmer (Rajasthan) around 1192, which became the prime mission center.
  • Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki: Born in 1173 in modern Afghanistan. Follower of Moinuddin Chishti; received khilafat and moved to Delhi during the Delhi Sultanate. Passed away in 1235; tomb in Mehrauli, Delhi. Qutub Minar was built in his memory by Qutb-ud-din Aibak.

Baba Fariduddin Ganjshakar (Baba Farid)

  • Birth & Roots: Born in 1173 CE near Shakarganj in Multan (Pakistan); family migrated from Kabul.
  • Spiritual Journey: Educated at Multan, where he met his mentor Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki. Moved to Delhi, then Hansi (Haryana). On Kaki's death in 1235, Farid became his spiritual successor and settled at Ajodhan (present-day Pakpattan, Pakistan).
  • Father of Punjabi Poetry: Among the first Sufi saints to compose verses in Punjabi.
  • Zawiya Al-Hindiya (Jerusalem): Prayed at Al-Aqsa Mosque and stayed at a lodge in Jerusalem. Became known as Zawiya Al-Hindiya ("Lodge of Hind"). Described by medieval traveler Evliya Chelebi in 1671 as one of the city's largest Zawiyas. Currently owned by the Waqf Board of India (accessible only to Indian citizens/heritage).

Nizamuddin Auliya, Chirag-e-Delhi & Salim Chishti

  • Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya: Disciple of Baba Farid; came to Delhi in 1259. Titles: Mehboob-i-Ilahi and Sultan-ul-Auliya. Kept aloof from rulers; defined renunciation as distributing food and clothing to the poor. Interacted with Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq.
  • Nasiruddin Mahmud Chirag-Dehlavi: Born Syed Nasiruddin Mahmud Al-Hassani (~1274, Ayodhya). Disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya at age 40. Title: "Roshan Chirag-e-Delhi" ("Illuminated Lamp of Delhi"). Tomb built by Firuz Shah Tughluq in 1358.
  • Sheikh Salim Chishti: Descendant of Baba Farid. Deeply influenced Emperor Akbar. Lived in Sikri (Fatehpur Sikri, temporary Mughal capital). Tomb is in the courtyard of Jama Masjid at Fatehpur Sikri.

Amir Khusrau (1253 – 1325 CE)

  • Court & Discipleship: Foremost disciple of Sheikh Nizamuddin Auliya. Witnessed the reign of seven Sultans (Balban, Muhammad, Kaiqubad, Jalaluddin Khilji, Alauddin Khilji, Mubarak Shah Khalji, Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq).
  • Titles: "Voice of India", "Parrot of India" (Tuti-e-Hind), "Father of Qawwali", and "Father of Urdu Literature".
  • Linguistic Firsts: First Muslim poet to freely use Hindi words; first to write jointly in Hindi, Hindavi, and Persian. Famous Kashmir quote: "Gar Firdaus, Bar Ruhe Zamin Ast, Hamin Asto, Hamin Asto, Hamin Ast".
  • Inventions & Works: Inventor of the Sitar in India. Author of Khaliq-e-bari (oldest printed dictionary in the world, dealing with Hindi/Persian words), Tuhfat us-Sighr (Gift of Childhood), Tughlaq Nama, and Afzal ul-Fawaid (teachings of Nizamuddin Auliya).

25. The Rishi Order of Kashmir Indigenous Order

Core Philosophy & Indigenous Roots

  • Origin: Originated in Kashmir in the early 15th century. While orders like Suhrawardi, Kubravi, Naqshbandi, and Qadiri arrived from abroad, the Rishi order evolved locally.
  • Etymology & Culture: Derived from Sanskrit and Indian traditions. Preached universal love regardless of caste, creed, or color; foundational to Kashmiriyat (social and cultural consciousness of Kashmir).

Nund Rishi (Sheikh Noor-ud-din Wali)

  • Founder: Founded the Rishi Silsila; died in 1438 and buried at Charar-e-Sharief.
  • Titles: Addressed by Muslims as Sheikh Noor-ud-Din Noorani, Sheikh-Ul-Alam ("spiritual guide of the world"), and Alamdar-e-Kashmir ("flag bearer of Kashmir").
  • Hindu Names: Revered by Hindus as Nund Lal and Sahajananda.

Baba Hyder Reshi & Hamza Makhdoom

  • Baba Hyder Reshi: Known as Hardi Rishi / Reshi Mir Saeb; born Jan 17, 1504 at Dantar (Anantnag) into a blacksmith family (Reshi Maloo). Shrine visited by Hindus and Muslims. Influenced Hamza Makhdoom, Resh Mir Saeb, and Shamas Faqir.
  • Hamza Makhdoom (Makhdoom Sahib): Born Muhammad Hamza Raina in Baramulla. Titles: Mehboob-ul-Alam ("beloved of the world") and Sultan-ul-Arifeen ("king among those who know God"). Died at age 82 in 1576 in Srinagar. Akbar built his shrine at Hari Parbat Hill (reconstructed 1821 by Afghan governor Atta Mohammad Khan).

26. The Qadiri Order (Qadiriyya) & Punjab Connections Qadiri Silsila

Origins & Ideology

  • Founder: Founded in the 12th century by Abdul Qadir al-Jilani (born in Persia, lived in Baghdad; died 1166). In 1535, Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent constructed a dome over his Baghdad shrine after Safavid Shah Ismail I had destroyed it.
  • Wahdat al-Wajud: Upheld the doctrine of Wahdat al-Wajud (Unity of Existence — all beings are part of a single reality). Emphasized strict adherence to Sharia alongside Sufi practices and maintained a historically non-political stance.

Sai Mian Mir (Lahore)

  • Lineage & Location: Resided in Dharampura near Lahore. Direct descendant of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab. Founder of the Mian Khel branch of the Qadiri order. Younger sister: Bibi Jamal Khatun (prominent Sufi saint).
  • Harmandir Sahib Foundation: Close friend of Guru Arjan Dev Ji, who invited him to lay the foundation stone of Sri Harmandir Sahib (recorded in Ghulam Muhayy-ud-Din / Bute Shah’s Tawarikh-i-Punjab, 1848).
  • Dara Shikoh Connection: Spiritual guide of Mughal prince Dara Shikoh, who compiled Mian Mir's biography titled Sakinat al-Aulia.

Mullah Shah Badakhshi & Sakhi Sarwar

  • Mullah Shah Badakhshi: Born in Araska (Badakhshan, Afghanistan). Successor to Mian Mir. Spiritual mentor of prince Dara Shikoh and princess Jahanara Begum (Dara Shikoh documented his teachings in a dedicated chapter in Sakinatul Awliya).
  • Syed Ahmad Sultan (Sakhi Sarwar): 12th-century Punjabi saint born to Syed Zain-ul-Abidin in 1128 at Shahkot in Jhang. Mother was daughter of a Khokhar herdsman. Relocated to Nigaha (Dera Ghazi Khan), killed by relatives in 1174. Shrine built during Aurangzeb's reign, repaired by Lakhpat Rai and Jaspat Rai in 1730; deeply revered across Hindu, Sikh, and Muslim communities.

27. The Suhrawardi & Firdausi Orders Multan & Eastern India

Suhrawardi Order (Multan Center)

  • Origins: Originated in Baghdad by Abu-Al-Najib Suhrawardi (studied Shafiite law at Nizamiyya, retreat on Tigris). Expanded by nephew Shahab al-Din Abu Hafs Umar Suhrawardi.
  • State Engagement: Unlike the Chishti order, Suhrawardis accepted gifts, royal jagirs, and government posts in the ecclesiastical department. Combined scholarship (ilm) with mysticism.
  • Bahauddin Zakariya (Baha-ul-Haq): Central Asian family settled at Kut Karur near Multan. Settled in Multan in 1222. Under his influence, Multan was titled "Baghdad of the East".

Firdausi Order (Branch of Suhrawardi)

  • Origin & Geography: Branch of Suhrawardi Silsila founded by Shaikh Badruddin Samarkandi (born 1216, Samarkand; prayed at Yamuna, buried near Firozshah Kotla). Popular in Bihar, Jharkhand, and Bengal. Established base at Rajgir, Bihar (late 14th century). Adhered to Wahdat-ul-Wajud.
  • Rukunudin Firdausi: Founder of Firdausi order in India; hospice in Kilokhari (built by Sultan Kaiqubad). Credited with starting Urs Mubarak in India.
  • Sheikh Najeeb Uddin Firdausi: Son of Imaduddin Firdausi; died 1332 under Muhammad bin Tughlaq; shrine east of Hauz-e-Shamsi in Mehrauli.
  • Sharfuddin Ahmed Yahya Maneri: Known as Makhdoom-ul-Mulk Bihari. Born at Maner (Patna); tutored 24 years by Sharfuddin Bukhari; disciple of Najeeb Uddin Firdausi. Settled at Bihar Sharif, where Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq built a Khanqah for him.

28. The Naqshbandi & Kubrawiya Silsilas Orthodoxy & Politics

Naqshbandi Order & Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi

  • Lineage & Early Masters: Traced to Prophet Muhammad and Abu Bakr. Yusuf Hamadani (born 1048; shrine at Merv, Turkmenistan) established eight principles ("holy phrases"). Indian pioneer was Khwaja Baqi Billah from Kabul (disciple of Amkanagi, pen name "Berang", died 1603 in Delhi).
  • Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi: Born May 26, 1564 in Sirhind, Punjab. Dubbed himself "Mujaddid Alif Sani" ("Reformer of the Second Millennium"). Opposed Akbar's liberal policies and pantheism; demanded reimposition of Jizyah. Influenced Emperor Jahangir; exerted pressure leading to the execution of Guru Arjan Dev Ji in 1606. Died Nov 30, 1624; tomb in Sirhind.

Kubrawiya Order

  • Founder: Founded in the 13th century by Najmuddin Kubra of Khwarezm (disciple of Ruzbihan Baqli and Ammar Bitlisi). Killed by Mongols during the 1221 conquest of Konye-Urgench. Lineage traces through Ali.
  • Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani: Spread order in Kashmir Valley. Born in Hamadan, Iran; died in Swat (1385), buried in Tajikistan. Titles: Shah-e-Hamadan, Amir-i-Kabir, and Ali Sani ("Second Ali"). Author of Zakhirat-ul-Muluk (book on politics, governance, and social conduct).

29. Bhakti Movement: Philosophical Foundations Devotional Surge

Origins, Meaning & Exponents

  • Etymology: Derived from Sanskrit root Bhaja, meaning "to utter" literally, with inner meaning "to love with honour". Devotionally denotes unquestionable faith and utter dedication to God.
  • Regional Origin: Began in South India in response to conquests, gathering momentum from the 8th century AD to the 15th century AD.
  • Earliest Reformer: Adi Shankaracharya was the earliest reformer-saint in the South.
  • Chief Exponents: Shankara, Ramanuja, Kabir, Nanak, Shri Chaitanya, Mirabai, Ramananda, Namdev, Nimbarka, Madhava, Eknath, Surdas, Tulsidas, Tukaram, Vallabhacharya, and Chandidas.

30. Bhagat Kabir Ji & Bhagat Ravidas Ji Prime Bhakti Saints

Bhagat Kabir Ji (1398 – 1518 AD)

  • Lifeline: Born 1398 AD in Benaras (Varanasi), 71 years before Guru Nanak Dev Ji. Kabirpanthis believe he lived 120 years; passed away in 1518 at Maghar (Uttar Pradesh).
  • Family & Trade: Adopted by Muslim weaver couple Neeru and Neema; trade: weaver. Wife: Mata Loi; children: son Kamal and daughter Kamali. Disciple of Ramananda.
  • Hymns in SGGS: 541 hymns in 17 Ragas — highest number of hymns by any Bhagat in Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Compositions: Kabir Bijak, Kabir Parachai, Sakhi Granth, Adi Granth, Anurag Sagar, Kabir Granthawali.

Bhagat Ravidas Ji (b. Jan 30, 1399)

  • Birth & Roots: Born in Kashi at village Sir Gobardhan (now Shri Guru Ravidass Janam Asthan). Mother: Mata Kalsi; Father: Santokh Dass. Belonged to the leather-working Chamar community. Monotheist; admired Kabir and Namdev.
  • Hymns in SGGS: 41 hymns in 16 Ragas included in Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Khuralgarh Sahib: Historical center at Kharali village, Garhshanker (Hoshiarpur district). Also termed Charan Choh Ganga Sri Guru Ravidas Ji. Visited Khuralgarh persuaded by disciple Meera Bai to uplift weaker sections.
  • Minar-e-Begampura: 151 feet high hallmark of Guru Ravidass Memorial at Shri Khuralgarh Sahib, built across 14.4 acres and completed by November 2016.

31. Devotional Bhagats, Mystics & Bhatt Bhikha Bhagats Dossier

Bhagat Namdev Ji (1270 – 1350)

  • Birth: Oct 29, 1270 at village Naras-Vamani, Maharashtra to tailor Damshet and Gonabai. Settled at Pandharpur in his 50s.
  • Compositions: Renowned for Abhangas and Tirthawali (hymns praising Lord Vitthala). Contributed 61 hymns in Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Shrine at Ghuman (Punjab): Dedicated shrine where he was cremated following travels from Maharashtra. Rebuilt by Sardar Jassa Singh Ramgarhia; tank repaired by Rani Sada Kaur (mother-in-law of Ranjit Singh).

Bhagat Sain Ji

  • Birth & Family: Born in Amritsar in Samvat 1400 (~1343 AD) to Bhai Mukand Rai and Mata Jivani (some sources state MP/Rajasthan). Married Bibi Sahib Devi; son named Bhai Nai.
  • Follower & Memorials: Follower of Ramanand. 6 December celebrated as birth anniversary in Punjab. Gurdwara and Sarovar mark his meditation site at Partabpura, Jalandhar District.

Bhagat Dhanna, Pipa & Beni

  • Bhagat Dhanna: Born April 20, 1415 at Dhuan Kalan near Deoli, Tonk district, Rajasthan into a Jatt farming family. Unlettered devotee. 3 Shabads in SGGS on Angs 487, 488, and 695.
  • Bhagat Pipa: Born ~1425 AD into a royal family; prince of Gagaraun (Jhalawar, Rajasthan) who renounced the throne. Contributed 1 hymn in Rag Dhanasari in SGGS. Legend states he met Guru Nanak in Toda town.
  • Bhagat Beni: Believed born in Asani (UP); contemporary of Guru Nanak. Contributed 3 hymns in SGGS across Siri Raag, Ramkali, and Prabhati.

Ramanand, Surdas, Sadhana, Bhikhan & Bhatt Bhikha

  • Bhagat Ramanand: Mid-14th century Brahmin Vaishnav saint; reviver of the Bairagi/Ramanand sect. Contributed 1 hymn under Basant Raga in SGGS. Authored Sri Vaisnav Matanbuj Bhaskar and Sri Ramacharan Padhati.
  • Bhagat Surdas: Born 1478–1483 in Gram Sihi (Haryana) or Runkata. Blind poet devoted to Krishna; authored Sursagar ("Ocean of Sur") and Surasaravali. Represented in SGGS by a single line.
  • Bhagat Sadhana (Sadhna Qasai): Born 1180 in Sehwan, Sindh; butcher by profession. Passed away in Sirhind, where a historic mosque built in his memory is preserved by the Punjab Government. (1 hymn in SGGS).
  • Bhagat Bhikhan: Born 1480 in Kakori near Lucknow; devoted to the Divine Name (Hari). Contributed 2 hymns on Page 659 of SGGS.
  • Bhatt Bhikha: Brahmin bard from Sultanpur Lodhi (Kapurthala). Received Amrit from Guru Amar Das Ji; contemporary of Guru Arjan Dev Ji. Introduced 16 other Brahmin minstrels to the Guru's Darbar. Contributed 2 hymns to Guru Granth Sahib.
