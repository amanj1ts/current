1.1 Prehistoric Cultures: Stone Age to Chalcolithic Farming 5 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

Human occupation in India dates back to the Middle Pleistocene (~5,00,000 BC). Prehistory is categorized by stone tool technology, geological climate shifts (Pleistocene Ice Age to warm Holocene), and subsistence transitions from food-gathering to food-producing.

Period & Timeline Climate & Geology Characteristic Tools & Material Subsistence & Features Key Archaeological Sites
Lower Palaeolithic
5,00,000 – 50,000 BC		 Greater part of Ice Age (Pleistocene) Large pebbles or flakes; Hand-axes, cleavers, and choppers. Hunting and food gathering; no knowledge of agriculture or house building. Traces of ash at Kurnool (AP) show discovery of fire. Soan/Sohan Valley (Punjab, now Pakistan), Belan Valley (Mirzapur, UP), Didwana (Rajasthan), Hunsgi (Karnataka), Bhimbetka (MP).
Middle Palaeolithic
50,000 – 40,000 BC		 Pleistocene (regional variations) Flake industries: varieties of blades, points, borers, and scrapers made of flakes. Hunting and gathering along river valleys and rock shelters. Nevasa (Maharashtra, on Pravara river — a tributary of Godavari), Narmada Valley, South of Tungabhadra, Soan Valley.
Upper (Late) Palaeolithic
40,000 – 10,000 BC		 Last phase of Ice Age; climate became less humid and comparatively warm. Massive flakes, parallel-sided blades, scrapers, and Burins (chisel-like tools used specifically for engraving on soft stone, wood, and bone). Appearance of modern humans (Homo sapiens) and new flint industries; earliest rock paintings. Bhimbetka caves (45 km South of Bhopal), Upper levels of Gujarat dunes, Patne (Maharashtra).
Mesolithic Age
9000/8000 – 4000 BC		 End of Ice Age (~9000 BC); warm and dry climate led to expansion of grasslands. Microlithic stone tools (tiny geometric stone tools hafted onto bone/wood handles). Transitional phase ("Hunters and Herders"); hunting, fishing, food gathering, and earliest domestication of animals. Majority of rock paintings. Bagor (Rajasthan) & Adamgarh (MP) — earliest animal domestication; Chhota Nagpur plateau, South of River Krishna.
Neolithic Age
7000 – 1000 BC		 Holocene (settled river valleys & flat plains) Polished stone tools and stone axes; bone implements; rubbing stones, mortars, and pestles. Earliest food producers (farming communities); invention of wheel and pottery (hand-made then wheel-made) for grain storage. Dog domesticated first. Mehrgarh (Baluchistan, 7000 BC: wheat, barley, cotton, mud-brick houses); Burzahom (J&K: pit-dwellings, coarse grey pottery, dogs buried with masters); Gufkral ("cave of the potter", J&K); Chirand (near Patna: abundant bone tools); Koldihwa/Allahabad (rice cultivation in 6th millennium BC); Piklihal, Maski, Brahmagiri, Hallur (South India: cattle herders, ash mounds); Daojali Hading (Assam).
Chalcolithic Age
2300/1800 – 800 BC		 Non-alluvial & riverine plains Blade-based stone tools (microliths) + Copper tools. First metal used was Copper. Shift to crop cultivation (barley, wheat, lentil, rice, bajra, cotton); Black-and-Red Ware (BRW) wheel-turned painted pottery (used lota, no thali); rural village economy; high infant mortality. Ahar & Gilund (Rajasthan: stone tools absent at Ahar; copper blade industry at Gilund); Kayatha, Eran, Navdatoli (MP: all foodgrains found at Navdatoli); Jorwe, Nevasa, Daimabad, Chandoli, Inamgaon (Maharashtra); Pandu Rajar Dhibi & Mahisdal (WB).
🎨 Prehistoric Rock Art (Bhimbetka & Lakhudiyar):
  • Bhimbetka (Vindhyas, MP): Discovered with over 500 painted rock shelters across 10 sq km extending from Upper Palaeolithic to Mesolithic periods. Depicts hunting scenes of bison, tigers, boars, and communal dances; perching birds (which live on grain) are absent in the earliest paintings, confirming a pre-agricultural hunter-gatherer society.
  • Lakhudiyar Caves (Almora, Uttarakhand): Located on the banks of the Suyal River ("Lakhudiyar" literally means one lakh caves). Famous for prehistoric paintings of humans in stick-like forms, a long-snouted animal, a fox, and multiple-legged lizard, alongside wavy lines and geometric designs in white, black, and red ochre.
🏺 Chalcolithic Social & Burial Nuances:
  • Beginnings of Social Inequality: In graves at Chandoli and Nevasa (Western Maharashtra), some children were buried with copper-bead necklaces around their necks, while others had only pots.
  • Burial Orientations: Dead were buried in urns under house floors — in North-to-South position in Maharashtra (extended burial), and East-to-West position in South India; fractional burial prevailed in West Bengal.
  • Fortified Settlements: Inamgaon (5-roomed houses, circular pit houses, Mother Goddess terracotta), Kayatha, and Eran were fortified and surrounded by moats.

1.2 Indus Valley Civilization (Harappan Bronze Age: 2600–1900 BC Mature Phase) 3 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

🏛️ Town Planning & Grid Layout:
  • Bipartite Division: Planned Harappan cities were characterized by an elevated Citadel (West) built on mud-brick platforms for the ruling class/public buildings, and a Lower Town (East) inhabited by common people.
  • Grid System: Streets in the lower town intersected each other at $90^\circ$ (right angles), dividing the city into rectangular blocks.
  • Exceptions: Chanhudaro is the only city without a citadel. Dholavira was divided into three parts (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town). Banawali had an oval-shaped layout with radial streets and lacked a systematic drainage pattern.
💧 Drainage, Bricks & Trade:
  • Burnt Bricks & Privacy: Harappans used standardized burnt bricks (gypsum mortar in the Great Bath). Ground-floor walls facing main streets had no windows, and entrances did not give a direct view of the central courtyard.
  • Trade & Economy: Barter system (no metal money). Mesopotamian records (from 2350 BC) call the Indus region Meluha, with intermediate stations at Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan.
  • Weights & Script: Cubical chert weights in multiples of 16 (binary for lower, decimal for higher). Undeciphered boustrophedon pictographic script (250–400 signs).
🧭 Geographical Boundaries & Imports:
  • North: Manda (Chenab river, Jammu)
  • South: Daimabad (Pravara river, Maharashtra)
  • East: Alamgirpur (Hindon river, Meerut, UP)
  • West: Sutkagendor (Dasht river, Baluchistan)
  • Key Imports: Copper from Khetri (Rajasthan) & Oman; Gold from Karnataka & Afghanistan; Lapis Lazuli from Shortughai (Afghanistan); Carnelian from Saurashtra; Jade from Central Asia.

Chanhudaro

Banawali Saraswati/Ghaggar (Fatehabad, Haryana) R.S. Bisht (1974) Terracotta model of plough (also in Cholistan), largest number of high-quality barley grains, oval-shaped settlement, radial streets, wash-basin structure.
Rakhigarhi Ghaggar-Hakra (Hisar, Haryana) Suraj Bhan (1969) Largest Harappan site in India; fire altars, cylindrical seal, terracotta wheels.
Ropar (Rupnagar) Sutlej river (Punjab) Y.D. Sharma (1953) First site excavated in independent India; dog buried with human in oval pit; buildings of stone and soil; 6 cultural phases (Harappan, PGW, NBPW, Kushana, Gupta, Medieval).
Surkotada Kachchh (Gujarat) J.P. Joshi (1964) Actual remains (bones) of horse reported here (though horse was not in regular use in IVC); pot burials.

1.3 The Vedic Age (1500–600 BC) & Heterodox Movements (Jainism & Buddhism) 5 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

📖 Vedic Corpus & Key Vocabulary (High-Yield):
  • Rig Veda (1500–1000 BC): Oldest Veda (first composed); 1028 hymns (Suktas) across 10 Mandalas. Mandalas II–VII are the oldest ("Family Books"). Mandala III has the Gayatri Mantra (composed by Vishvamitra); Mandala IX is dedicated to Soma; Mandala X contains the Purusha Sukta (four varnas) and Nadi Stuti Sukta.
  • Gomat: Term used in Vedic literature for a wealthy person who possessed cattle.
  • Duhitri: Rig Vedic term for a daughter (literally "one who milks the cow").
  • Gavishthi: Rig Vedic term for war (literally "search for cows").
  • Aghanya: Term for cow ("not to be killed").
  • Ayas: Copper/Bronze in Rig Veda; in Later Vedic texts, Iron is called Shyama or Krishna Ayas.
  • Vrihi: Term for Rice in Later Vedic texts; Yava: Barley; Godhuma: Wheat.
  • Boghaz-Koi Inscription (1400 BC, Asia Minor): Mentions four Vedic gods — Indra, Varuna, Mitra, and Nasatya.
🕉️ 24th Tirthankara Mahavira & Buddhism Essentials:
  • Jainism Tirthankaras: 1st: Rishabhanatha/Adinath (Bull symbol, born in Ayodhya); 23rd: Parshvanatha (Snake symbol, born in Varanasi, gave 4 vows); 24th & Last: Vardhaman Mahavira (Lion symbol).
  • Vardhaman Mahavira (540–468 BC): Born at Kundagrama (Vaishali); father Siddhartha (chief of Jnatrika clan), mother Trishala (sister of Lichchhavi chief Chetaka). Attained Kaivalya at age 42; died at age 72 at Pavapuri (near Rajgir). Added the 5th vow of Brahmacharya (celibacy). Taught in Prakrit.
  • Jain Councils: 1st Council (300 BC, Pataliputra) under Sthulabhadra (compilation of 12 Angas; split into Shwetambara & Digambara under Bhadrabahu). 2nd Council (512 AD, Vallabhi, Gujarat) under Devardhi Kshemashramana (final compilation of Agamas in Ardhamagadhi).
  • Gautama Buddha (563–483 BC): Born at Lumbini (Shakya clan); Enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; First Sermon (Dhammachakrapravartana) at Sarnath; Mahaparinirvana at Kushinagar. Taught in Pali.
Dimension Early / Rig Vedic Period (1500–1000 BC) Later Vedic Period (1000–600 BC)
Core Texts & Geography Rig Veda only. Settled in Sapta Sindhu (Land of Seven Rivers: Indus, Saraswati/Naditama, and 5 Punjab rivers: Vitasta/Jhelum, Askini/Chenab, Parushni/Ravi, Vipasa/Beas, Sutudri/Sutlej). Sama, Yajur, Atharva Vedas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads. Eastward expansion into Indo-Gangetic Doab & Upper Ganga plains; emergence of prominent kingdoms like Kuru, Panchala, Kashi, Kosala, and Videha.
Economic Base & Technology Primarily pastoral society with cattle as the central economic resource. Semi-nomadic; limited barley (Yava) cultivation; voluntary tribute called Bali. Agriculture became the main occupation driven by the wider use of Iron tools (Shyama/Krishna Ayas) for clearing forests and ploughing. Rice (Vrihi), wheat, and sugarcane grown; Painted Grey Ware (PGW) pottery.
Polity & Assemblies Tribal chief (Rajan) checked by clan assemblies: Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha, Gana. Women attended Sabha and Vidatha. Loyalty was to the tribe (Jana — mentioned 275 times; Janapada not used once). Rise of territorial kingdoms (Rashtra and Janapada). Vidatha disappeared; women excluded from Sabha. Royal power elevated via grand rituals: Rajasuya, Ashvamedha, and Vajapeya. Tax collector: Sangrihitri / Bhagadugha.
Society & Religion Tribal, largely egalitarian, occupation-based divisions; widow remarriage (Niyoga) allowed; no child marriage. Nature gods: Indra (Purandara, 250 hymns), Agni (200 hymns), Varuna (Rita/cosmic order). Rigid birth-based 4-fold Varna system; institution of Gotra appeared; 3 Ashramas mentioned. Prajapati (Creator), Rudra, and Vishnu replaced Indra and Agni in prominence; elaborate animal sacrifices dominated by Brahmanas.

1.4 Mauryan & Post-Mauryan Era (Shungas, Satavahanas, Indo-Greeks, Shakas & Kushanas) 9 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

🦁 Mauryan Empire Quick-Fire Anchors (322–185 BC):
  • Chandragupta Maurya: Identified as Sandrokottas by Greek writers; defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BC), who sent ambassador Megasthenes (author of Indica). Embraced Jainism under Bhadrabahu and died via Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola.
  • Bindusara: Called Amitrochates (Amitraghata — Slayer of Foes) by Greeks; Syrian king Antiochus I sent ambassador Deimachus; patronized the Ajivika sect (founded by Makkhali Gosala).
  • Ashoka (269–232 BC): Kalinga War (261 BC, mentioned in Major Rock Edict XIII); called Devanampiya Piyadassi (personal name 'Ashoka' in Maski, Gujjara, Nettur, Udegolam minor edicts). Scripts: Brahmi (India), Kharosthi (NW Pakistan — Mansehra & Shahbazgarhi), Greek & Aramaic (Kandahar); deciphered by James Prinsep (1837).
  • Mauryan Administration: 18 top officials called Tirthas; 27 economic superintendents called Adhyakshas. Samaharta (Chief of Revenue Assessment) and Sannidhata (Chief Custodian of Treasury).
🛣️ Post-Mauryan Trade Routes & Architecture:
  • Uttarapatha & Dakshinapatha: The two major internal land trade routes facilitating commerce in ancient/post-Mauryan India. Uttarapatha stretched from Taxila/Purushapura in the North-West across the Gangetic plains to the port of Tamralipti (Bengal). Dakshinapatha linked Pataliputra/Kaushambi through Vidisha and Ujjain down to Pratishthana (Paithan) in the Deccan.
  • Shungas & Satavahanas in Art: Following the Mauryan decline, the Shungas (North/Central) and Satavahanas (Deccan) contributed immensely to stupa architecture and rock-cut sculpture.
  • Sanchi & Bharhut Stupas: Originally built of brick by Ashoka, the Great Stupa at Sanchi (MP) was enlarged to twice its size, encased in stone, and adorned with ornate stone railings (Vedika) and sculptured gateways (Toranas) under the Shungas (Agnimitra/Pushyamitra) and Satavahanas.
Dynasty & Capital Key Rulers & Titles Haryana 2026 Exam Pointers & NCERT Facts
Shunga Dynasty
(185–75 BC) | Capital: Pataliputra & Vidisha		 Pushyamitra Shunga (Founder), Agnimitra, Bhagabhadra, Devabhuti (Last). • Pushyamitra Shunga served as the Commander-in-Chief (Senapati) of the Mauryan army and assassinated the last Mauryan ruler Brihadratha.
• Came into conflict with the invading Bactrian Greek ruler Demetrius in the North-West.
• Performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices (officiated by grammarian Patanjali, author of Mahabhashya).
• Greek ambassador Heliodorus (sent by Antialkidas of Taxila) erected the Besnagar/Vidisha Garuda Pillar dedicated to Lord Vasudeva during Shunga king Bhagabhadra's reign.
Satavahana Dynasty
(c. 1st C. BC – 3rd C. AD) | Capital: Pratishthana (Paithan)		 Simuka (Founder), Satakarni I (Lord of Dakshinapatha), Hala, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Vashishthiputra Pulumayi, Yajna Sri Satakarni. • In the Puranas, the Satavahanas are referred to as Andhras (Andhrabhritya / Andhrajatiyah).
• First Indian rulers to grant tax-free land/villages to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks with administrative immunities.
• Issued predominantly lead coins (also potin, copper, bronze); matronymic prefixes (named after mothers, e.g., Gautamiputra), though succession was patriarchal.
• King Hala compiled the Prakrit anthology Gathasaptashati (Gaha Sattasai). Patronized Karle Chaitya, Amaravati, and Nagarjunakonda stupas.
Indo-Greeks & Parthians
Capital: Sakala (Sialkot) & Taxila		 Demetrius, Menander I (Milinda); Parthian king: Gondophernes. • Indo-Greeks were the first rulers in India to issue coins attributed to specific kings and the first to issue gold coins.
• Menander converted to Buddhism under monk Nagasena (dialogues recorded in Pali text Milindapanho).
• Famous Parthian king Gondophernes (St. Thomas visited India during his reign; Takht-i-Bahi inscription).
Shakas (Western Kshatrapas)
Ujjain & Kathiawar		 Maues, Nahapana, Rudradaman I (130–150 AD). • Rudradaman I is famous for the Girnar / Junagadh Rock Inscription (150 AD) — the first ever long inscription in chaste (pure) Sanskrit.
• Records the repair of Sudarshana Lake (originally built by Pushyagupta, governor of Chandragupta Maurya) without imposing forced tax (Vishti). Defeated Satavahanas twice.
Kushana Empire
(Yuezhi tribe of Central Asia) | Capitals: Purushapura (Peshawar) & Mathura		 Kujula Kadphises (minted copper coins), Vima Kadphises (introduced gold coins), Kanishka I, Huvishka, Vasudeva I. • Kushana rulers adopted the title Devaputra ("Son of God") inspired by Chinese rulers who called themselves "Sons of Heaven".
• Kanishka I assumed power in 78 AD, inaugurating the Shaka Era, and oversaw the empire's widest territorial expansion.
• Kanishka I convened the Fourth Buddhist Council at Kundalavana (Kashmir) presided over by Vasumitra (with Ashvaghosha as deputy), marking the rise of Mahayana Buddhism.
• Patronized Gandhara and Mathura schools of art, and scholars Charaka (Charaka Samhita), Ashvaghosha (Buddhacharita), and Nagarjuna (Madhyamika Sutra). Issued the purest gold coins (Dinars).

1.5 The Imperial Guptas, Post-Gupta Successor States & Temple Builders 8 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

Ruler / Dynasty Inscriptions & Titles Conquests, Coinage & Administrative Features (Haryana 2026 Focus)
Chandragupta I
(319–335 AD)		 Title: Maharajadhiraja (First Gupta after Srigupta & Ghatotkacha). Started the Gupta Era (319–320 AD); married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi and issued King-and-Queen (Kumaradevi) type gold coins.
Samudragupta
(335–380 AD)		 Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prasasti) composed by court poet Harisena in Sanskrit Champu Kavya style. • Fate of the 9 Rulers of Aryavarta (Ganga-Yamuna Doab): They were uprooted, exterminated, and slain (Prasabhoddharana), and their kingdoms were directly annexed into the Gupta Empire.
• 12 Rulers of Dakshinapatha (South India): Conquered and then liberated/reinstated on paying tribute (Grahana-moksha-anugraha).
• Called "Napoleon of India" by V.A. Smith; depicted playing the Veena on his coins; granted permission to Sri Lankan king Meghavarman to build a monastery at Bodh Gaya.
Chandragupta II
(380–412 AD)		 Title: Vikramaditya & Sakari; Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription. Defeated Western Shakas of Malwa & Gujarat, gaining access to Western sea ports (Bharuch/Broach) and issuing silver coins. Married daughter Prabhavatigupta to Vakataka king Rudrasena II. Adorned by Navratnas (Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira, Dhanvantari); Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien visited India.
Kumaragupta I &
Skandagupta
(415–467 AD)		 Bilsad, Mandsaur (Kumaragupta); Bhitari Pillar & Junagadh Rock (Skandagupta). • Kumaragupta I (415–455 AD) founded Nalanda University.
• Skandagupta (455–467 AD): Repulsed the fierce invasion of the Hunas (Bhitari inscription) and repaired Sudarshana Lake again (by governor Chakrapalita). He was the last Gupta ruler to issue Silver coins in Western India.
Maukharis of Kannauj
(6th Century AD)		 Haraha Inscription; Title: Maharajadhiraja. • Ruled Western UP (Kannauj) and parts of Magadha after Gupta decline.
• Isanavarman (554 AD) was the first Maukhari ruler to assume the title Maharajadhiraja after defeating the Later Guptas and Andhra/Sulika forces, asserting independent sovereignty. Later ruler Grahavarman married Harsha's sister Rajyashri and was killed by Gauda king Shashanka.
Pushyabhuti (Vardhana) Dynasty
(606–647 AD)		 Banskhera & Madhuban copper plates; Banabhatta's Harshacharita. • Original Capital: Thanesar (Kurukshetra, Haryana); Harshavardhana later shifted the capital to Kannauj.
• Chinese pilgrim Hiuen-Tsang (Xuanzang) visited his court and attended the grand quinquennial assembly at Prayag. Harsha authored three Sanskrit plays: Priyadarsika, Ratnavali, and Nagananda.
Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi)
(6th–8th C. AD)		 Aihole Inscription (Bagalkot, Karnataka) composed in Sanskrit by Jain court poet Ravikirti. • Pulakeshin II (609–642 AD): Most celebrated Chalukya ruler; defeated Harshavardhana on the banks of the Narmada river, halting Harsha's southward expansion (recorded in the Aihole Prasasti).
• Developed Vesara/Deccan temple architecture at Aihole ("Town of Temples" — Ladh Khan, Durga, Meguti temples), Badami, and Pattadakal (Virupaksha & Papanatha temples).
📜 Post-Gupta Administrative Hallmark (Brahmadeya & Agrahara):

Across Gupta successor states — Vardhanas, Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas, and Rashtrakutas — the most prominent administrative and socio-economic development was the extensive grant of tax-free land (Agrahara / Brahmadeya) to Brahmanas and religious institutions, as well as land assignments to state officials in lieu of cash salaries.

  • Surrender of royal fiscal and administrative rights (police/magisterial immunities) to priestly landlords fostered Indian Feudalism and decentralized authority.
  • Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj (8th–10th C. AD): Fought among Palas (Gopala, Dharmapala — founded Vikramashila University), Gurjara-Pratiharas (Nagabhatta I & II, Vatsaraja, Mihir Bhoja — title Adivaraha), and Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga, Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha I — author of Kannada text Kavirajamarga).
🛕 Iconic Post-Gupta & Early Medieval Monuments (Core Syllabus List):
  • Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram, Tamil Nadu): Built by Pallava king Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla, 630–668 AD). He defeated and killed Pulakeshin II, captured Badami, and assumed the title Vatapikonda. Famous for monolithic rock-cut Rathas (Seven Pagodas) and the Shore Temple (Narasimhavarman II).
  • Kailasa Temple at Ellora (Cave 16, Maharashtra): Magnificent monolithic rock-cut Shiva temple carved top-down from a single cliff by Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (756–773 AD).
  • Rani ki Vav (Patan, Gujarat): Multi-storeyed stepwell (Maru-Gurjara style) on the banks of Saraswati river, commissioned in the 11th century by Queen Udayamati in memory of her husband Bhima I of the Solanki (Chaulukya) dynasty.
  • Konark Sun Temple ("Black Pagoda", Odisha): Built in the 13th century (c. 1250 AD) in Kalinga Nagara style (designed as a colossal 24-wheeled chariot of Surya) by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty.

2.1 Turkish Conquest & Landmark Battles of Medieval India 2 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

Battle / Invasion & Year Combatants Victor, Strategic Trigger & Historical Consequence
Arab Conquest of Sind
712 AD		 Muhammad bin Qasim (commander of Al-Hajjaj) vs. King Dahir of Sind. First Arab conquest in India; recorded in Chach Nama (by Abu Bakr). Further Arab expansion into Western India was checked by Gurjara-Pratihara ruler Nagabhatta I.
Mahmud of Ghazni's 17 Raids
1001–1027 AD		 Mahmud of Ghazni vs. Jayapala & Anandapala (Hindushahi rulers of Punjab/Peshawar). Defeated Jayapala (1001 AD) and Anandapala near Waihind/Nandana (1008–09 AD); plundered Somnath Temple (Gujarat) in 1025/1026 AD during Solanki king Bhima I's reign. Scholar Al-Biruni accompanied him and wrote Kitab-ul-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind in Arabic.
First Battle of Tarain
1191 AD		 Prithviraj Chauhan III (Chauhan Rajput ruler) vs. Muhammad Ghori (Muizzuddin Muhammad bin Sam). Prithviraj Chauhan won decisively. Triggered by rival claims over the strategic fort of Tabarhind (Bathinda, Punjab). Ghori was wounded and saved by a Khalji horseman.
Second Battle of Tarain
1192 AD		 Muhammad Ghori vs. Prithviraj Chauhan III. Muhammad Ghori won due to superior cavalry speed and organization. Prithviraj was captured near Saraswati (Sirsa); laid the foundation of Turkish rule in North India.
Battle of Chandawar
1194 AD		 Muhammad Ghori vs. Jaichand (Gahadavala ruler of Kannauj). Ghori won; Jaichand was killed, extending Turkish control over Eastern UP and Bihar (where Bakhtiyar Khalji destroyed Nalanda & Vikramashila universities).
First Battle of Panipat
21 April 1526 AD		 Zahiruddin Muhammad Babur vs. Ibrahim Lodi (Last Lodi Sultan). Babur won (using Tulughma flanking tactic and Ottoman artillery under Ustad Ali & Mustafa) after invitations from Daulat Khan Lodi (Governor of Punjab) and Rana Sanga. Ibrahim Lodi was the only Sultan of Delhi killed on the battlefield, establishing Mughal rule in India.

2.3 The Mughal Empire & Sur Administration (1526–1707 AD) 5 Qs in Haryana 2026 Shifts

⚖️ Sher Shah Suri, Akbar's Zabt & Sulh-i-Kul (Haryana 2026 Tested):
  • Zabt Revenue System: First introduced by Sher Shah Suri (1540–45 AD) based on crop yields (Ray schedule) and measurement of cultivable land, and later perfected by Akbar and his finance minister Raja Todar Mal into the Ain-i-Dahsala (Bandobast) system in 1580 AD (average produce and prices of last 10 years, with 1/3rd fixed as state share). Land was classified into Polaj (annually cultivated), Parauti (left fallow 1–2 years), Chachar (fallow 3–4 years), and Banjar (uncultivated 5+ years).
  • Doctrine of Sulh-i-Kul ("Universal Peace"): Formulated by Akbar (with Abul Fazl); aimed at promoting interfaith dialogue, religious harmony, and multi-religious administrative appointments across the empire without discrimination. Akbar abolished pilgrimage tax (1563) and Jizyah (1564), and established the Ibadat Khana (Hall of Prayer) at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 AD.
  • Badalgarh / Agra Fort: Originally a brick fort known as Badalgarh (held by Sikandar/Ibrahim Lodi and Raja Badal Singh), Akbar reconstructed it using red sandstone from Barauli (Rajasthan) beginning in 1565 AD.
📜 Mughal Administration, Provincial Diwan & Persian Farmans:
  • Provincial Diwan: In Mughal provincial (Suba) administration, the Diwan was the head of the revenue department, primarily responsible for revenue administration and maintaining provincial financial accounts (acting as an independent check on the Subedar / Nazim, who handled executive and military affairs).
  • Other Key Mughal Officials: Mir Bakshi (Head of Military Department, intelligence, and Mansabdari payrolls); Mir Saman (In-charge of imperial household and royal Karkhanas); Faujdar (law and order head of a Sarkar/district); Amalguzar (revenue collector of a Sarkar); Kotwal (town police chief).
  • Language of Imperial Farmans: Mughal imperial farmans (royal orders), court chronicles (Akbarnama, Padshahnama), and official administrative records were primarily prepared in Persian. (Note: Babur's memoir Tuzuk-i-Baburi / Baburnama was written in Chagatai Turkish and later translated into Persian by Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana).

Part III: Targeted Punjab Module (Ancient & Medieval Anchor)

State-Specific High-Probability Module

In the Haryana version of the exam, the regional focus appeared through questions on Thanesar (Pushyabhuti capital), Banawali/Rakhigarhi, and Battles of Tarain & Panipat. For the Punjab Court Clerk Exam, the equivalent regional intersection with NCERT Ancient & Medieval History centers on the sites and events below:

🏺 1. Prehistoric, Harappan & Vedic Punjab:
  • Soan/Sohan Valley Culture: Early/Lower Palaeolithic pebble-tool (chopper-chopping) sites discovered in the valley of the Soan river in undivided Punjab.
  • Harappan Sites in Punjab:
    • Ropar (Rupnagar, on Sutlej river): Excavated by Y.D. Sharma (1953); first Harappan site excavated in independent India; yielded 6 cultural sequences and a dog buried with a human.
    • Sanghol (Uchha Pind, Fatehgarh Sahib): Late Harappan (Bara culture) and major Kushana stupa/monastery site yielding 117 red sandstone Mathura-school sculptures.
    • Rohira (Sangrur/Malerkotla), Dher Majra (Rupnagar), and Kotla Nihang Khan (Rupnagar).
  • Rig Vedic Rivers & Battle of Ten Kings: The Dasarajna Yudha (Battle of Ten Kings) mentioned in Mandala VII of the Rig Veda was fought on the banks of the Parushni (modern Ravi) river in Punjab, where Bharata king Sudas (guided by priest Vasishtha) defeated a confederacy of 10 tribes (guided by Vishvamitra).
  • Later Vedic & Post-Mauryan Kingdoms in Punjab: Uttara Madra (near Amritsar), Eastern Madra (near Kangra), and Kekaya (on the bank of Beas river). Battle of Hydaspes (326 BC): Fought on the banks of the Jhelum (Vitasta) between Alexander and King Porus.
⚔️ 2. Medieval Sultanate, Mughal & Sikh Guru Corridors in Punjab:
  • Tabarhind (Bathinda Fort / Qila Mubarak): Strategic frontier fortress disputed by Prithviraj Chauhan and Muhammad Ghori (1191 AD); Raziya Sultan was imprisoned here in 1240 AD by rebel governor Altunia.
  • Frontier Forts of Balban: To check Mongol invasions across the River Beas, Balban repaired and garrisoned the forts of Tabarhind (Bathinda), Sunam, and Samana.
  • Firoz Shah Tughlaq's Sutlej Canal: Constructed a 200-km-long irrigation canal taking off from the River Sutlej to Hansi.
  • Daulat Khan Lodi (Governor of Lahore/Punjab): Invited Babur to invade India against Ibrahim Lodi (leading to Babur's capture of Bhera, Sialkot, and Lahore before Panipat in 1526).
  • Kalanaur (Gurdaspur): Site of Akbar's coronation on a brick platform in February 1556 AD by Bairam Khan upon Humayun's sudden death.
  • Bhakti & Sikh Movement Synchronism: Guru Nanak Dev Ji (1469–1539 AD) was a contemporary of Bahlol Lodi, Sikandar Lodi, Ibrahim Lodi, Babur (witnessed Babur's attack on Saidpur/Eminabad, described in Babur Vani), and Humayun. Sufi Saint Baba Farid (Sheikh Fariduddin Ganjshakar) of Pakpattan/Faridkot had 112 Slokas and 4 Shabads incorporated into the Adi Granth (compiled by Guru Arjan Dev Ji in 1604 AD at Amritsar).
