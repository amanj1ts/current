1.1 Acids, Bases, Salts, pH Scale & Indicators

Arihant Magbook Ch. 5 (pp. 83–84) | 38 Questions Asked in Haryana Shifts

Ultra High-Yield (1–2 Qs Every Shift)
Arrhenius Concept (Most Tested)

Acid: Furnishes H⁺ (or hydronium H₃O⁺) ions in aqueous solution (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄,柠檬 Citric acid).
Base: Furnishes OH⁻ (hydroxide) ions in aqueous solution (e.g., NaOH, KOH, NH₄OH).

Exam Trap (Asked Twice): Dry HCl gas and dry solid NaOH pellets do not show acidic/basic properties or conduct electricity because they lack water to ionize into mobile ions (H₃O⁺ / OH⁻).
Brønsted–Lowry & Water Self-Ionization

Acid: Proton (H⁺) donor.
Base: Proton (H⁺) acceptor.
Example: NH₃ (Base) + H₂O (Acid) ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻

Self-Ionization of Water (Asked 31/08 Shift-2): H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻. Water is amphoteric (amphiprotic) — it behaves as both an acid and a base.
Lewis Concept (Probable for Punjab)

Lewis Acid: Electron-pair acceptor (incomplete central octet, e.g., BF₃, AlCl₃, CO₂, SO₂ and all positively charged cations).
Lewis Base: Electron-pair donor (lone pair present, e.g., NH₃, H₂O, ROH, F⁻, Cl⁻, OH⁻).

Physical Feel & Taste: Acids taste sour (Latin acere); Bases taste bitter and feel slippery/soapy because they react with skin oils to form soap-like substances.

Must-Know Reactions & Everyday Applications (Tested Repeatedly)

1. Neutralization Reaction: Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat (Exothermic).
Classic Exam Pair: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (forms a neutral solution, pH = 7).

2. Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen Gas (H₂):
Zn + 2HCl (dil.) → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑ (burns with a pop sound).

3. Acid + Metal Carbonate / Bicarbonate → Salt + H₂O + CO₂↑ (Effervescence):
NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑ (Acid–base neutralization with gas evolution).

4. Amphoteric Oxides & Hydroxides (React with BOTH Acids & Bases):
Zinc Oxide (ZnO), Aluminium Oxide (Al₂O₃), and Beryllium Hydroxide (Be(OH)₂). Most other metal oxides (CaO, MgO) are basic; non-metal oxides (CO₂, SO₂, NO₂) are acidic.

5. Salt Hydrolysis (Why some salts are not neutral):
• Strong Acid + Strong Base → Neutral Salt (NaCl, KCl, pH = 7).
• Weak Acid + Strong Base → Basic Salt (Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, CH₃COONa — turns red litmus blue!).
• Strong Acid + Weak Base → Acidic Salt (FeCl₃, CuSO₄, NH₄Cl — turns blue litmus red!).

6. Everyday Applications of pH & Neutralization:
• Acidic Soil Treatment: Farmers add Quick lime (Calcium oxide, CaO) or Slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂).
• Toothpaste Basicity: Basic to neutralize mouth acids produced by bacteria acting on food sugars.
• Stomach Acidity: Neutralized by mild non-corrosive antacid Milk of Magnesia, Mg(OH)₂ or NaHCO₃.
• Ant Sting (Formic Acid): Neutralized by rubbing moist Baking soda (NaHCO₃) or Calamine (ZnCO₃).
• Acid Rain: NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ (Nitric acid); SO₂/SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄.

1.2 Chemical Reactions, Laws of Combination & Industrial Processes

Arihant Magbook Ch. 4 & 5 (pp. 70–85) | 44 Questions Asked in Haryana Shifts

1–2 Qs Every Shift

Physical vs. Chemical Changes

• Physical Change: Only physical state, shape, or size changes; no new substance formed; reversible. Examples: Melting of ice/wax/sulfur, boiling water, dissolving sugar/salt in water, crushing copper, breaking glass, mixing iron filings & sulfur powder, sublimation of camphor/NH₄Cl.

• Chemical Change (Reaction): One or more new substances with completely different properties are formed via bond breaking/making. Examples: Burning wood/magnesium ribbon (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO), heating iron with sulfur to form FeS, rusting of iron, souring of milk (lactose fermentation), photosynthesis, digestion, ripening of fruit.

3 Laws of Chemical Combination (All 3 Asked!)

1. Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier): Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products in a closed flask/container (e.g., burning Mg in a sealed container leaves total mass unchanged).

2. Law of Constant / Definite Proportions (Proust): A pure chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed proportion by mass, regardless of source (e.g., H₂O always has 1:8 H-to-O mass ratio).

3. Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton): When two elements combine to form more than one compound (e.g., CO and CO₂), the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other bear a simple whole-number ratio.

Reaction Type Core Mechanism Exact Equations Tested in Exam & Magbook
1. Combination Two or more substances combine to form a single product (A + B → AB). CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat (Slaking of lime)
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (Both Combination & Oxidation!)
2. Decomposition Single compound splits into two or more simpler substances (AB → A + B) using Heat, Light, or Electricity. • Thermal: CaCO₃(s) —(Heat)→ CaO(s) + CO₂(g); 2FeSO₄ —(Δ)→ Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃
• Photolytic (Sunlight): 2AgCl(s) —(Sunlight)→ 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g) (Black & white photography)
• Electrolytic: 2NaCl(l) —(Electric current)→ 2Na + Cl₂
3. Displacement More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from its salt solution (Redox electron transfer). Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq, blue) → FeSO₄(aq, pale green) + Cu(s, brown deposit)
Reactivity Series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au
4. Double Displacement Exchange of ions between two reactants; includes Precipitation & Neutralization. Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s, white ppt)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
5. Redox (Oxidation–Reduction) Simultaneous Oxidation (gain of O / loss of H / loss of electrons) & Reduction (loss of O / gain of H / gain of electrons). CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O (CuO reduced to Cu; H₂ oxidized to H₂O).
Oxidising Agent: Accepts e⁻ (gets reduced). Reducing Agent: Donates e⁻ (gets oxidized).
6. Exothermic vs. Endothermic Exothermic: Releases heat (vessel warms up).
Endothermic: Absorbs energy/light/heat.		 • Exothermic: Combustion, Respiration, Neutralization, Setting of cement.
• Endothermic: Photosynthesis (sunlight converted to chemical energy in glucose), Thermal decomposition.

Corrosion, Tarnishing & Rancidity

• Rusting of Iron (Redox): Requires both oxygen (O₂) and moisture (H₂O) to form brown flaky hydrated iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O). Prevented by Galvanisation (coating with Zinc), painting, oiling, or alloying (Stainless steel = Fe + Cr + Ni).

• Tarnishing of Silver (Asked 02/09): Silver articles turn black in air due to reaction with sulphur compounds (H₂S) to form black Silver Sulphide (Ag₂S).

• Green Coating on Copper: Basic copper carbonate (CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂).

• Rancidity (Asked 01/09): Aerial oxidation of fats and oils in food (producing foul smell/taste). Potato chip packets are flushed with unreactive Nitrogen (N₂) gas to prevent rancidity.

Industrial Processes & Catalysts (Arihant p. 73)

Role of a Catalyst: Increases reaction rate by lowering activation energy without being consumed or altering the equilibrium constant.

  • • Haber Process: Manufacture of Ammonia (NH₃) for fertilizers (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃). Catalyst: Finely divided Iron (Fe) + Promoter: Molybdenum (Mo).
  • • Ostwald Process: Conversion of Ammonia into Nitric Acid (HNO₃). Catalyst: Platinum (Pt) gauze.
  • • Contact Process: Manufacture of Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄, King of Chemicals) (used in fertilizers & lead-acid batteries). Catalyst: Vanadium Pentoxide (V₂O₅).
  • • Solvay Process: Industrial manufacture of Sodium Carbonate (Washing Soda, Na₂CO₃) using NaCl, NH₃, and CaCO₃.
  • • Hall–Héroult / Electrolytic Reduction: Extraction of Aluminium (Al) from molten Bauxite (Al₂O₃·2H₂O) dissolved in Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆). Al₂O₃ cannot be reduced by carbon because Aluminium has a greater affinity for oxygen than carbon.
  • • Deacon Process: Manufacture of Chlorine (Cl₂) using Cupric chloride (CuCl₂) catalyst.
  • • Hydrogenation of Oils: Vegetable oils → Vanaspati ghee using Nickel (Ni) catalyst.

Elements (Pure Substance)

Contain only one kind of atom; cannot be broken down into simpler substances by physical or chemical methods.
• Monoatomic: Cu, Fe, He, Ne, Ar
• Diatomic Molecules (Asked 3x): O₂, H₂, N₂, Cl₂, F₂, Br₂, I₂ (homoatomic molecules — still elements, not compounds!).
• Liquid Metal at Room Temp: Mercury (Hg) (mp −39°C); Ga & Cs melt slightly above 30°C.
• Liquid Non-Metal: Bromine (Br₂).
• Oxygen Discovery: Joseph Priestley (1774).

Compounds (Pure Substance)

Two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed mass ratio (e.g., H₂O, NH₃, CO₂, NaCl, MgO).
• Properties are entirely different from constituent elements because a new substance is formed.
• Have sharp melting/boiling points.
• Cannot be separated by physical methods (filtration, distillation, evaporation); can only be separated into elements by chemical reactions / electrolysis.

Mixtures & Separation Methods

Variable composition; retain constituent properties; separated by physical methods:
• Magnetic Separation: Iron filings + Sulfur powder.
• Fractional Distillation: Refining crude petroleum into petrol, diesel, kerosene (small boiling point difference).
• Sublimation: Camphor, Naphthalene, Iodine, NH₄Cl.
• Centrifugation: Cream from milk.
• Chromatography: Pigments/dyes & drugs from blood.
• Reverse Osmosis (RO): Desalination of sea water.

1.4 Atomic Structure, Periodic Table Trends & Chemical Bonding

Arihant Magbook Ch. 2 & 3 (pp. 59–69) | 12 Questions Asked in Haryana Shifts

1 Q Every Shift
Subatomic Particles & Models

• Electron (e⁻): Discovered by J.J. Thomson (cathode rays); established that atoms contain negatively charged particles. Charge measured by Millikan's oil drop (-1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C).

• Nucleus & Proton (p⁺): Ernest Rutherford's α-particle gold-foil scattering experiment proved that most of the atom is empty space and all positive charge + mass is concentrated in a tiny central nucleus (10⁻¹⁵ m).

• Neutron (n⁰): Discovered by James Chadwick (neutral). Protium (¹₁H) is the only isotope with zero neutrons.

• Isotopes: Same Z, different A (e.g., ¹²C, ¹⁴C). Isobars: Same A, different Z (e.g., ⁴⁰Ar, ⁴⁰K, ⁴⁰Ca).

Shell Configuration & Bonding

• Bohr–Bury Rule: Max electrons in shell = 2n² (K=2, L=8, M=18, N=32); outermost octet max = 8.

• Sodium (Z=11 → 2, 8, 1): Period 3, Group 1 (Alkali metal); loses 1e⁻ to form Na⁺ (+1) ion; highest metallic character in Period 3.

• Magnesium (Z=12 → 2, 8, 2): Period 3, Group 2; loses 2e⁻ to form Mg²⁺ (+2) ion.

• Chlorine (Z=17 → 2, 8, 7): Period 3, Group 17 (Halogen); gains 1e⁻ to form Cl⁻ (-1) ion.

• Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond: Electron transfer between metal & non-metal (NaCl, MgO, CaCl₂); high MP/BP; conducts electricity when dissolved/molten due to free mobile ions.

• Covalent Bond: Sharing of electron pairs between non-metals (O₂, H₂O, CH₄, CO₂, Glucose); does not conduct electricity.

Modern Periodic Table Trends (Moseley, Z)

7 Periods (rows) & 18 Groups (columns):

• Across a Period (Left → Right):
— Increases: Electronegativity, Ionization Enthalpy, Electron Gain Enthalpy, Non-metallic character, Acidic nature of oxides.
— Decreases: Atomic radius, Metallic character, Basic nature of oxides.

• Down a Group (Top → Bottom):
— Increases: Atomic radius (new electron shells added), Metallic character, Basic nature of oxides.
— Decreases: Electronegativity, Ionization Enthalpy.

• Radioisotopes (Arihant p. 63): C-14 (Carbon dating), U-238/Pb-206 (Rock dating), Co-60 (Cancer therapy), I-131 (Thyroid), Na-24 (Blood clots), P-32 (Leukemia).

2.1 Cell Biology, Organelles, Cell Division & Biomolecules

Arihant Magbook Biology Ch. 1 (pp. 110–116) | Core Foundation & Probable Conceptual Stems

High-Probability Concept Area
Founders & Key Branches (p. 110–111)

• Father of Biology / Zoology / Embryology: Aristotle (Book: Historia Animalium). Term 'Biology' coined by Lamarck & Treviranus (1802).

• Father of Botany: Theophrastus (Book: Historia Plantarum). Father of Medicine: Hippocrates.

• High-Yield Branches: Mycology (Fungi), Phycology (Algae), Pomology (Fruits), Anthology (Flowers), Dendrochronology (Age of trees via annual growth rings), Pedology (Soil formation), Entomology (Insects), Ornithology (Birds), Ichthyology (Fishes), Herpetology (Reptiles/Amphibians), Ophiology (Snakes), Osteology (Bones), Odontology (Teeth), Haematology (Blood), Gerontology (Ageing).

Cell Discovery & Cell Theory (p. 112)

• Discovery: Robert Hooke (1665) observed honeycomb-like dead cork chambers. Protoplasm (cytoplasm + nucleoplasm) is the physical basis of life; non-living inclusions are called deutoplasm.

• Cell Theory: Formulated by M. Schleiden (German Botanist) & T. Schwann (British Zoologist). Expanded by Rudolf Virchow: "Omnis cellula e cellula" (All cells arise from pre-existing cells).

• Exception to Cell Theory: Viruses (lack cellular machinery and energy-generating enzymes; obligate parasites).

• Plant vs. Animal Cell Exclusives:
— Plant Cell Only: Rigid Cell Wall (cellulose), Chloroplasts (plastids), Large Central Vacuole, Plasmodesmata.
— Animal Cell Only: Centrioles / Centrosome (forms spindle fibres during cell division).

Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells (p. 113)

• Prokaryotic Cells (Bacteria, Cyanobacteria/BGA, Mycoplasma):
— Primitive nucleus (nucleoid) without nuclear membrane.
— Membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes) are absent.
— Ribosomes are 70S type only.
— Respiratory enzymes associated with plasma membrane (mesosomes).
— Both transcription and translation occur in cytoplasm.

• Eukaryotic Cells (Protists, Fungi, Plants, Animals):
— True nucleus with double nuclear membrane + histone proteins.
— Membrane-bound organelles present; 80S (cytoplasm) & 70S (mitochondria/plastids) ribosomes.
— Transcription in nucleus; translation in cytoplasm.

Cell Organelles — Structure, Functions & Exam Nicknames (Arihant pp. 112–116)

Organelle Membrane & Structure Key Physiological Function & Exam Clues
Plasma Membrane Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (Selectively permeable) Regulates transport of ions and nutrients into and out of the cell via diffusion, osmosis, and active transport. Present in both plant & animal cells.
Mitochondria Double-membraned; inner membrane folded into cristae (contains own circular DNA & 70S ribosomes) "Powerhouse of the Cell" — site of aerobic cellular respiration (Krebs cycle & Electron Transport Chain) producing energy currency ATP (1 ATP ≈ 7.6 kcal / 31.8 kJ). Nucleus & Nucleolus Largest organelle; double nuclear membrane with pores; contains DNA + basic Histone proteins "Brain/Director of the Cell" — controls heredity & metabolism. Nucleolus (discovered by Fontana, 1781) is the site of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis.

Cell Cycle (G₁ → S → G₂ → M) & Mitosis vs. Meiosis (pp. 113–114)

• Cell Cycle Phases: G₁ (Gap 1): Cell growth (least DNA). S (Synthesis Phase): DNA replication occurs (DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C). G₂ (Gap 2): Preparation for mitosis (maximum DNA in interphase). G₀ Phase: Quiescent stage for non-dividing cells (e.g., mature neurons). M-Phase: Karyokinesis (nuclear division: Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase) + Cytokinesis (cytoplasmic division).

• Mitosis (Equational Division):
— Occurs in somatic (body) cells for growth and tissue repair.
— 1 division produces 2 genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells.
— No homologous pairing or crossing-over; centromeres split during Anaphase.

• Meiosis (Reductional Division):
— Occurs in germ/gamete-producing cells to form haploid sperms and ova.
— 2 divisions produce 4 genetically distinct haploid (n) cells.
— Homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis) and exchange DNA (crossing-over) during Prophase-I; centromeres split only in Anaphase-II.

Biomolecules: Carbohydrates, Proteins, Lipids & Enzymes (pp. 114–115)

• Carbohydrates (CH₂O)ₙ: Monomers = Monosaccharides (Glucose, Fructose, Galactose); Disaccharides = Sucrose (glucose + fructose), Lactose (milk sugar: glucose + galactose), Maltose (glucose + glucose); Polysaccharides = Starch (plant storage), Glycogen (animal liver/muscle storage), Cellulose (plant cell wall).

• Proteins (Polymers of Amino Acids): Make up ~50% of cell dry weight. Deficiency causes Protein Energy Malnutrition (PEM): Kwashiorkor & Marasmus.
— Key Functional Proteins: Collagen (connective tissue strength), Keratin (hair/nails/feathers/horns), Actin & Myosin (muscle contraction), Haemoglobin (O₂ transport), Antibodies / Immunoglobulins (immune defense), Insulin (blood sugar signalling).

• Lipids & Fats: Hydrophobic esters of fatty acids + glycerol. Unsaturated fats (double bonds, liquid at room temp) are healthy; Trans fats (hydrogenated oils) damage cardiovascular health. Steroids include Cholesterol, Oestrogen, Testosterone.

• Nucleic Acids (Polymers of Nucleotides = Nitrogenous Base + Pentose Sugar + Phosphate): DNA (B-DNA is major human form; Z-DNA is zig-zag helix) & RNA (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA; catalytic RNAs are ribozymes).

• Enzymes (Biological Catalysts — Discovered by Buchner in yeast, 1900): All enzymes are proteins (except ribozymes). Holoenzyme = Apoenzyme (protein part) + Cofactor. Optimum activity at 35–40°C and pH 7–8 (except gastric Pepsin at pH ~2).

2.2 Biological Classification, Microorganisms, Vectors & Human Diseases

Taxonomy & Whittaker's 5 Kingdoms (pp. 117–118)

• Binomial Nomenclature: Carolus Linnaeus (Book: Species Plantarum, 1758). Hierarchy (Ascending): Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum → Kingdom.

• R.H. Whittaker (1969) 5-Kingdom System:
1. Monera: Prokaryotic unicellular (Bacteria, Cyanobacteria, Mycoplasma); non-cellulosic polysaccharide/peptidoglycan wall.
2. Protista: Eukaryotic unicellular (Diatoms, Euglena, Protozoa like Amoeba, Plasmodium).
3. Fungi: Eukaryotic heterotrophs (saprophytic/parasitic); cell wall made of Chitin; body of hyphae/mycelium.
4. Plantae: Eukaryotic autotrophs; cell wall of Cellulose.
5. Animalia: Eukaryotic holozoic heterotrophs; no cell wall.

Monera, Fungi, Lichens & Mycorrhiza (pp. 119–122)

• Mycoplasma (PPLO — "Joker of Microbiology"): Discovered by Nocard & Roux; smallest living cells, lack cell wall, pleomorphic (change shape), unaffected by penicillin.

• Plasmid: Extra-chromosomal circular DNA in bacteria used as a cloning vector in biotechnology.

• Aflatoxins: Potent carcinogenic mycotoxins produced by Fungi/Mould (Aspergillus flavus/fumigatus) in spoiled grains/oilseeds.

• Lichens (Algae + Fungi Mutualism): Algae prepares food; fungus absorbs water/minerals. Act as bio-indicators of SO₂ air pollution (do not grow in polluted areas).

• Mycorrhiza (Fungi + Roots of Higher Plants, e.g., Pinus): Helps plants absorb phosphorus/water, resist drought, and tolerate extreme pH.

Plant & Animal Kingdom Highlights (pp. 122–124)

• Algae (Thallophyta): Chlorella & Spirulina (astronaut food/O₂); Gelidium & Gracilaria (yield Agar-agar); Laminaria (brown algae yields Iodine); Nostoc & Anabaena (N₂-fixing biofertilisers).

• Bryophytes ("Amphibians of Plant Kingdom"): Need water for fertilization; lack true vascular tissues (e.g., Sphagnum moss — forms peat).

• Pteridophytes: First terrestrial plants with vascular tissues (xylem & phloem); spore-bearing (ferns).

• Gymnosperms: Naked seeds (ovules not enclosed in ovary), e.g., Cycas (coralloid roots), Pinus, Sequoia (tallest redwood).

• Vertebrate Traps (IAS/Magbook): Sea cow & Sea lion are Mammals, whereas Sea horse is a true bony Fish (Pisces)! Crocodile is a reptile with a 4-chambered heart.

Photosynthesis & Respiration (pp. 144–145)

• Primary Site (Asked 24/08): Leaves (Chloroplasts containing green pigments Chlorophyll-a & b with central Mg²⁺ ion). Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) = 400–700 nm.

• Equation (Endothermic):
6CO₂ + 12H₂O —(Sunlight/Chlorophyll)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂↑ + 6H₂O

• 3 Core Events (Asked 02/09):
1. Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
2. Conversion of radiant light energy into chemical energy (ATP & NADPH) + photolysis of water (releases O₂ byproduct).
3. Reduction (fixation) of CO₂ into carbohydrates (glucose).
Exam Trap: Oxidation of carbon to CO₂ does NOT occur in photosynthesis (that occurs in respiration!).

• Day vs. Night (Asked 31/08): At night, photosynthesis stops completely (no sunlight), whereas cellular respiration continues day and night in mitochondria.

Stomata, Guard Cells & Transpiration (p. 130, 145)

• Stomata (Asked 24/08 & 02/09): Microscopic pores in leaf epidermis that carry out gaseous exchange (CO₂ & O₂) with the atmosphere and evaporative water loss.

• Guard Cells (Asked 24/08 & 31/08): Specialized bean/kidney-shaped epidermal cells containing chloroplasts that control the opening and closing of stomatal pores via turgor pressure changes driven by Potassium (K⁺) ions.

• Transpiration Pull (Asked 04/09): Evaporative loss of water vapour from leaves (demonstrated by water droplets condensing inside a plastic bag tied around sunlit leaves). Creates a negative transpirational suction pull that lifts water and minerals upward from roots to tall treetops.

• Xerophytic Adaptations: Desert plants (Cactus/Opuntia) have thick waxy cuticle and leaves modified into spines/thorns (stem modified into green photosynthetic phylloclade) to minimize transpiration.

Plant Tissues: Xylem vs. Phloem (pp. 129–130)

• Meristematic Tissue: Actively dividing cells at root/shoot tips (Apical), internodes (Intercalary), and cambium (Lateral/Secondary growth — increases girth and forms annual growth rings).

• Simple Permanent Tissues:
— Parenchyma: Thin-walled living cells (storage & photosynthesis).
— Collenchyma: Living cells with corner thickenings (flexibility & mechanical support to young stems).
— Sclerenchyma: Dead, thick lignified walls (rigidity & hard mechanical support, e.g., coconut husk).

• Complex Permanent (Vascular) Tissues:
— Xylem (Asked 31/08): Conducts water and dissolved minerals unidirectionally from roots to leaves. Composed of Tracheids, Vessels, Xylem parenchyma, and Xylem fibres. Forms major part of wood (Central dark durable Heartwood vs. outer light Sapwood).
— Phloem: Translocates prepared food (sucrose) bidirectionally from leaves to roots/fruits. Composed of Sieve tubes, Companion cells, Phloem parenchyma, and Phloem fibres.

B. Human Respiratory System (Arihant pp. 135–136) 3 Haryana PYQs

• Air Conducting Pathway: Nostrils → Nasal cavity → Pharynx → Larynx (Voice box) → Trachea (Windpipe) → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli (Air sacs) → Blood capillaries → Tissues.

• Mechanism of Breathing (Diaphragm & Intercostal Muscles):
— Inspiration (Inhaling): Dome-shaped diaphragm contracts & flattens; rib cage moves outward and upward → thoracic volume increases → air rushes in.
— Expiration (Exhaling): Diaphragm relaxes (dome-shaped); rib cage moves inward and downward → thoracic cavity reduces.

• Lung Volumes (Asked 02/09):
— Tidal Volume (TV): Volume of air inhaled or exhaled during a normal effortless breath (~500 mL).
— Residual Volume (RV): Air remaining in lungs even after forcible expiration (~1100–1200 mL).
— Vital Capacity (VC): Maximum air exhaled after maximum inhalation (TV + IRV + ERV ≈ 4000–4600 mL).

• Gas Transport & Respiratory Quotient (RQ):
— Oxygen (O₂): 98.5% transported by iron-containing RBC pigment Haemoglobin (1 Hb molecule carries up to 4 O₂ molecules as oxyhaemoglobin).
— Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): 70% transported as Bicarbonates (HCO₃⁻), 23% as carbaminohaemoglobin, 1–7% dissolved in plasma.
— Respiratory Quotient (RQ = Vol of CO₂ released / Vol of O₂ absorbed): Measured by Ganong's respirometer. Carbohydrates = 1.0; Proteins = 0.80; Fats = 0.70.

C. Blood & Circulatory System (Arihant pp. 131, 136–137) 5 Haryana PYQs

• Human Heart (4-Chambered, covered by Pericardium, 280–340 g):
— Pulmonary Vein (Asked 01/09): The only vein that carries oxygenated blood — brings oxygen-rich blood from the lungs into the Left Atrium.
— Left Ventricle (Asked 04/09): Thickest-walled chamber; pumps oxygen-rich blood into the Aorta for distribution to the entire body.
— Right Atrium: Receives deoxygenated blood from the body via Superior & Inferior Vena Cava.
— Right Ventricle & Pulmonary Artery: Right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood into the Pulmonary Artery (the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood) to the lungs.

• Arteries vs. Veins (Asked Twice on 07/09):
— Arteries: Thick, elastic, muscular vessels that carry blood AWAY from the heart under high systolic pressure (no valves).
— Veins: Thin-walled vessels with valves that carry blood towards the heart under low diastolic pressure.

• Blood Pressure, ECG & ABO Blood Groups (Carl Landsteiner):
— Normal BP: 120/80 mmHg (120 = Systolic contraction; 80 = Diastolic relaxation; cardiac cycle = 0.8 s).
— ECG Waves: P-wave (Atrial depolarisation), QRS complex (Ventricular depolarisation), T-wave (Ventricular repolarisation).
— Blood Group O: Universal Donor (No A/B antigens on RBCs; both anti-a & anti-b antibodies in plasma).
— Blood Group AB: Universal Recipient (Both A & B antigens on RBCs; no antibodies in plasma).
— Cardiovascular Disorders: Atherosclerosis (cholesterol deposition narrowing arteries); Arteriosclerosis (hardening/calcification of arteries); Angina pectoris (chest pain due to insufficient O₂ to heart muscle).

• Human Excretory System (Ureotelic — excretes Urea):
— Kidney (Asked 02/09): Removes nitrogenous wastes (urea) from blood while regulating the body's water and electrolyte balance (critical in dehydrated athletes).

Gravitation, Lift Weight & Satellites (Arihant pp. 9–11)

• Universal Law of Gravitation: F = Gm₁m₂ / r² (G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²). Weakest fundamental force in nature (Strong Nuclear > Electromagnetic > Weak Nuclear > Gravitational).

• Variation of g = GM/R² = 9.8 m/s²:
— Shape of Earth: Equatorial radius is ~42 km greater than polar radius → g is MAXIMUM at Poles and MINIMUM at Equator.
— Earth's Rotation (g' = g − Rω² cos²λ): No effect at poles (λ = 90°); maximum reduction at equator (λ = 0°).
— Altitude & Depth: g decreases with both altitude (h) and depth (d), and becomes zero at the centre of the Earth.
— On the Moon: Mass remains constant everywhere, while weight becomes w_moon = w_earth / 6.

• Apparent Weight of a Person in a Lift (p. 10):
1. Lift at rest or uniform velocity: w' = mg (No change).
2. Lift accelerating upward: w' = m(g + a) (Apparent weight increases).
3. Lift accelerating downward: w' = m(g − a) (Apparent weight decreases).
4. Lift cable breaks (free fall, a = g): w' = 0 (Weightlessness).

• Kepler's Laws, Satellites & Escape Velocity (p. 11):
— Kepler's 3 Laws: (1) Elliptical orbits with Sun at one focus; (2) Areal speed is constant (conserves angular momentum); (3) T² ∝ a³.
— Geostationary Satellite: Equatorial orbit at height ~36,000 km, time period T = 24 hours; used for live TV telecast & telecommunications.
— Polar Satellite: Low polar orbit at height ~800 km, time period T ≈ 84 min; used for weather monitoring & mapping.
— Escape Velocity (v_e = √(2gR) = √2 × v_orbital): 11.2 km/s on Earth (v_o ≈ 7.9 km/s; if orbital speed increases by √2 or 41%, the satellite escapes orbit).

Elasticity & Hooke's Law (pp. 13–14)

• Elasticity: Property of a body to regain its original shape after removing deforming force. Quartz and phosphor bronze are almost perfectly elastic.

• Hooke's Law: Within elastic limit, Stress ∝ Strain ⇒ Stress / Strain = Modulus of Elasticity (E) (Unit: N/m² or Pascal, Pa; Strain is unitless).
— Young's Modulus (Y): Longitudinal stress / strain.
— Bulk Modulus (K): Volumetric stress / strain.
— Modulus of Rigidity (η): Shearing stress / strain.

• Classic Exam Facts:
— Steel is MORE elastic than rubber (Y_steel > Y_rubber); hence springs are made of steel, not copper.
— Hollow shaft is stronger than a solid shaft of the same mass.
— Girders are I-shaped to prevent buckling.
— Bridges are declared unsafe after long use due to elastic fatigue.

Pressure, Pascal's Law & Floatation (pp. 14–15)

• Fluid Pressure (p = F/A = hρg): SI unit = Pascal (Pa = N/m²). Atmospheric pressure at sea level = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa ≈ 1 bar (1 torr = 1 mm Hg), measured by Barometer.

• Pascal's Law: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions. Basis of Hydraulic lift, Hydraulic press, and Hydraulic brakes.

• Archimedes' Principle & Floatation:
— Apparent loss in weight of an immersed body equals the weight of liquid displaced (Buoyant force / Upthrust).
— Hydrometer / Lactometer: Measures relative density of liquids / purity of milk based on the Law of Floatation.
— Floating Iceberg: Density of ice (0.92 g/cm³) < water (1.0 g/cm³ at 4°C maximum density). In pure water, 11/12th part is submerged and 1/12th is above water (in sea water, 8/9th submerged, 1/9th outside). When floating ice melts in a glass, the water level remains unchanged.
— Swimming in Sea vs. River: Easier in sea water due to its higher density and greater buoyant upthrust.

Surface Tension, Capillarity & Bernoulli (pp. 15–17)

• Surface Tension (S = F/l; Unit: N/m): Liquid minimizes free surface area → Raindrops and mercury droplets are spherical.
— Decreases with increase in temperature (becomes zero at critical temperature) and when soap, detergent, oil, kerosene, or Dettol is added:
1. Soap/detergent lowers surface tension of water so it penetrates fibres and cleans greasy stains better.
2. Pouring kerosene oil on stagnant water lowers surface tension so mosquito larvae sink and drown.
3. Hot soup tastes better than cold soup because lower surface tension lets it spread over the tongue.

• Capillarity (h = 2S cosθ / rρg): Rising/falling of liquid in narrow bore tubes. Examples: Water rising in plant xylem/soil capillaries, blotting paper sucking ink, oil rising in a lamp wick, towel soaking water. (Water rises; mercury falls due to strong cohesive force).

• Viscosity & Terminal Velocity: Internal fluid friction (Unit: Pa·s or Poise = 0.1 Pa·s). Viscosity of liquids decreases with temperature, whereas viscosity of gases increases with temperature.

• Bernoulli's Theorem (p + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant): Conservation of energy in streamlined flow — where fluid velocity is high, pressure is low. Explains: lifting of tin roofs during cyclones/storms, aeroplane wing lift, atomizer/sprayer, Venturimeter, and Magnus effect (curving of a spinning cricket ball).

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) & Simple Pendulum (pp. 19–20)

Mechanical vs. Electromagnetic & Transverse vs. Longitudinal Waves (p. 21)

• Wave Speed Formula (Asked 01/09): v = f × λ (where f = 1/T is frequency in Hertz and λ is wavelength).
— Haryana Shift Rule: If wave speed v remains constant and frequency (f) is doubled, its wavelength (λ) becomes HALF.
— When a wave travels from one medium to another, speed (v) and wavelength (λ) change, but frequency (f) remains constant.

• Longitudinal vs. Transverse Waves:
— Longitudinal Waves (Sound waves): Particles vibrate parallel to wave propagation in compressions and rarefactions; can travel through solids, liquids, and gases (cannot be polarised).
— Transverse Waves (Light / EM waves, waves on a string): Particles vibrate perpendicular to propagation in crests and troughs; can be polarised.

Sound Waves, Speed, Echo & SONAR (Arihant pp. 21–23)

• Frequency Ranges of Sound:
— Infrasonic Waves: < 20 Hz (earthquakes, whales, elephants).
— Audible Sound Waves: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz) (human ear range).
— Ultrasonic Waves: > 20,000 Hz (20 kHz) (bats, dolphins, dogs; used in SONAR ~50,000 Hz, ultrasonography, cleaning, flaw detection).

• Speed of Sound in Different Media (v_solid > v_liquid > v_gas):
— Cannot travel in vacuum (hence astronauts on the Moon cannot hear each other directly).
— Air (0°C): 332 m/s (~340 m/s at room temp) | Water: 1483 m/s | Iron (Solid): 5130 m/s.
— Factors Affecting Speed in Air: Increases with temperature (+0.61 m/s per 1°C rise; v ∝ √T); faster in humid (moist) air than dry air; independent of pressure at constant temperature.
— Lightning vs. Thunder: Light (3 × 10⁸ m/s) travels vastly faster than sound (340 m/s), so lightning is seen before thunder is heard.

• Echo Calculation & Reverberation (Asked 25/08):
— Echo Formula: Total Distance = 2d = v × t ⇒ t = 2d / v
— Haryana PYQ Numerical (25/08): Student stands d = 170 m from a wall, speed of sound v = 340 m/s → t = (2 × 170) / 340 = 1.00 s.
— Minimum Distance for Distinct Echo: Persistence of hearing of human ear is 0.1 s (1/10 s) → d_min = (340 × 0.1)/2 = 17 m (~16.6 m).
— Reverberation: Persistence of sound in a hall due to repeated multiple reflections.

• Characteristics of Sound, Mach Number & Doppler Effect:
— Loudness / Intensity: Proportional to square of Amplitude (I ∝ A²); sound level measured in decibels (dB): β = 10 log₁₀(I / I₀).
— Pitch (Shrillness vs. Grave note): Depends directly on Frequency (f).
— Mach Number: Speed of body / Speed of sound (Supersonic if Mach > 1; produces shock waves).
— Doppler's Effect: Apparent change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer (used in police speed radar, airport tracking, echocardiography, and measuring star/galaxy velocities).

Temperature Scales & Expansion (pp. 25–26)

Specific Heat, Latent Heat & Humidity (pp. 26–27)

• Specific Heat (Q = msΔθ):
— Water has the highest specific heat capacity (4200 J/kg°C = 1 cal/g°C); hence water is used as a coolant in car radiators and in hot-water fermentation bags.

• Latent Heat (Q = mL — Heat absorbed/released at CONSTANT temperature during phase change):
— Latent Heat of Fusion of Ice: 80 cal/g (336 kJ/kg).
— Latent Heat of Vaporisation of Steam: 536 cal/g (2260 kJ/kg).
— Exam Application: Steam at 100°C causes much more severe burns than boiling water at 100°C because steam releases an extra 536 cal/g of latent heat of vaporisation.

• Evaporation & Humidity:
— Evaporation causes cooling (e.g., sweating, earthen pitcher matka cooling water, refrigerator volatile liquid coil).
— Relative Humidity is measured by a Hygrometer (comfortable at ~50% at 22–25°C). High humidity slows sweat evaporation, making weather feel sultry.

• Laws of Thermodynamics:
— Zeroth Law: Defines Thermal Equilibrium & Temperature.
— First Law (ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW): Conservation of Energy.
— Second Law: Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a colder to a hotter body (Clausius — basis of refrigerator); 100% efficient heat engine is impossible (Kelvin).

Conduction, Convection & Radiation (pp. 27–28)

• 1. Conduction (Solids & Mercury): Heat transfer without actual movement of particles.
— Metals are good conductors; wood, wool, glass, thermocol, and dry air are bad conductors (insulators).
— Two thin blankets are warmer than one thick blanket of the same total thickness because the trapped layer of air between them acts as an insulator.

• 2. Convection (Liquids & Gases): Heat transfer by actual movement of heated fluid particles (convection currents).
— Explains Sea Breeze (daytime: sea to land) and Land Breeze (night: land to sea).
— Refrigerator freezer box is fitted near the top so dense cold air sinks and cools the entire interior by convection.

• 3. Radiation (Electromagnetic Waves — No Medium Required):
— Heat from the Sun reaches Earth via radiation.
— Cloudy nights are warmer than clear nights because clouds reflect terrestrial infrared radiation back to Earth.
— Light/White clothes in summer (good reflectors, bad absorbers); Dark/Black clothes in winter (good absorbers).
— Kirchhoff's Law: Good absorbers are good emitters.
— Stefan's Law: E ∝ T⁴. Wien's Law: λ_m ∝ 1/T (as a black body heats up, colour shifts from Red → Orange → Yellow → Blue/Violet).

Total Internal Reflection (TIR) Asked 24/08 Complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when light travels from denser to rarer medium at angle of incidence greater than Critical Angle (i > C), where μ = 1 / sin C. • Optical Fibers (Asked 24/08): Transmit light signals over long distances with minimal loss (high-μ inner core surrounded by low-μ cladding); used in telecommunications and medical endoscopy (stomach examination).

• Danger Signals are Red: Red has the longest wavelength and scatters least in fog/smoke.
• Clouds Appear White: Large water droplets scatter all wavelengths equally.

• Diffraction: Bending of waves around obstacle edges.
• Polarisation: Restricting transverse electric field vectors to a single plane. 		• Polarisation proves light waves are TRANSVERSE (Sound waves cannot be polarised because they are longitudinal).

Human Eye, Vision Defects & Colour Mixing (pp. 34–36)

• Human Eye Anatomy: Convex eye lens forms a real and inverted image on the Retina. Iris controls pupil size & light intensity; Ciliary muscles adjust focal length (Power of Accommodation).
— Least Distance of Distinct Vision (Near Point, D): 25 cm for a normal healthy eye; Far Point: Infinity (∞).

• Defects of Vision & Corrective Lenses (Arihant p. 35):
1. Myopia (Short-Sightedness): Can see nearby objects clearly, cannot see distant objects (far point shifts closer; image forms in front of retina). Corrected by a Concave Lens of suitable power.
2. Hypermetropia (Long-Sightedness): Can see distant objects clearly, cannot see nearby objects (near point moves beyond 25 cm; image forms behind retina). Corrected by a Convex Lens.
3. Presbyopia: Age-related loss of ciliary muscle accommodation → Bifocal lens.
4. Astigmatism: Cannot focus horizontal & vertical lines simultaneously due to uneven corneal curvature → Corrected by Cylindrical Lens.
5. Cataract: Opaque white membrane over cornea/lens → Corrected by surgery.

• Primary, Secondary & Complementary Colours of Light (p. 34):
— Primary Colours of Light: Red, Green, and Blue (RGB) (used in Colour TV cameras via dichromic mirrors).
— Secondary Colours: Red + Green = Yellow; Red + Blue = Magenta; Green + Blue = Cyan.
— Complementary Pairs (produce White): Red + Cyan = White; Green + Magenta = White; Blue + Yellow = White.
— Rose in Green Light Trap: A red rose reflects only red light; when viewed in green light, it absorbs the green light and appears BLACK!

3.6 Electricity, Ohm's Law, Heating Effect, Magnetism & Electromagnetic Induction

Charge, Potential, Ohm's Law & Resistance (pp. 38–40)

• Electric Charge (q = ne; Unit: Coulomb, C): e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Coulomb's Law: F = (1/4πε₀) q₁q₂ / r². Inside a closed hollow metallic conductor, electric field is zero (E = 0) and charge resides only on the outer surface (car acts as Faraday cage during lightning).

• Potential Difference (V = W/q; Volt = J/C) & Capacitance (C = q/V; Farad / μF).

• Electric Current (I = q/t; Unit: Ampere; Scalar) & Ohm's Law (V = IR):
— Resistance (R = ρ l / A; Unit: Ohm, Ω): Directly proportional to length (l) and inversely proportional to cross-sectional area (A).
— Resistivity (ρ; Unit: Ω·m): Depends only on nature of material and temperature (independent of length/area!).
— Effect of Temperature: Resistance of metals increases with temperature (+α), whereas resistance of semiconductors (Si, Ge) and electrolytes DECREASES with temperature (−α).

• Series vs. Parallel Resistors:
— Series (R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃): Same current flows; used for Fuse wire & Ammeter.
— Parallel (1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃): Same voltage (220 V) across all appliances; used in domestic household wiring & Voltmeter.

Heating Effect, Fuse, Cells & Meters (pp. 40–42)

• Joule's Law of Heating: H = I²Rt = VIt = (V²/R)t.
— Heating Element (Heater, Iron, Geyser): Made of Nichrome alloy (Ni + Cr + Fe) because it has high resistivity and high melting point (does not oxidize easily).
— Bulb Filament: Made of Tungsten (W) (high melting point ~3422°C).
— Electric Fuse Wire: Safety device always connected in series with live wire; made of Tin–Lead alloy (63% Sn + 37% Pb) characterized by HIGH resistance and LOW melting point.

• Kirchhoff's Laws:
— 1st Law (Junction / Current Law): Conservation of Electric Charge.
— 2nd Law (Loop / Voltage Law): Conservation of Energy.

• Ammeter vs. Voltmeter (Arihant p. 42):
— Ammeter (Measures Current): Always connected in SERIES; ideal ammeter has ZERO resistance. Galvanometer → Ammeter by connecting a low-resistance Shunt in parallel.
— Voltmeter (Measures Potential Difference): Always connected in PARALLEL; ideal voltmeter has INFINITE (∞) resistance. Galvanometer → Voltmeter by connecting a high resistance in series.

Magnetism, EMI, AC/DC & Transformer (pp. 41–44)

• Electromagnets vs. Permanent Magnets:
— Temporary / Electromagnets: Made of Soft Iron (easily magnetised and demagnetised; used in electric bells, cranes, transformers).
— Permanent Magnets: Made of Steel / Alnico.
— Magnetic Materials: Diamagnetic (repelled: Au, Ag, Cu, Zn, H₂O, Bi); Paramagnetic (feebly attracted: Al, Pt, Cr, Mn, O₂); Ferromagnetic (strongly attracted: Fe, Co, Ni; lose ferromagnetism above Curie Temperature: 770°C for Fe, 358°C for Ni).

• Electromagnetic Induction (Faraday & Lenz):
— Induced EMF E = −N dφ/dt. Lenz's Law obeys Conservation of Energy.
— Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Force on current-carrying conductor (Electric Motor: Electrical → Mechanical).
— Fleming's Right-Hand Rule: Induced current direction (AC Generator / Dynamo: Mechanical → Electrical).

• Alternating Current (AC) & Transformer:
— Frequency of domestic AC in India = 50 Hz (220 V RMS) (I_rms = I₀ / √2 = 0.707 I₀). Rectifier converts AC → DC; Inverter converts DC → AC.
— Transformer: Works on Mutual Induction (works only on AC, never on DC!).
• Step-Up (N_s > N_p): Low V → High V.
• Step-Down (N_s < N_p): High V → Low V.

Radioactivity, Nuclear Fission, Reactor & Fusion (pp. 46–48)

• Radioactivity: Discovered by Henri Becquerel (1896); SI unit of activity = Becquerel (Bq) (1 Curie = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ decays/s).
— Alpha (α) Decay (₂He⁴ nucleus): Atomic number Z decreases by 2; Mass number A decreases by 4.
— Beta (β⁻) Decay (₋₁e⁰ electron): Z increases by 1; A remains unchanged.
— Gamma (γ) Decay (EM photon): Highest penetrating power; Z and A remain unchanged.
— Half-Life (T_½ = 0.693 / λ): Remaining undecayed atoms after n half-lives: N = N₀ (½)ⁿ.

• Nuclear Fission vs. Nuclear Fusion (ΔE = Δmc²; 1 amu = 931 MeV):
— Nuclear Fission: Splitting of a heavy nucleus (₉₂U²³⁵) bombarded by a slow thermal neutron into ₅₆Ba¹⁴¹ + ₃₆Kr⁹² + 3 ₀n¹ + Energy. Basis of Atom Bomb (uncontrolled chain reaction) and Nuclear Reactor (controlled chain reaction).
— Nuclear Reactor Components (p. 47):
1. Fuel: U-235, U-233, Pu-239.
2. Moderator (Slows down fast neutrons): Heavy Water (D₂O), Graphite, or Beryllium oxide.
3. Control Rods (Absorb excess neutrons to control/stop chain reaction): Cadmium (Cd) or Boron (B) rods.
4. Coolant: Cold water, liquid sodium, heavy water.
— Nuclear Fusion: Combining two light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) into a heavier helium nucleus at extreme temperature (10⁷ K). Source of energy of the Sun and Stars and basis of the Hydrogen Bomb.

Photoelectric Effect, Semiconductors & Universe (pp. 48–52)

• Photoelectric Effect (Einstein's Nobel Prize — Quantum Nature of Light):
— Emission of photoelectrons from a metal surface when light of frequency above threshold frequency (ν > ν₀) falls on it: E_k = ½mv² = hν − φ = h(ν − ν₀).
— Rate of emission (photocurrent) depends on intensity of light, whereas maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends ONLY on frequency (ν), not intensity!

• Semiconductors & Electronics (pp. 50–51):
— Intrinsic Semiconductors: Pure Silicon (Si, barrier potential 0.7 V) and Germanium (Ge, 0.3 V).
— p-type Semiconductor (Trivalent doping: B, Al, Ga, In): Majority carriers = Holes (+).
— n-type Semiconductor (Pentavalent doping: P, As, Sb): Majority carriers = Electrons (−).
— Diodes: LED (GaAsP — converts electrical energy to light); Zener Diode (works in reverse breakdown as a Voltage Regulator); Photodiode / Solar Cell.

• Solar System & Universe Highlights (pp. 51–52):
— Sun: Photosphere (bright disc, ~6000 K), Chromosphere; powered by nuclear fusion.
— Terrestrial (Rocky) Planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Jovian (Gas) Planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
— Key Planet Facts: Mercury (closest to Sun); Venus (closest to Earth & hottest planet due to CO₂ greenhouse effect); Mars (Red planet); Jupiter (largest planet); Asteroid Belt lies between Mars and Jupiter (largest asteroid: Ceres).

Electromagnetic Spectrum & Diagnostic Devices (Arihant pp. 49–50)

All electromagnetic waves are transverse, require no medium, and travel in vacuum at the same speed c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
Order of Increasing Frequency (and Decreasing Wavelength): Radio Waves → Microwaves → Infrared (IR) → Visible Light (R-O-Y-G-B-I-V) → Ultraviolet (UV) → X-Rays → Gamma (γ) Rays.
