1B. Six Principal Organs of the United Nations Structure & Voting

Principal Organ HQ & Key Exam Pointers
1. General Assembly (UNGA) HQ: New York. 1st Session: Held on 10 Jan 1946 at Methodist Central Hall, London, England PYQ.
Function: Main stage for multilateral diplomacy & policy deliberation PYQ.
Voting: Decisions on important questions (peace/security, new members, budget) require a Two-Thirds (2/3) Majority of members present and voting PYQ. (1st Woman President: Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit, 1953 PROBABLE).
2. Security Council (UNSC) HQ: New York. Chiefly responsible for maintaining international peace & security PYQ.
Composition: 15 Members — 5 Permanent (P5) (China, France, Russia, UK, USA) PYQ + 10 Non-Permanent (elected for 2-year terms).
Veto Power: A negative vote cast by any P5 member blocks adoption of a substantive resolution PYQ (abstention/absence does not count as veto).
Role in SG Appointment: UNSC recommends the appointment of the Secretary-General to the UNGA PYQ.
3. Economic & Social Council (ECOSOC) HQ: New York. 54 Members elected by UNGA for overlapping 3-year terms PROBABLE.
Principal Responsibility: Coordinating international economic and social activities of the UN and its specialized agencies PYQ.
4. Int'l Court of Justice (ICJ) HQ: Peace Palace, The Hague, Netherlands (only principal organ outside NY). Established in 1945 PYQ.
Role: Principal judicial organ of the UN PYQ.
Judges: 15 Judges serving 9-year terms, elected jointly by the UN General Assembly (UNGA) and UN Security Council (UNSC) voting independently PYQ.
5. Trusteeship Council Established under Article 75 PYQ to supervise 11 Trust Territories. Suspended operations on 1 Nov 1994 after Palau gained independence PROBABLE.
6. UN Secretariat Carries out day-to-day substantive & administrative work of the UN; headed by the Secretary-General (5-year renewable term).

2A. Indian Musical Instruments (Natya Shastra 4-Fold Classification)

1. Tat Vadya (Chordophones / Stringed — Plucked or Bowed):
• Examples: Veena, Sitar PYQ, Sarod, Santoor, Sarangi, Gottuvadyam, Ektara.
• Tambura (Tanpura): Regarded as the classical drone instrument of Indian music PYQ.
2. Sushir Vadya (Aerophones / Wind — Blowing Air):
• Examples: Shehnai (produces sound by blowing air rather than striking/plucking) PYQ, Bansuri (Flute), Nadaswaram, Pungi/Been.
• Harmonium Component: The Bellows is the rear foldable section of the portable wooden box that pumps/pushes air into the instrument PYQ.
3. Avanaddha Vadya (Membranophones / Percussion — Stretched Skin):
• Tabla: Principal Hindustani percussion instrument PYQ.
• Pakhawaj: Barrel-shaped two-headed drum that traditionally accompanies the Dhrupad form of Hindustani music PYQ.
• Thavil: Carnatic temple percussion instrument played with one face by hand (with finger caps) and the other face by a stick PYQ.
• Others: Mridangam, Chenda, Dholak, Khanjira, Damaru.
4. Ghan Vadya (Idiophones / Solid Non-Membranous Percussion):
• Ghatam: Earthenware clay pot used in Carnatic music; classified as an Idiophone (Ghan Vadya) PYQ.
• Others: Manjira (Cymbals), Khartal, Jaltarang, Chimta, Ghungroo.

2B. Classical Music: Hindustani vs. Carnatic & Musicology

  • • Two Major Classical Forms of India: Hindustani (North India) & Carnatic (South India) PYQ.
  • • Core Distinction: Hindustani music emphasizes improvisation (alap, taan), whereas Carnatic music focuses more on fixed compositions (Kriti/Kruti) PYQ.
  • • Etymology of 'Nada' (Musical Sound): Combination of two syllables — phonetically 'Na' indicates Prana (Life Breath / Air) and 'Da' indicates Agni (Fire) PYQ.
  • • Hindustani Forms:
    • Khayal: Evolved under the patronage of the Mughal courts (popularized by Sadarang & Adarang under Muhammad Shah Rangila; origin attributed to Amir Khusrau); predominant North Indian vocal form PYQ.
    • Dhrupad: Oldest surviving Hindustani vocal style (Raja Man Singh Tomar, Tansen); accompanied by Pakhawaj PYQ. (Note: Dhrupad is NOT a Carnatic Tala anga PYQ).
    • Others: Thumri (semi-classical romantic), Tarana, Tappa (originated from camel riders of Punjab), Dadra.
  • • Carnatic Compositional Forms:
    • Kriti / Kruti: Principal compositional form in Carnatic music, valued primarily for its musical structure and melodic expression rather than lyrical content alone PYQ (Trinity: Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri PROBABLE).
    • Varnam: Performed at the beginning of a recital to warm up & reveal the raga's structure.
    • Tillana: Rhythmic concluding piece (counterpart to Hindustani Tarana).
  • • Shadangas (6 Subdivisions / Angas of Carnatic Taalas):
    1. Anudhrutam: 1 beat (clap) — symbol `U` PYQ.
    2. Dhrutam: 2 beats (1 clap + 1 wave) — symbol `O` PYQ.
    3. Laghu: Consists of one beat (clap) followed by a variable number of finger counts (3, 4, 5, 7, or 9 depending on Jati) — symbol `|` PYQ.
    4–6. Guru (8 aksharas), Plutam (12 aksharas), Kakapadam (16 aksharas).

4A. Folk Dances & Theatre of Punjab

  • • Nakal (Naqal) — Direct Counterpart to Haryana's 'Saang': Traditional folk theatre/mimicry of Punjab performed by Mirasis / Naqqals; literally means "to imitate / impersonate" through satire and wit PROBABLE.
  • • Bhangra: Vigorous harvest folk dance of Punjabi men (originated in Sialkot/Majha region during Vaisakhi); accompanied by Dhol, Chimta, Sapp, Katto.
  • • Giddha: Energetic women's folk dance derived from ancient ring dance; accompanied by rhythmic clapping and Boliyan (couplets); no musical instrument required (except Dholki).
  • • Kikli: Fast whirling folk dance performed by young girls in pairs holding hands crosswise (`Tirkhi`).
  • • Jhumar (Jhoomar): Ecstatic, slow-rhythmic men's folk dance originating from Sandal Bar (West Punjab); called "Babbu/Satluj rhythm".
  • • Malwai Giddha: Performed by men of the Malwa region using traditional instruments like Bugdu, Chimta, Kato, Sapp.
  • • Luddi: Victory dance performed by men (snapping fingers, clapping, half-turns).
  • • Sammi: Traditional women's dance of the Sandal Bar / Pothohar region (named after legendary heroine Sammi).
  • • Gatka: Traditional Sikh martial art associated with Guru Hargobind Ji & Guru Gobind Singh Ji (recognized in National Games).

4B. Folk Songs, Instruments & Craft Heritage

  • • Folk Songs by Occasion:
    • Suhag: Sung by bride's family during wedding ceremonies.
    • Ghorian: Sung by groom's family at weddings.
    • Sithniyan: Humorous/satirical teasing songs sung by women at weddings.
    • Alahniyan / Vain: Elegies/mourning songs sung on death.
    • Jugni, Mahiya, Tappe, Dhola, Jindua: Popular lyrical folk forms.
  • • Traditional Folk Instruments of Punjab:
    • Tat (String): Tumbi (1-string plucked instrument popularized by Lal Chand Yamla Jatt), Sarangi, Rabab (played by Bhai Mardana Ji), Taus (peacock-shaped string instrument designed by Guru Hargobind Ji), Bugchu/Bugdu.
    • Sushir (Wind): Algoza (paired twin bamboo flutes played simultaneously).
    • Avanaddha (Percussion): Dhol, Dhad (hourglass drum used by Dhadi singers with Sarangi), Dholki.
    • Ghan (Idiophone): Chimta (tongs with brass jingles), Sapp / Chikka (collapsible wooden clapper), Kato (squirrel-on-stick clapper), Gagar (metal pitcher).
  • • Phulkari (GI Tag): Literally "Flower Work" — traditional embroidery done with untwisted silk floss (Pat) on coarse handspun cotton (Khaddar) from the reverse side. Bagh is when embroidery covers the entire fabric leaving no base visible.

4C. Fairs, Festivals & Literary / Sports Icons

  • • Major Fairs & Festivals of Punjab:
    • Hola Mohalla (Anandpur Sahib): Started by Guru Gobind Singh Ji (1701) on the day after Holi to display martial skills.
    • Maghi Mela (Sri Muktsar Sahib): Commemorates the martyrdom of the Chali Mukte (40 Liberated Ones) in Jan (14 Jan).
    • Chapar Mela (Ludhiana): Held in Sept in memory of Guga Pir.
    • Jarag Mela (Payal, Ludhiana): Held in March-April to honour Goddess Seetla Mata (also called Baheria Mela; donkeys are decorated).
    • Roshni Fair (Jagraon, Ludhiana): Held at the Mazar of Pir Baba Mohkam Din.
    • Harballabh Sangeet Sammelan (Jalandhar): Oldest festival of Indian classical music in the world (since 1875 at Devi Talab Mandir).
    • Teeyan: Monsoon festival celebrated by women in the month of Sawan.
  • • Punjabi Literary Giants (High-Yield):
    • Baba Sheikh Farid Ji: Father of Punjabi Poetry.
    • Waris Shah: Authored masterpiece Heer Ranjha (1766).
    • Hashim Shah: Sassi Punnu | Fazal Shah / Peelu: Sohni Mahiwal / Mirza Sahiban.
    • Amrita Pritam: 1st woman to win Sahitya Akademi Award (1956, Sunehade); Jnanpith Award (1981, Kagaz Te Canvas); Pinjar, Ajj Aakhaan Waris Shah Nu.
    • Gurdial Singh: Jnanpith Award (1999); wrote Marhi Da Deeva.
    • Shiv Kumar Batalvi: Youngest Sahitya Akademi winner (1967, Loona); called Birha Da Sultan.
    • Bhai Vir Singh: Father of Modern Punjabi Literature (Sundri).
    • Nanak Singh: Father of Punjabi Novel (Chitta Lahu, Pavittar Paapi).
