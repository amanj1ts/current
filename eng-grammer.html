Module 1: Active & Passive Voice Transformation Matrix (Rules 127 – 131)

Voice Conversion Core Principles:
  • Tense does NOT change in Voice (do not confuse with Narration). Continuous keeps continuous via being, Perfect keeps perfect via been.
  • Future Continuous and all three Perfect Continuous tenses have no standard passive voice forms.
  • Interrogative Trap: Auxiliary remains inverted before the imported subject. Do not change into assertive order.

Voice Rule 128: Verbs with Fixed Prepositions Instead of 'By' in Passive

Certain verbs never take 'by' when converted to passive voice. They take dedicated fixed prepositions:

• Known to: Act: I know you. → Pass: You are known to me (known by me).
• Surprised at: He was surprised at her conduct.
• Annoyed at / with: Annoyed at something / with someone.
• Pleased with: I was pleased with his manners.
• Satisfied with: The manager was satisfied with him.
• Absorbed in: He was absorbed in his studies.
• Married to: Rama was married to Sita.
• Contained in: This jug contains milk → Milk is contained in this jug.
• Consisted in: The solution consisted in regular practice.

Person Shift: The S-O-N Formula

• 1st Person (I, My, Me, We, Our, Us) → Changes according to the Subject of the Reporting Verb.

• 2nd Person (You, Your) → Changes according to the Object of the Reporting Verb.

• 3rd Person (He, She, It, They, Their) → No Change (N).

Example: He said to me, "I like you." → He told me that he liked me.
Example: She said to me, "You did not sing well." → She told me that I had not sung well.
Interrogative Sentence Rules (Rules 148 – 149)

• Yes/No Questions: Use connector if or whether.

• Wh- Questions: Use the Wh-word itself as the conjunction. Never add that, if, or whether.

• Word Order Transformation: In indirect speech, the question structure shifts back to Subject + Verb, and the question mark is removed.

NARRATION EXAM TRAPS:

1. Said to → Told. Never say told to me.

2. Asked + if/whether is correct. Never say asked that.

3. Told + that is correct. Never say told if.

4. Direct: She said to him, "Why has she played?"
Indirect: She asked him why she had played.
Trap: She asked him if/whether why she had played. (Double conjunction error)

Module 3: Non-Finite Verbs: Infinitives, Gerunds & Participles (Rules 140 – 146)

Rule 140 & 141: Verbs & Adjectives followed by Infinitive with 'To'

The following verbs require an Infinitive (to + $V_1$), never a gerund:

Want, Wish, Desire, Hope, Decide, Determined, Promise, Prefer, Forget, Need, Plan, Offer, Hesitate.

• I planned to go there. (I planned going there.)
• He wants to open a restaurant. (He wants opening...)

Adjectives requiring to + $V_1$:

Able, Unable, Happy, Glad, Anxious, Pleased, Displeased, Lucky, Surprised.

• I am pleased to help you. (I am pleased helping you.)
Rule 142: Bare Infinitive After Idiomatic Expressions

The following modal idioms take a Bare Infinitive (without 'to'):

Had better, Had sooner, Had rather, Would rather, Would sooner + V1 (bare)
• You had better go now. (You had better to go now.)
• I would rather die than beg. (would rather to die)
Rules 143 & 144: Verbs & Prepositions Followed by Gerund ($V_1 + ing$)

Verbs taking Gerund ($V_1 + ing$) directly:

Avoid, Dislike, Enjoy, Keep, Mind, Postpone, Forgive, Deny, Excuse.

• I dislike going there. (I dislike to go there.)
• She enjoyed helping him. (She enjoyed to help him.)

Rule 144 (Golden Rule): Any standard preposition must be followed by a Gerund:

at, in, on, by, of, from, for, with, without, before, after + V1 + ing
• I am fond of teaching. (fond of teach / to teach)
• He will come after finishing his work. (after finish)
Rules 145 & 146: Idiomatic Phrases Ending in 'To' + Gerund
THE DEADLIEST PSSSB EXAM TRAP: Students see 'to' and pick a bare verb. The following fixed phrases contain a prepositional 'to' and MUST be followed by $V_1 + ing$:
• Accustomed to + $V_1+ing$
• Addicted to + $V_1+ing$
• Prone to + $V_1+ing$
• Confess to + $V_1+ing$
• In addition to + $V_1+ing$
• Look forward to + $V_1+ing$
• With a view to + $V_1+ing$
• Habituated to / Used to + $V_1+ing$
• I look forward to meeting you. (look forward to meet you)
• He is addicted to gambling. (addicted to gamble)
• I am used to working at night. (used to work)

Rule 146 Idioms: It is no use, Can't help, Can't bear, Can't stand, Busy, Worth take Gerunds:

• It is no use talking to him. | I am busy writing this book.

Module 4: Subject-Verb Agreement, Pronouns & Articles (Rules 1 – 90)

Rules 1 – 3: Indefinite Pronouns

• There: Introductory subject. Takes verb according to following real subject:

There is a chair. | There are chairs.

• Person Pronouns: Everyone, Someone, Anyone, No one, Everybody, Somebody, Anybody, Nobody take singular verbs and singular pronouns (he/his/him):

• Everyone wants to crack the exam.
• Everyone should love his parents (their).

• Thing Pronouns: Everything, Something, Anything, Nothing take singular verbs and singular pronoun it/its/itself:

• Everything has its own beauty (his).
Rules 4 – 10: Each, Every, Either, Neither

• Rule 4: Nothing but / Everything but + (Singular/Plural Noun) + Singular Verb:

• Nothing but dresses was bought. (were)

• Rule 6: Each of / Either of / Neither of + Plural Noun/Pronoun + Singular Verb:

• Neither of them was a culprit.

• Rule 8: Each + Each = Singular Verb:

• Each boy and each girl is present.

• Rule 9 Trap: Plural Noun/Pronoun + each = Plural Verb:

• We each have a dress. (We each has)
• The girls each have done this work.

• Rule 10: 'Every' is only an adjective, never a pronoun. Every of men is incorrect; use Each of the men.

Rules 11 – 15: Dual vs Plural Usage

• Each vs Every: 'Each' is for 2 or 2+; 'Every' is strictly for more than 2 (2+):

• I can write with each hand (every hand - humans have only 2 hands).

• Rule 13: Both, Several, Various, Both of, Several of + Plural Noun + Plural Verb:

• Both boys are arrogant. | Several of them were amicable.

• Rule 14 & 15 Trap: 'Both' and 'Not' can NEVER be used together. 'Both' is positive:

Both Rahul and Sahil are not coming. (Wrong)
Neither Rahul nor Sahil is coming. (Correct)
Rules 16 – 24: Few vs Little Nuances

Few (Countable / Plural Verb):

• Few: Almost none (Negative sense): I have few books, so I will buy new ones.

• A few: Some / small positive quantity: I have a few books, so I don't need to buy.

• The few: The whole small quantity available: I have read the few books that I had.

Little (Uncountable / Singular Verb):

• Little: Almost none (Negative): I have little money, so I cannot buy.

• A little: Some (Positive): I have a little money, I can buy it.

• The little: The whole small amount: I spent the little money I had.

Rules 25 – 32: One of, Many, and Number

• Rule 24: One of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb:

• One of my attires is dear (costly).

• Rule 25 (Crucial Trap): The only one of + Plural Noun + who/that + Singular Verb:

• Radha is the only one of the girls who has aided me.

• Contrast with Relative Pronoun: One of the boys who are playing (plural).

• Rule 27: Many a / Many an + Singular Noun + Singular Verb:

• Many a man has tried, but few have succeeded.

• Rules 29 & 30:

• A number of + Plural Noun → Plural Verb (A number of workers are present).
• The number of + Plural Noun → Singular Verb (The number of workers is present).

• Rules 31 & 32: A variety of... takes plural verb; The variety of... takes singular verb.

Rules 33 – 35: Subject Alignment Formulas

• Rule 33 (1st Subject Governs): Joined by With, Together with, Along with, In addition to, As well as, Accompanied by, Like, Unlike, Including, Excluding, But, Besides, Except, Rather than, Headed by, Led by → Verb agrees with the FIRST subject:

• The manager along with his staff members is present.
• She, and not her friends, is exquisite.

• Rule 34 (2nd / Nearest Subject Governs): Joined by Neither... nor, Either... or, Not only... but also, Or → Verb agrees with 2nd subject:

• Neither he nor I am accusable.
• Not only you but also he wants to join.

• Rule 35: Relative pronouns (who, which, that) take verbs agreeing with their antecedent:

• It is I who am singing this song. (who is)
Rules 36: Nouns Always Singular vs Nouns Always Plural

Always Singular (Uncountable - Cannot be made plural):

Advice, stationery, crockery, information, machinery, furniture, fuel, repair, news, poetry, business, bedding, rice, economics, physics, mathematics, cutlery, innings, jewellery, gallows.

• Her advice is commendable. (advices are)
• Second-hand furniture was sold here. (furnitures were)

Always Plural (Take Plural Verb - Cannot be made singular):

Cattle, gentry, vermin, peasantry, artillery, people, clergy, company, police, trousers, scissors, spectacles, stockings, shorts, measles, goods, alms, premises, thanks, tidings, annals.

• The cattle are grazing. (cattle is / cattles are)
• The police are looking for the convict.
• Exception: The police of Punjab is looking for the thief (specific unit).

Irregular Foreign Plural Forms:

Axis → Axes | Bacterium → Bacteria
Phenomenon → Phenomena | Criterion → Criteria
Datum → Data | Index → Indices
Medium → Media | Oasis → Oases | Radius → Radii
Rules 37 – 44: Special Noun Structures & Collective Nouns

• Rule 37: The + Adjective works as a Plural Subject for the whole class, taking a Plural Verb:

• The poor are diligent. (The poor is / A poor is)
• The rich are arrogant. | He is a rich boy (correct).

• Rule 38: Noun + Preposition + Same Noun takes Singular Verb and singular nouns:

• Book after book was read. (Books after books were read)

• Rules 39 – 42: Units (Hundred, Thousand, Dozen, Score, Million):

• Definite Numeral: She bought two dozen dresses. (two dozens dresses)
• Indefinite / of: Two dozens of these dresses are costly.
• Ascending Hierarchy: Hundreds of thousands of students took the exam.

• Rule 43: Collective Nouns: Jury, Committee, Council, Choir, Union:

• Unanimous: The Jury has given its decision.
• Divided: The Jury were divided in their opinion.

• Rule 44: Common gender infants take neuter pronouns: The baby has broken its toy (his/her).

Rules 45 – 50: Pronoun Order & Cases

• Rules 45 & 46: Possessive Adjective comes before noun (my dress). Possessive Pronoun replaces noun (Hers is a new phone).

• Rule 47: Case Comparison:

• She is better than I (than me) [Subject vs Subject].
• He helped me more than him [Object vs Object].

• Rule 48: 2-3-1 Order (Good / Normal Sense): 2nd → 3rd → 1st Person:

• You, she and I are friends.

• Rule 49: 1-2-3 Order (Guilt, Sin, Error, Fault Sense): 1st → 2nd → 3rd Person:

• I, you and she are culprits.
Rules 51 – 57: Punctuation & Pronoun Traps

• Rule 51 & 52: It's = It is / It has. Its = Possessive adjective (The baby broke its toy).

• Rule 53: Who vs Whom: Who is subjective (Who helped you?). Whom is objective (Whom did you tell?).

• Rules 54 & 55: Article Subject Agreement:

• Two Articles: The principal and the manager have done their duties (2 people).
• One Article: The principal and manager has done his duty (1 person).

• Rules 56 & 57: Each other is for two people. One another is for more than two (2+):

• Pawan and Atul helped each other.
• All the dealers helped one another.
Rules 58 – 74: Adjective Degrees & Comparison

• Rule 63: of + the + two takes The + Comparative (2nd degree):

• Ravi is the better of the two boys.

• Rules 64 & 65: As... as (positive context). So... as (strictly negative context).

• Rule 69: Parallel Comparison: Compare equals using that of / those of:

• Roads of Punjab are wider than those of Himachal (than Himachal).

• Rules 70 & 71: Cases of 'Enough':

• Noun modifier: I have enough money (precedes noun).
• Adj modifier: She is rich enough (follows adjective).

• Rule 72: Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer, preferable take to (never 'than').

• Rule 73: Comparing 2 qualities of the SAME person requires more + positive degree:

• She is more wise than beautiful (wiser than beautiful).

• Rule 74: Absolutes: Round, square, unique, perfect, ideal, complete, chief, excellent never take more/most.

Rules 75 – 85: Article Rules & Omissions

• Rule 75: 'A' before consonant sounds; 'An' before vowel sounds (An M.L.A, An hour, An S.D.O, A university, A one-rupee note, A union).

• Rule 76: A black and white dog = 1 dog. A black and a white dog = 2 dogs.

• Rule 80 & 81: Ordinals take 'The' (The first chapter). But no article before Last/Next + time:

• last night / last year (the last night).

• Rule 84 & 85: Article Omission: No article before diseases, cities, seasons, universities, languages, festivals, sports, uncountable nouns.

• Exception: When specified: The typhoid of 2019 was severe; The gold of China.
Rules 86 – 88: Apostrophe ('s) Rules

• Rule 86: ('s) is NOT used with non-living things:

• The page of a book (A book's page).
• The ink of this pen (This pen's ink).

• Exceptions: Time, distance, weight, country, personified entities:

• Nature's law | India's history | A month's holiday | At a stone's throw.

• Rule 87 Trap: Pronouns already possessive never take apostrophe:

• Yours faithfully (Your's faithfully) | hers (her's).

• Rule 88: Indefinite pronouns accept apostrophe: Someone's book | Everybody's duty.

Rules 89 – 90: Until vs Unless

• Until = Refers strictly to Time.

• Unless = Refers strictly to Condition / Action.

TRAP: Both are negative connectors. Never use 'not' inside the until/unless clause:
• Don't go anywhere until I get back. (until I do not get back)
• You cannot pass unless you work hard. (unless you do not work hard)

• Rule 90: Till and Until have identical meaning: Wait till noon / Wait until noon.

Module 5: Conjunctions, Tenses, Conditionals & Question Tags (Rules 91 – 139, 150)

Rules 91 – 100: Correlative Conjunction Pairs

• Rule 91: Both + and (Never use 'as well as'):
Both boys and girls are good. (Both boys as well as girls)

• Rule 92: Lest + Should (or bare $V_1$). Never use 'not' or 'would':
Work hard, lest you should fail.

• Rules 93 & 94: When (point in time, age, sequential); While (two simultaneous continuous actions):
While I was playing, my mother was cooking.

• Rules 95 & 96: Too much + Uncountable Noun (too much pain); Much too + Adjective (much too painful / much too strong).

• Rule 97: No sooner + than (Never use 'then' or 'when').

• Rule 98: Hardly / Scarcely / Barely + when / before (Never 'than').

• Rule 99: Although / Though + yet / comma (,).

• Rule 100: Between... and | From... to.

Rules 101 – 120: Tense Trigger Keywords

• Simple Present (Rules 101-103): Daily, every day, usually, generally, sometimes, at times → $V_1 / V_1+s(es)$.

• Present Continuous (Rule 105): Now, right now, nowadays, at present, at the moment → $\text{is/am/are} + V_1+ing$.

• Present Perfect (Rules 107-108): Just, just now, already, recently, lately, yet, so far, ever → $\text{has/have} + V_3$.

• Rule 108: It is the first time / second time... takes Present Perfect: It is the first time I have seen you upset.

• Since vs For (Rules 110-113): Since = Point of time (Monday, 2020, morning); For = Period/amount of time (2 hours, 5 days).

• Rule 112: Since last Monday. | Rule 113: For the last Monday.

• Simple Past (Rules 114-115): Yesterday, ago, last week/year, It is time, It is high time, It is about time takes $V_2$:
It is high time she started a new job (starts).

• Future Perfect (Rule 120): By + time expression (by Monday, by 2025) takes will have + $V_3$.

Rules 121 – 126: Conditionals Architecture

• Zero: If + Simple Pres, Simple Pres (Universal truth):
If we heat butter, it melts.

• Type 1: If + Simple Present ($V_1$), Simple Future ($\text{will} + V_1$):
If you study, you will pass.

• Type 2: If + Simple Past ($V_2$), would / could / might + $V_1$:
If you studied, you would pass.

• Type 3: If + Past Perfect ($\text{had} + V_3$), would have / could have + $V_3$:
If you had studied, you would have passed.

• Rule 123 (Past Action Precedence): Past Perfect ($\text{had} + V_3$) for 1st action + Simple Past ($V_2$) for 2nd action:
Before we reached the station, the train had left.

• Rule 126: Present Perfect + since + Past Simple ($V_2$):
We have not seen him since he left the army.

Rules 132 – 139: Question Tag Comprehensive Matrix
• Positive Statement → Negative Tag
• Negative Statement → Positive Tag
• Tag contraction must always use n't
• I am → aren't I? / ain't I?
• I am not → am I?
• Will → won't + subject?
• Shall → shan't + subject?
• Rule 134: Indefinite Pronouns of Person (Everyone, Someone, Nobody, Somebody) take pronoun they in question tag:
Everybody is happy, aren't they? (isn't he?)
• Rule 139: Indefinite Pronouns of Thing (Everything, Nothing) take pronoun it:
Everything looks captivating, doesn't it?
TAG TRAP (Rules 135 & 136): Sentences containing inherent negative words (Few, Little, Hardly, Barely, Scarcely, Never, Neither, None, Nothing, Seldom) require a Positive Question Tag:
• A barking dog seldom bites, does it? | Few students have this book, have they?
• Contrast: A few / A little are positive words → take Negative Tag: I read a few stories, haven't I?

• Rules 137 & 138: Dare & Need: When used as Main Verbs (takes do/does/did in tag). When used as Auxiliary (takes dare/need in tag):
She dares to speak, doesn't she? (Main verb) | She dare not speak, dare she? (Auxiliary)

Rule 150: Adverb Order: Manner – Place – Time (M-P-T)

When multiple adverbs qualify the same verb in a sentence, they must strictly follow the M-P-T order:

Subject + Verb + [Manner (How)] + [Place (Where)] + [Time (When)]
• She sang beautifully (M) at the concert (P) yesterday (T).
• I went yesterday there. → I went there (P) yesterday (T).
