01
Number System

Divisibility Rules: A number is divisible by 4 if its last 2 digits are divisible by 4; by 8 if its last 3 digits are divisible by 8; by 9 if the sum of digits is divisible by 9; and by 11 if the difference between the sum of digits in odd places and even places is 0 or a multiple of 11[cite: 2, 4, 20].

Algebraic Identities: When $a + b + c = 0$, the identity states $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$[cite: 23]. This is a frequent PSSSB direct scoring model[cite: 23].

Sum of First n Natural Numbers
$$S_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}$$
Conditional Cubic Identity
$$\text{If } a + b + c = 0 \implies a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$$
Difference of Reversed Two-Digit Number
$$\Delta = (10a + b) - (10b + a) = 9(a - b) \quad (\text{Always divisible by 9})$$
Unit Digit Cyclicity: Digits $\{0, 1, 5, 6\}$ always end in the same digit for any positive power[cite: 18]. For 4 and 9: $4^{\text{odd}} = 4, 4^{\text{even}} = 6$; $9^{\text{odd}} = 9, 9^{\text{even}} = 1$. Digits $\{2, 3, 7, 8\}$ have cyclicity 4 (divide exponent by 4 and use remainder)[cite: 18].
PYQ #1767[cite: 23] PSSSB Anganwadi / Technical
If $a = 15, b = -7,$ and $c = -8$, then the value of $a^3 + b^3 + c^3$ is:[cite: 23]
A) 2520 B) 1260 C) 0 D) -840[cite: 23]
Standard Method: Check $a + b + c = 15 + (-7) + (-8) = 15 - 15 = 0$[cite: 23].
Since $a + b + c = 0$, $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$[cite: 23].
$$= 3 \times 15 \times (-7) \times (-8) = 45 \times 56 = 2520$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 23]
PYQ #1690[cite: 18] PSSSB JE Civil / Electrical

Solution:

3. Unit digit of $341^{491}$: Base ends in 1, any power of 1 ends in $1 \implies 1$[cite: 18].
Combine: $4 \times 5 \times 1 = 20 \rightarrow \text{Unit digit is } 0$[cite: 18].

PYQ #1741[cite: 21] Chandigarh NTT / PSSSB
The difference of a number consisting of two digits and the number formed by interchanging the digits is always divisible by:[cite: 21]
A) 5 B) 7 C) 9 D) 11[cite: 21]
Direct Proof: Let original number be $10a + b$. Interchanged number $= 10b + a$[cite: 21].
Difference $= (10a + b) - (10b + a) = 9(a - b)$[cite: 21]. Hence always divisible by 9[cite: 21].

02
HCF & LCM

Fundamental Relationship: For any two numbers $A$ and $B$, $\text{HCF}(A, B) \times \text{LCM}(A, B) = A \times B$[cite: 3, 7, 21].

Co-prime numbers: If the ratio of two numbers is $a:b$, then the numbers are $a \cdot H$ and $b \cdot H$, and their $\text{LCM} = a \times b \times H$[cite: 9, 20].

HCF and LCM of Fractions
$$\text{LCM}\left(\frac{a}{b}, \frac{c}{d}\right) = \frac{\text{LCM}(a, c)}{\text{HCF}(b, d)}, \quad \text{HCF}\left(\frac{a}{b}, \frac{c}{d}\right) = \frac{\text{HCF}(a, c)}{\text{LCM}(b, d)}$$
Product Formula
$$\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = \text{Product of Two Numbers}$$
Simultaneous Bell / Traffic Light Interval: When lights change every $t_1, t_2, t_3$ seconds, find $\text{LCM}(t_1, t_2, t_3)$[cite: 12]. Convert LCM seconds to minutes/seconds and add to initial start time[cite: 12].
PYQ #1511[cite: 5] PSSSB Hostel Superintendent
Find the LCM of $\frac{2}{3}, \frac{3}{5}, \frac{4}{7},$ and $\frac{9}{13}$:[cite: 5]
A) 36 B) 1/36 C) 1/1365 D) 12/455[cite: 5]
Formula Application: $\text{LCM of fractions} = \frac{\text{LCM of Numerators}}{\text{HCF of Denominators}}$[cite: 5].
Numerators: $2, 3, 4, 9 \implies \text{LCM} = 36$[cite: 5].
Denominators: $3, 5, 7, 13$ (all prime to each other) $\implies \text{HCF} = 1$[cite: 5].
$$\text{LCM} = \frac{36}{1} = 36$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 5]
PYQ #1613[cite: 12] PSSSB Clerk / Driver Operator
Traffic lights at three crossings change after 48 s, 72 s, and 108 s respectively. If they all change simultaneously at 8:20:00 hrs, when will they change simultaneously again?[cite: 12]
A) 8:27:12 h B) 8:27:24 h C) 8:27:36 h D) 8:27:48 h[cite: 12]
Step 1: Find $\text{LCM}(48, 72, 108)$[cite: 12]:
$48 = 2^4 \times 3$, $72 = 2^3 \times 3^2$, $108 = 2^2 \times 3^3$
$\text{LCM} = 2^4 \times 3^3 = 16 \times 27 = 432\text{ seconds}$[cite: 12].
Step 2: Convert to minutes: $432\text{ s} = 7\text{ min } 12\text{ s}$[cite: 12].
Step 3: Next simultaneous change: $8:20:00 + 0:07:12 = 8:27:12\text{ hrs}$[cite: 12].
Correct Answer: A[cite: 12]
PYQ #1763[cite: 23] PSSSB Group-B
The LCM of two prime numbers $p$ and $q$ ($p < q$) is 407. Then $q - 3p$ is equal to:[cite: 23]
A) 1 B) 3 C) 4 D) 7[cite: 23]
Concept: For two prime numbers, $\text{LCM}(p, q) = p \times q = 407$[cite: 23].
Factoring 407: $407 = 11 \times 37$[cite: 23]. Since both 11 and 37 are prime and $p < q \implies p = 11, q = 37$[cite: 23].
Now calculate $q - 3p$:
$$37 - 3(11) = 37 - 33 = 4$$ Correct Answer: C[cite: 23]
03
Average

Arithmetic Mean: $\text{Average} = \frac{\text{Total Sum}}{\text{Total Count}}$. When numbers form an Arithmetic Progression (consecutive odd/even numbers), the Average is exactly the middle term or $\frac{\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term}}{2}$[cite: 18].

Sum of Extremes in AP
$$\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term} = 2 \times \text{Average}$$
Inclusion Formula (Manager / Teacher)
$$\text{Value of New Member} = \text{Old Average} + (\text{New Count} \times \text{Increase in Average})$$
Zero Average Constraint (PYQ Pattern): If the average of $n$ numbers is 0, their sum must be 0[cite: 18]. At most, $(n - 1)$ numbers can be positive (balanced out by a single large negative number)[cite: 18].
PYQ #1516[cite: 5] PSSSB Hostel Superintendent
The average salary of 35 workers is Rs. 1800. When the salary of the manager is included, the average increases by Rs. 125. What is the salary of the manager?[cite: 5]
A) 6200 B) 6300 C) 6400 D) 6500[cite: 5]
Direct Deviation Shortcut:
$$\text{Manager's Salary} = \text{Old Average} + (\text{Total Persons Including Manager} \times \text{Increase})$$
$$= 1800 + (36 \times 125) = 1800 + 4500 = 6300\text{ Rs.}$$ Correct Answer: B[cite: 5]
PYQ #1682[cite: 18] PSSSB JE Civil
If the average of 35 consecutive even numbers is 44, find the sum of the first and last numbers.[cite: 18]
A) 70 B) 79 C) 88 D) 78[cite: 18]
Speed Shortcut: In any AP series, $\text{Average} = \frac{\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term}}{2}$[cite: 18].
$$\text{First Term} + \text{Last Term} = 2 \times \text{Average} = 2 \times 44 = 88$$ Zero need to calculate the actual individual numbers![cite: 18]
Correct Answer: C[cite: 18]
PYQ #1688[cite: 18] PSSSB JE Electrical
Twenty numbers have an average of zero. At most, how many may be greater than zero?[cite: 18]
A) 0 B) 1 C) 10 D) 19[cite: 18]
Logic: Sum of 20 numbers $= 20 \times 0 = 0$[cite: 18]. We can have 19 numbers positive (e.g., $+1$ each, sum $= +19$) and the 20th number as $-19$[cite: 18].
Thus, at most $20 - 1 = 19$ numbers can be greater than zero[cite: 18].
Correct Answer: D[cite: 18]
04
Percentage

Base Shifting & Expenditure Invariance: Since $\text{Price} \times \text{Consumption} = \text{Expenditure}$, if expenditure remains constant, price and consumption are inversely proportional[cite: 6]. An increase of $\frac{a}{b}$ in price requires a decrease of $\frac{a}{a+b}$ in consumption[cite: 6].

Successive Percentage Change
$$\text{Net } \% = x + y + \frac{xy}{100}$$
Equal Increase and Decrease Shortcut
$$\text{Net Change} = -\frac{x^2}{100}\% \quad (\text{Always a Decrease})$$
Sugar Price Increase $\to$ Consumption Decrease: If price increases by $20\% = \frac{1}{5}$, consumption must decrease by $\frac{1}{5+1} = \frac{1}{6} = 16\frac{2}{3}\% = \frac{50}{3}\%$[cite: 6]. Solved mentally in 2 seconds!
PYQ #1520[cite: 6] PSSSB School Librarian
The price of sugar increases by 20%. By what percent must a housewife reduce consumption of sugar so that expenditure remains unchanged?[cite: 6]
A) 50/3 % B) 20 % C) 80 % D) 260/3 %[cite: 6]
Shortcut: Reduction $\% = \frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100 = \frac{20}{120} \times 100 = \frac{100}{6} = \frac{50}{3}\%$[cite: 6].
Correct Answer: A[cite: 6]
PYQ #1695[cite: 19] PSSSB JE Public Health
A number is increased by 15% and then reduced by 15%. What is the effective increase or decrease in the original number?[cite: 19]
A) Does not change B) Decreases by 2.25% C) Increases by 2.25% D) Increases by 2.75%[cite: 19]
Rule: When a value is changed by $+x\%$ and then $-x\%$, it always decreases by $\frac{x^2}{100}\%$[cite: 19].
$$\text{Decrease} = \frac{15^2}{100} = \frac{225}{100} = 2.25\%$$ Correct Answer: B[cite: 19]

05
Ratio & Proportion

Coin Bag Value Equation: When coin counts are given in ratio $c_1 : c_2 : c_3$ with denominations $d_1, d_2, d_3$, express the monetary value in a single common variable: $\sum (\text{Ratio Unit} \times \text{Value}) = \text{Total Cash}$[cite: 12, 17, 23].

Partnership Profit Sharing
$$\frac{\text{Profit}_A}{\text{Profit}_B} = \frac{I_A \times T_A}{I_B \times T_B}$$
Cross-Multiplication for Age Problems: If ratio changes from $a:b$ to $c:d$ after $t$ years, the unit value $x = \frac{t(c - d)}{|ad - bc|}$[cite: 9].
PYQ #1672[cite: 17] PSSSB Clerk
In a bag, there are coins of denominations Rs. 1, Rs. 2, and Rs. 5 in the ratio 3:4:5. If the total value of the coins in the bag is Rs. 756, find the total number of coins in the bag.[cite: 17]
A) 332 B) 356 C) 252 D) 116[cite: 17]
Step 1: Let the count of coins be $3x, 4x, 5x$[cite: 17]. Total coins $= 3x + 4x + 5x = 12x$[cite: 17].
Step 2: Total monetary value: $(3x \times 1) + (4x \times 2) + (5x \times 5) = 3x + 8x + 25x = 36x$[cite: 17].
Step 3: Equate with Rs. 756: $36x = 756 \implies x = \frac{756}{36} = 21$[cite: 17].
Step 4: Total coins $= 12 \times 21 = 252$[cite: 17].
Correct Answer: C[cite: 17]
PYQ #1568[cite: 9] PSSSB Restorer
The ratio of the present age of father to that of son is 7:2. After 10 years, their ages will be in the ratio 9:4. The present age of the father is:[cite: 9]
A) 35 years B) 40 years C) 30 years D) 25 years[cite: 9]
Ratio Gap Method:
Present ratio: $7:2$ (Gap $= 5$)
After 10 yrs: $9:4$ (Gap $= 5$)
Since the difference is already balanced: Increase in ratio units $= 9 - 7 = 2\text{ units}$[cite: 9].
$2\text{ units} = 10\text{ years} \implies 1\text{ unit} = 5\text{ years}$[cite: 9].
Father's present age $= 7 \times 5 = 35\text{ years}$[cite: 9].
Correct Answer: A[cite: 9]
PYQ #1589[cite: 10] PSSSB Patwari
Twice the investment of A is equal to thrice the investment of B and five times the investment of C. If the total profit after a year is 15.5 lakhs, find the share of B in the profit.[cite: 10]
A) 7.5 lakhs B) 3 lakhs C) 4.5 lakhs D) 5 lakhs[cite: 10]
Ratio Inversion: $2A = 3B = 5C = k \implies A:B:C = \frac{1}{2} : \frac{1}{3} : \frac{1}{5}$[cite: 10].
Multiply by $\text{LCM}(2, 3, 5) = 30$: Ratio $= 15 : 10 : 6$[cite: 10].
Sum of ratio units $= 15 + 10 + 6 = 31\text{ units}$[cite: 10].
$$B\text{'s share} = \frac{10}{31} \times 15.5 = 10 \times 0.5 = 5\text{ lakhs}$$ Correct Answer: D[cite: 10]
06
SI & CI

Compound Interest Compounding Frequencies: Compounded annually $n = t, r = R\%$; Compounded half-yearly $n = 2t, r = \frac{R}{2}\%$; Compounded quarterly $n = 4t, r = \frac{R}{4}\%$[cite: 21, 23].

2-Year CI - SI Difference
$$D_2 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$
3-Year CI - SI Difference
$$D_3 = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(\frac{300 + R}{100}\right)$$
When Rate equals Time in SI: If $\text{SI} = \frac{m}{n} P$ and $R = T$, then $R = T = \sqrt{\frac{m}{n} \times 100}$[cite: 2].
PYQ #1459[cite: 2] PSSSB Supervisor / Clerk
The simple interest on a sum of money is 4/9 of the principal, and the number of years is equal to the rate percent per annum. Find the rate per annum.[cite: 2]
A) 5% B) 6 2/3% C) 6% D) 7 1/5%[cite: 2]
Formula Application: $\text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100} \implies \frac{4}{9}P = \frac{P \times R^2}{100}$[cite: 2].
$$R^2 = \frac{400}{9} \implies R = \frac{20}{3} = 6\frac{2}{3}\%$$ Correct Answer: B[cite: 2]

What is the difference between compound and simple interest on Rs. 400 after 2 years at 5% per annum?[cite: 3]
A) 10 B) 4 C) 3 D) 1[cite: 3]
Direct Formula: $D = P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 = 400 \times \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = 400 \times \frac{1}{400} = 1\text{ Rs.}$[cite: 3].
Correct Answer: D[cite: 3]
PYQ #1591[cite: 10] PSSSB Patwari
If the difference between CI and SI on a certain sum of money for 3 years at 5% p.a. is Rs. 122, then the sum is:[cite: 10]
A) Rs. 25,000 B) Rs. 19,000 C) Rs. 16,000 D) None of above[cite: 10]

$$122 = P \times \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{305}{100}\right) = P \times \frac{1}{400} \times \frac{61}{20}$$ $$P = \frac{122 \times 8000}{61} = 2 \times 8000 = 16,000\text{ Rs.}$$ Correct Answer: C[cite: 10]
07
Profit, Loss & Discount

Articles Equating: When $x \times \text{CP} = y \times \text{SP}$, the ratio $\frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{x}{y}$[cite: 4]. Profit exists if $x > y$[cite: 4].

Cost Price to Selling Price Transition
$$\text{SP}_2 = \text{SP}_1 \times \left(\frac{100 + P_2\%}{100 - L_1\%}\right)$$
Golden CP to MP Ratio
$$\frac{\text{CP}}{\text{MP}} = \frac{100 - D\%}{100 + P\%}$$
Comparing Successive Discounts (PYQ #1766): Which is greater: single 50% or 40% + 20%?[cite: 23]
Single 50% discount gives exactly 50% off[cite: 23].
$40\% + 20\% \implies 40 + 20 - \frac{800}{100} = 52\%$ off! Option II yields greater savings[cite: 23].
PYQ #1492[cite: 4] PSSSB Forest Paper
If the cost price of 18 notebooks is equal to the selling price of 10 notebooks, the profit percentage on cost price is:[cite: 4]
A) 50% B) 80% C) 100% D) None of above[cite: 4]
Ratio Method: $18 \times \text{CP} = 10 \times \text{SP} \implies \frac{\text{SP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{18}{10} = \frac{9}{5}$[cite: 4].
$$\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 = \frac{9 - 5}{5} \times 100 = \frac{4}{5} \times 100 = 80\%$$ Correct Answer: B[cite: 4]
PYQ #1506[cite: 5] PSSSB Forester
By selling an article for Rs. 3956 a trader incurs a loss of 8%. What will be the selling price if he earns a profit of 6%?[cite: 5]
A) Rs. 4558 B) Rs. 4193 C) Rs. 3718 D) None of above[cite: 5]
Direct Scaling:
$$\text{New SP} = 3956 \times \left(\frac{100 + 6}{100 - 8}\right) = 3956 \times \frac{106}{92} = 43 \times 106 = 4558\text{ Rs.}$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 5]
PYQ #1757[cite: 23] PSSSB Group-B
A shopkeeper purchased 90 kg of an item at Rs. 120 per kg. He packed 900 gm in place of 1 kg and sold all the packs at Rs. 135 per pack. What is his profit?[cite: 23]
A) 11.11% B) 20% C) 25% D) 22.22%[cite: 23]
Per-Pack Cost Analysis:
Actual Cost for 900 gm $= \frac{120}{1000} \times 900 = 108\text{ Rs.}$[cite: 23]
Selling Price per 900 gm pack $= 135\text{ Rs.}$[cite: 23]
$$\text{Profit } \% = \frac{135 - 108}{108} \times 100 = \frac{27}{108} \times 100 = \frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25\%$$ Correct Answer: C[cite: 23]
08
Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns

Total Work Efficiency Principle: Assume total work to be the LCM of individual days[cite: 2, 3, 21]. The daily efficiency is $\frac{\text{Total Units}}{\text{Days}}$[cite: 2, 3, 21]. For leaks or outlet pipes, efficiency is considered negative[cite: 2, 4].

MDH Work Equating Formula
$$\frac{M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2}{W_2}$$
Leak Emptying Time
$$T_{\text{leak}} = \frac{T_{\text{in}} \times T_{\text{total}}}{T_{\text{total}} - T_{\text{in}}}$$
Person Leaving Before Completion: If person $A$ leaves $k$ days before scheduled completion, add $A$'s $k$ days of work to the Total Work, and divide by the combined efficiency of all workers[cite: 3, 21].
PYQ #1461[cite: 2] PSSSB Anganwadi Supervisor
A and B individually complete a job in 20 days and 25 days respectively. A left after working together for 5 days. How many days will B take to complete the remaining work?[cite: 2]
A) 13 3/4 days B) 12 1/2 days C) 11 1/2 days D) 13 1/3 days[cite: 2]
Step 1: Total Work $= \text{LCM}(20, 25) = 100\text{ units}$[cite: 2].
Efficiency of A $= 5\text{ units/day}$, Efficiency of B $= 4\text{ units/day}$[cite: 2].
Step 2: Work done in 5 days together $= 5 \times (5 + 4) = 45\text{ units}$[cite: 2].
Step 3: Remaining Work $= 100 - 45 = 55\text{ units}$[cite: 2].
$$\text{Time taken by B} = \frac{55}{4} = 13\frac{3}{4}\text{ days}$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 2]
PYQ #1462[cite: 2] PSSSB Anganwadi Supervisor
A pump can fill a tank in 2 hours. Because of a leak, it was taking $2\frac{1}{3}$ hours to fill the tank. In how many hours can the leak drain all the water?[cite: 2]
A) 8 hours B) 7 hours C) 4 1/3 hours D) 14 hours[cite: 2]
Formula: $T_{\text{drain}} = \frac{T_1 \times T_2}{T_2 - T_1} = \frac{2 \times \frac{7}{3}}{\frac{7}{3} - 2} = \frac{14/3}{1/3} = 14\text{ hours}$[cite: 2].
Correct Answer: D[cite: 2]
PYQ #1677[cite: 17] PSSSB Clerk
If 12 men or 20 women can do a piece of work in 54 days, in how many days can 9 men and 12 women together do the work?[cite: 17]
A) 40 B) 33 C) 45 D) 56[cite: 17]
Equating Efficiencies: $12M = 20W \implies 3M = 5W \implies M = \frac{5}{3}W$[cite: 17].
Convert team into Women: $9M + 12W = 9\left(\frac{5}{3}W\right) + 12W = 15W + 12W = 27W$[cite: 17].
Using $M_1 D_1 = M_2 D_2$:
$$20 \times 54 = 27 \times D_2 \implies D_2 = \frac{20 \times 54}{27} = 40\text{ days}$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 17]
09
Speed, Distance, Trains & Boats

Boats & Streams: If boat speed in still water is $u$ and stream speed is $v$, then downstream speed is $D = u + v$ and upstream speed is $U = u - v$[cite: 2, 4, 10]. Therefore, $u = \frac{D + U}{2}$ and $v = \frac{D - U}{2}$[cite: 10].

Late / Early Arrival Distance Formula
$$\text{Distance} = \frac{S_1 \times S_2}{|S_1 - S_2|} \times \left(\frac{\Delta t_{\text{minutes}}}{60}\right)$$
Train Crossing Platform
$$T = \frac{L_{\text{train}} + L_{\text{platform}}}{\text{Speed of Train}}$$
Harmonic Intermediate Speed (PYQ #1749): If walking at $10\text{ km/h}$ takes you to $1\text{ PM}$ and $15\text{ km/h}$ gets you there at $11\text{ AM}$, the speed required to reach exactly at the midpoint ($12\text{ Noon}$) is the Harmonic Mean: $$S = \frac{2 \times 10 \times 15}{10 + 15} = \frac{300}{25} = 12\text{ km/h}$$[cite: 22, 23]
PYQ #1460[cite: 2] PSSSB Anganwadi Supervisor
A person walks at 5 km/h and misses the train by 7 minutes. If he walks at 6 km/h, he reaches the station 5 minutes before the train arrives. What is the distance covered?[cite: 2]
A) 6 km B) 7 km C) 9 km D) 10 km[cite: 2]
Formula Application: $\Delta t = 7\text{ min late} + 5\text{ min early} = 12\text{ minutes}$[cite: 2].
$$\text{Distance} = \frac{S_1 S_2}{S_2 - S_1} \times \frac{\Delta t}{60} = \frac{5 \times 6}{6 - 5} \times \frac{12}{60} = 30 \times \frac{1}{5} = 6\text{ km}$$ Correct Answer: A[cite: 2]
PYQ #1582[cite: 10] PSSSB Patwari
A train of length 350 m crosses a bridge of length 250 m in 20 seconds. What is the speed of the train in km/hr?[cite: 10]
A) 95 B) 72 C) 108 D) 88[cite: 10]
Step 1: Total distance $= 350 + 250 = 600\text{ m}$[cite: 10].
