1. Union & Its Territory (Part I) Statehood & Nomenclature
Article 3 Part I: Articles 1–4
State Renaming: Formal Transition of "Kerala" to "Keralam" [cite: 2]
  • Executive Action: The Union Cabinet approved the proposal to alter the name of Kerala to "Keralam", and the President referred the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 to the State Legislature [cite: 2].
  • Constitutional Mechanism: Under the proviso to Article 3, any bill altering state names, areas, or boundaries can only be introduced in Parliament with the prior recommendation of the President [cite: 2].
  • Non-Binding State Views: The President must refer the bill to the concerned State Legislature to ascertain its opinion within a stipulated time [cite: 2]. However, the views expressed by the State Assembly are purely consultative and not binding upon Parliament.
  • Majority Requirement: Under Article 4, such legislation is enacted by Parliament using a Simple Majority and is not classified as an amendment under Article 368.
2. Fundamental Rights (Part III) Judicial Expansion & Equality
Articles 14, 15 & 16 Right to Equality
Open Competition Quota, OBC Creamy Layer, and Judicial Eligibility Criteria [cite: 1, 3, 5]
  • Merit on Unreserved Seats: The Supreme Court ruled that unreserved seats are open to all meritorious candidates and cannot be treated as a quota for the General Category alone [cite: 1]. Reserved category candidates (SC/ST/OBC) qualifying purely on merit without concessions must be adjusted against unreserved positions [cite: 1].
  • Creamy Layer Determination: The Supreme Court established that parental income alone cannot determine "creamy layer" exclusion for OBC applicants in civil services recruitment [cite: 3].
  • Judicial Recruitment Bar Experience: The Supreme Court reinstated the mandatory eligibility norm of at least three years of active legal practice at the bar for candidates appearing for Civil Judge (Junior Division) examinations [cite: 5].
Article 21 • Expanded Jurisprudence Right to Life & Personal Liberty
Landmark Orders Expanding Personal Liberty, Health, Mobility, and Bodily Autonomy [cite: 1, 3, 4, 5, 6]
  • Right to Menstrual Health: In Dr. Jaya Thakur v. Union of India, the Supreme Court declared access to menstrual hygiene an essential facet of the Right to Life with Dignity under Article 21, directing all government and private schools to supply free sanitary napkins and separate functional toilets [cite: 1].
  • Bodily Integrity & Reproductive Autonomy: Overriding the gestational cap of the MTP Act, the Supreme Court allowed a 15-year-old minor to terminate a 31-week pregnancy, ruling that reproductive decision-making is protected under Article 21 ("No individual, especially a minor, can be forced to sustain an unwanted pregnancy") [cite: 4].
  • Commuter Safety & Highway Protection: The Supreme Court declared that protection against avoidable highway accidents and the right to safe travel on public roads fall within the purview of the Fundamental Right to Life [cite: 4].
  • Pedestrian Primacy: The Supreme Court ruled that the freedom of citizens to walk on unobstructed, maintained footpaths is an actionable fundamental right that takes priority over vehicular convenience [cite: 6].
  • Right to Digital Access: Expanding Article 21, the Supreme Court mandated user-friendly accessibility modifications in digital KYC frameworks, affirming that digital empowerment is essential for persons with disabilities [cite: 5].
  • Right to Die with Dignity: In the Harish Rana case, the Supreme Court allowed passive euthanasia through the withdrawal of artificial life support, reaffirming that the right to die with dignity is an integral facet of Article 21 [cite: 3].
  • Gender Self-Determination: Under the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment framework, Parliament strengthened identity protections stemming from the landmark NALSA (2014) doctrine rooted in Article 21 [cite: 3].
Statutory Privacy vs. Article 21 RTI Act & Privacy Limits
Delhi High Court Pronouncement on PM CARES Fund [cite: 1]
  • Ruling: The Delhi High Court observed that while the PM CARES Fund operates with public elements, it cannot be stripped of privacy protections regarding third-party donor disclosures under the RTI Act [cite: 1].
  • Legal Principle: The court clarified that such confidentiality is a statutory right provided under the RTI framework, distinct from the fundamental right to privacy recognized under Article 21 in K.S. Puttaswamy (2017) [cite: 1].
3. Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) Governance Directives
Article 39A Part IV: Articles 36–51
Equal Justice, Free Legal Aid & Institutional Expansion [cite: 4, 5]
  • Institutional Leadership: Supreme Court Judge Justice Surya Kant was appointed Executive Chairman of the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) [cite: 5].
  • Digital Legal Aid: Launch of the voice-first multimodal AI assistant Nyaya Setu (mascot: Dishika) under the DISHA scheme [cite: 4].
  • Constitutional Mandate: Inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, Article 39A obligates the State to secure justice across society by offering free legal services so opportunities are not denied due to economic or physical constraints.
Article 44 Directive Principles
Uniform Civil Code: State Legislative Rollouts & Drafting Panels [cite: 3, 4, 6]
  • Legislative Milestone: Gujarat enacted the Uniform Civil Code Bill, 2026, becoming the second state in India after Uttarakhand to legislate a state-level UCC [cite: 3].
  • Drafting Committees: Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, and Rajasthan constituted state panels headed by retired Supreme Court Judge Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai to formulate draft UCC frameworks [cite: 4, 6].
  • Constitutional Scope: Article 44 provides that the State shall endeavour to secure for all citizens a Uniform Civil Code across India, unifying diverse personal laws on marriage, divorce, adoption, maintenance, and inheritance [cite: 3].
4. The Union Executive, Parliament & Judiciary (Part V) Institutional Checks & Procedures
Article 87 Parliamentary Procedures
Presidential Address and the Motion of Thanks Debate [cite: 2]
  • Procedure: Parliament commenced its session with a Motion of Thanks debate following President Droupadi Murmu's joint address [cite: 2].
  • Constitutional Provision: Under Article 87(1), the President must address both Houses assembled together at the beginning of the first session after a general election and at the first session of each calendar year (the Budget Session).
  • Significance: The Motion of Thanks must be passed by the Lok Sabha; its rejection is equivalent to a vote of no-confidence against the ruling government.
Articles 93, 94(c) & 96 Presiding Officers
Notice for the Removal / No-Confidence Motion against the Speaker [cite: 2]
  • Context: Opposition parties submitted a formal removal notice against Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla [cite: 2].
  • Removal Mechanism: Under Article 94(c), the Speaker can be removed by a resolution passed by an Effective Majority (a majority of all the then members of the House, taking total seats minus vacancies) [cite: 2].
  • Mandatory Conditions: Requires at least 14 days' advance notice. Under Article 96, while a removal resolution is under consideration, the Speaker cannot preside over the House, though they retain the right to speak, participate, and cast an initial vote (but no casting vote in a tie).
Articles 105 & 194 Legislative Powers
Privilege Notice against the Leader of Opposition (LoP) [cite: 2]
  • Parliamentary Action: A formal notice for breach of privilege was initiated against the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha [cite: 2].
  • Privileges Defined: Article 105 (for Parliament) and Article 194 (for State Assemblies) confer collective and individual immunities—such as freedom of speech in the House and exemption from judicial proceedings for parliamentary statements/votes—to enable legislators to discharge their duties without outside pressure [cite: 2].
Article 102(2) • Tenth Schedule Anti-Defection Law
Two-Thirds Merger Rule and Political Defections in the Upper House [cite: 4]
  • Current Development: Seven Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) Rajya Sabha MPs defected to the BJP, avoiding disqualification under party merger exemptions [cite: 4].
  • Merger Exemption: Inserted via the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985 and amended by the 91st Amendment Act, 2003, the Tenth Schedule protects members from disqualification if not less than two-thirds of the party's elected legislators agree to a merger with another party [cite: 4].
Article 114 Financial Legislation
Passage of the Appropriation Bill, 2026 [cite: 3]
  • Legislative Mandate: Parliament passed the Appropriation Bill, 2026 following the completion of demands for grants [cite: 3].
  • Constitutional Bar: Under Article 114, no money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266) except under an appropriation law enacted by Parliament [cite: 3].
Articles 124–128 Union Judiciary
Appointment of Justice B.R. Gavai as the 52nd Chief Justice of India [cite: 5]
  • Historic Milestone: Justice Bhushan Ramkrishna Gavai was sworn in by the President as the 52nd Chief Justice of India, becoming the first Buddhist CJI in Indian history [cite: 5].
  • Appointment & Tenure: CJI appointments are made by the President under Article 124(2) following the Memorandum of Procedure (MoP). Supreme Court judges serve until reaching the age of 65 years.
Article 143 Supreme Court Jurisdiction
Presidential Reference on Governor Timelines for State Bills [cite: 5]
  • Constitutional Reference: President Droupadi Murmu invoked Article 143 to seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court regarding judicial attempts to set rigid timelines for Governors and the President to clear State Bills [cite: 5].
  • Advisory Nature: Under Article 143(1), the President may refer questions of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court [cite: 5]. The Supreme Court's opinion is advisory and not binding upon the President.
5. State Executives & High Courts (Part VI) Governors, Assent & Judicial Administration
Articles 200 & 201 State Legislative Powers
Gubernatorial Assent to the Punjab Sacrilege Satkar Amendment Bill [cite: 4]
  • Punjab Development: Punjab Governor Gulab Chand Kataria granted assent to the Jaagat Jot Sri Guru Granth Sahib Satkar (Amendment) Bill, 2026, providing for enhanced penalties up to life imprisonment and ₹5–25 lakh fines for sacrilege [cite: 4].
  • Constitutional Alternatives: Under Article 200, when a Bill passed by the State Legislature is presented to the Governor, the Governor can: (1) Assent, (2) Withhold assent, (3) Return the Bill for reconsideration (non-money bills), or (4) Reserve the Bill for the President's consideration under Article 201.
Articles 214–217 & 222 High Court Benches
High Court Chief Justice Inductions, Firsts, and Judicial Transfers [cite: 1, 4, 5, 6]
  • Historic Firsts: Justice Lisa Gill was appointed as the first woman Chief Justice of the Andhra Pradesh High Court [cite: 4], and Justice Revati Mohite Dere was sworn in as the first woman Chief Justice of the Meghalaya High Court [cite: 1]. Justice Meenakshi Madan Rai became Chief Justice of the Patna High Court [cite: 6].
  • Judicial Transfers: The Supreme Court Collegium recommended transfers of Chief Justices across Rajasthan, Tripura, Jharkhand, and Madras High Courts under Article 222, which empowers the President to transfer a judge from one High Court to another after consulting the CJI [cite: 5].
  • Judge Pensions: The Supreme Court upheld "One Rank One Pension" for all retired High Court judges under a bench led by CJI B.R. Gavai [cite: 5].
6. Local Governance (Part IX & IX-A) Tribal Self-Rule & Local Body Reforms
Article 243M • PESA Act, 1996 Tribal Self-Governance
Jharkhand Notifies PESA Rules 25 Years Post-Statehood [cite: 1]
  • State Implementation: Jharkhand formally notified its PESA Rules, extending statutory local governance to its Fifth Schedule tribal regions [cite: 1].
  • Legal Mechanism: Part IX of the Constitution does not apply automatically to Fifth Schedule areas (Article 243M). Parliament enacted the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA) based on the Bhuria Committee recommendations, recognizing the Gram Sabha as the competent authority over local resources, customary dispute resolution, minor minerals, and tribal land transfers [cite: 1].
Articles 243F & 243V Panchayat & Urban Elections
Repeal of the "Two-Child Disqualification" for Local Body Polls [cite: 1, 3, 6]
  • State Reforms: Telangana repealed its 1994 statutory disqualification barring persons with more than two children from contesting local body elections via the Telangana Panchayat Raj (Amendment) Bill, 2026 [cite: 1]. Rajasthan similarly removed the two-child restriction for Urban Local Body (ULB) polls and repealed leprosy disqualifications [cite: 3]. Madhya Pradesh also ended the two-child restriction for state government recruitment [cite: 6].
  • Constitutional Basis: Articles 243F (Panchayats) and 243V (Municipalities) grant State Legislatures full authority to define qualifications and disqualifications for local elected offices.
7. Constitutional Commissions & Special Categories (Part XVI) Safeguards & Classification
Articles 338, 338A & 338B Constitutional Commissions
Appointments and Milestones Across NCSC, NCST, and NCBC [cite: 2, 3, 5]
  • National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338): Dr. Partha Biswas took charge as Member [cite: 3].
  • National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A): Celebrated its 23rd Foundation Day on 19 February 2026 [cite: 2]; established via the 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 (operationalized in 2004) [cite: 2].
  • National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B): Sadhvi Niranjan Jyoti assumed office as Chairperson [cite: 3], presenting annual reports for 2022–2024 to the President [cite: 5]; granted constitutional standing through the 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018.
  • Powers: All three commissions hold the investigatory powers of a Civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC) when examining complaints.
Articles 341 & 342 Special Provisions
Religious Criteria for SC Status and Tribal Quota Adjudication [cite: 1, 3]
  • Scheduled Castes Religious Criteria (Article 341): The Supreme Court affirmed that under the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950, individuals professing religions other than Hinduism, Sikhism, or Buddhism cannot be classified as Scheduled Castes [cite: 3].
  • Scheduled Tribes Notification (Article 342): The Supreme Court was moved regarding the ST status of the Lambadi / Sugali community in Telangana [cite: 1]. Under Article 342, modifying the Scheduled Tribes list requires an Act of Parliament.
8. Finance, Elections & Emergency Frameworks Institutional Pillars
Article 280 Finance Commission
Submission of the 16th Finance Commission Final Report (2026–2031) [cite: 3]
  • Award Period: The 16th Finance Commission, chaired by Dr. Arvind Panagariya, submitted its final report governing fiscal devolutions for the 2026–2031 cycle [cite: 3].
  • Constitutional Function: Constituted by the President under Article 280 every 5 years, the Commission recommends: (1) Vertical tax distribution between Union and States, (2) Horizontal allocation among states, and (3) Grants-in-aid principles under Article 275.
Article 324(5) • CEC Removal Elections (Part XV)
Removal Protections for the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) [cite: 3]
  • Parliamentary Action: Opposition parties initiated moves regarding an impeachment motion against CEC Gyanesh Kumar [cite: 3].
  • Removal Safeguard: Under the proviso to Article 324(5), the Chief Election Commissioner can only be removed from office in like manner and on like grounds as a Supreme Court Judge (i.e., through an address passed by both Houses of Parliament with a Special Majority on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity) [cite: 3]. Other Election Commissioners can be removed by the President upon the recommendation of the CEC.
Articles 352 & 356 Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII)
President's Rule in Manipur and Emergency Retrospectives [cite: 2, 6]
  • President's Rule (Article 356): Imposed in Manipur following the Chief Minister's resignation, with the State Assembly placed under suspended animation (not dissolved) and later revoked by President Droupadi Murmu [cite: 2]. Under S.R. Bommai (1994) guidelines, the legislative assembly cannot be dissolved until Parliament formally approves the proclamation within two months.
  • National Emergency (Article 352): June 25 was observed as Samvidhaan Hatya Diwas, and NCERT introduced chapters examining the 1975 Emergency [cite: 6]. The 44th Amendment Act, 1978 restricted internal emergency declarations strictly to cases of "armed rebellion" and prohibited the suspension of Articles 20 and 21 under Article 359 during any emergency proclamation.
