⭐ 1. October 2026: Constitutional & Polity Developments

October Week 1, 2026
  • People's Plan Campaign 2026–27 & GPDP Formulation Article 243G / 11th Schedule

    Summary: Launched nationwide on October 2 under the campaign theme “Sabki Yojana, Sabka Vikas”. It mandates Gram Panchayats across India to formulate annual Gram Panchayat Development Plans (GPDPs) for FY 2027–28 by exercising decentralized local planning powers devolved under Article 243G and the Eleventh Schedule of the Constitution.

  • Assam Muslim Marriage Registration (Compulsory) Rules, 2026 Article 44 / Concurrent List Entry 5

    Summary: The Assam Cabinet notified the statutory compulsory registration rules under the Assam Repealing Act 2024, officially ending the colonial-era Assam Moslem Marriages and Divorces Registration Act of 1935. All marriages and divorces are now brought under mandatory government registration machinery to curtail child marriages and align with personal law reforms.

  • Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) Centenary ‘Manthan’ Articles 315–323 (Part XIV)

    Summary: The UPSC celebrated 100 years of establishment (set up on October 1, 1926 as the Public Service Commission on recommendations of the 1924 Lee Commission). Commemorated with a national conference titled ‘Manthan’ at Bharat Mandapam, examining the constitutional role of the independent central recruiting agency under Articles 315 to 323.

  • Statutory Mega Port Notification for Paradip Port Indian Ports Act, 2025 (Section 73) / Union List Entry 27

    Summary: Paradip Port was formally designated as a statutory “Mega Port” under Section 73 of the newly enacted Indian Ports Act, 2025, vesting it with specialized administrative jurisdiction and fast-tracked statutory clearances for deep-water berths.

⭐ 2. September 2026: Constitutional & Polity Developments

September 2026
  • Proposed Article 371(K) for Union Territory of Ladakh Part XXI / Article 371(K)

    Summary: The Ministry of Home Affairs tabled a constitutional proposal to insert Article 371(K) into Part XXI of the Constitution. It seeks to grant Ladakh a directly elected legislature with exclusive legislative competence over local land rights, transfer of agricultural properties, cultural heritage, and environmental safeguards—modelled on special provisions in Article 371A (Nagaland) and Article 371G (Mizoram).

  • Supreme Court Directives on EVM Totaliser Machines Article 324 / Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961

    Summary: A three-judge Supreme Court bench presided over by Chief Justice Surya Kant directed the Union Government and the Election Commission of India (ECI) to articulate a definitive timeline regarding the introduction of 'Totaliser' machines. Totalisers aggregate votes across ~14 EVMs simultaneously prior to declaration, preventing booth-wise disclosure of voting trends and protecting voter anonymity.

  • ECI Special Intensive Revision (SIR) Electoral Purge Article 326 / Representation of the People Act, 1950

    Summary: The Election Commission conducted a pan-India Special Intensive Revision (SIR) purging over 13 crore duplicate, expired, and unverified entries from draft voter rolls across 30 States and Union Territories to enforce the universal adult suffrage mandate and clean voter lists.

  • En-Masse High Court Chief Justices Appointments Article 217

    Summary: The President appointed Chief Justices across multiple High Courts under Article 217 on recommendation of the SC Collegium: Justice Sanjay Kumar Agrawal (Rajasthan HC), Justice V. Kameswar Rao (Patna HC), Justice Ravindra V. Ghuge (Calcutta HC), Justice Pushpendra Singh Bhati (J&K and Ladakh HC), Justice Ashwani Kumar Mishra (Punjab and Haryana HC), and Justice Mahesh Chandra Tripathi (Bombay HC).

  • Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026 Legal Metrology Act, 2009 / Union List Entry 51

    Summary: The Ministry of Consumer Affairs notified the rules establishing Indian Standard Time (IST, UTC+5:30) as the sole legal and verifiable standard of time for all judicial, telecom, administrative, and financial transaction platforms across the country, synchronized through CSIR-NPL and NavIC.

  • Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) Reconstitution Cinematograph Act, 1952 / Article 19(2)

    Summary: The Union Government reconstituted the statutory CBFC board for a three-year term under the Cinematograph Act, 1952 (incorporating recent Cinematograph Amendment provisions for age-based classification U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+, and anti-piracy mechanisms within reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2)).

⭐ 3. July – August 2026: Constitutional & Polity Developments

July – August 2026
  • Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 Article 124(1)

    Summary: Introduced in Parliament under Article 124(1) to increase the statutory sanctioned judge strength of the Supreme Court of India from 33 to 37 judges (excluding the Chief Justice of India, taking total sanctioned strength to 38) to address mounting constitutional bench pendency.

  • Parliament Monsoon Session & Rajya Sabha Rule 267 Invocation Article 85(1) / RS Rules of Procedure Rule 267

    Summary: The Monsoon Session convened fulfilling the constitutional mandate under Article 85(1) that six months shall not intervene between the last sitting in one session and the date appointed for the first sitting in the next session. The opposition invoked Rule 267 seeking total suspension of listed parliamentary business to debate national paper leak crises.

  • Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 Concurrent List Entry 25

    Summary: Enacted to enhance statutory penalties for paper leaks and organized cheating syndicates. Hikes maximum imprisonment terms from 5 to 10 years, institutes mandatory Special Fast Track Courts, and implements high-tech auditing protocols designed by a task force chaired by Nandan Nilekani.

  • Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 Entry 54 List I vs Entry 50 List II / Federal Taxation

    Summary: Passed by Parliament to resolve jurisdictional ambiguity arising from Supreme Court rulings on state mineral taxation. The statutory amendment expressly curtails state governments from imposing unilateral parallel cess and royalties on mineral rights and mineral-bearing lands.

  • The Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026 BSA, 2023 / Indian Evidence Framework

    Summary: Repealed the archaic colonial Bankers' Books Evidence Act of 1891. Establishes legal admissibility standards for encrypted electronic financial ledgers, cloud banking architectures, and digital transaction logs as primary legal evidence in courts of law.

  • Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026 & National Tribunals Commission (NTC) Articles 323A & 323B / Judicial Independence

    Summary: Replaces the 2021 enactment following Supreme Court scrutiny. Creates an overarching statutory authority—the National Tribunals Commission (NTC)—to superintend appointments, service conditions, infrastructure, and performance monitoring across all central tribunals, insulating them from executive influence.

  • BNSS Section 356: Trials in Absentia Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (Sec 356) / Article 21

    Summary: Special National Investigation Agency (NIA) Courts invoked Section 356 of the BNSS to commence criminal trials against proclaimed absconding offenders and designated foreign-based terrorists (e.g., Hafiz Saeed) in absentia, creating a novel criminal jurisprudence precedent.

  • Supreme Court Re-articulates Doctrine of Per Incuriam Article 141 (Law declared by Supreme Court)

    Summary: The Supreme Court reaffirmed that judicial orders or judgments rendered in ignorance or forgetfulness of express statutory provisions or binding decisions of coordinate/larger benches are per incuriam and do not hold binding precedential value under Article 141.

  • Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026 & FCRA 2.0 Article 19(1)(c) / Union List Entry 10

    Summary: The Ministry of Home Affairs introduced legislation establishing a structured receiver and asset-disposal mechanism for property, premises, and foreign contributions belonging to non-governmental organisations whose FCRA licenses have been cancelled for statutory non-compliance.

  • Legal Personhood Accorded to River Tamirabharani Articles 21, 48A & 51A(g) / Environmental Jurisprudence

    Summary: The Madras High Court (Madurai Bench) invoked the doctrine of parens patriae to confer the status of a 'Living Entity / Legal Person' with all corresponding fundamental rights and duties upon Tamil Nadu's perennial River Tamirabharani (Porunai) to halt unchecked industrial effluents.

  • Madhya Pradesh Uniform Civil Code (UCC) Bill, 2026 Article 44 (Directive Principles of State Policy)

    Summary: Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly passed the state Uniform Civil Code Bill, making MP the 4th Indian state (after Uttarakhand, Gujarat, and Assam) to adopt a uniform legal framework governing civil registration, inheritance, divorce, and live-in relationships while exempting Scheduled Tribes.

  • VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 Enters into Force Articles 39(a), 41 & 43 / Welfare Legislation

    Summary: Rollout of the Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Aajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 across all states, formally repealing the MGNREGA 2005. Increases statutory guarantee of wage employment to 125 days per household, resets the cost-sharing ratio between Centre and States to 60:40 (90:10 in Special Category States), and disbursed a mother sanction of ₹25,863 crore.

⭐ 4. April – June 2026: Constitutional & Polity Developments

April – June 2026
  • Historic Defeat of Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill in Lok Sabha Articles 82, 334A & 368(2)

    Summary: The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill failed to secure the mandatory two-thirds special majority of members present and voting in Lok Sabha (298 in favor, 200 against; short of 2/3rd majority). The Bill attempted to decouple the 33% Women's Reservation (enacted under the 106th CAA) from post-2026 delimitation by amending Articles 82 and 334A to operationalize quotas using 2011 census demographics. This was the first defeat of a Constitutional Amendment Bill on the floor of the House in 12 years.

  • Delimitation Bill, 2026 Tabled in Parliament Articles 81, 82, 170 & 327

    Summary: Proposed repealing the Delimitation Act, 2002 to initiate post-freeze electoral boundary reallocation. It proposes expanding the total strength of the Lok Sabha to 850 members (815 representing States and 35 representing Union Territories). Reaffirmed that once orders of the Delimitation Commission are published under Article 327, they acquire the force of law and cannot be questioned in any court.

  • Assent to Jaagat Jot Sri Guru Granth Sahib Satkar (Amendment) Bill Article 200 (Governor's Assent) / IPC/BNS State Amendment

    Summary: Punjab Governor Gulab Chand Kataria granted assent under Article 200. The Act prescribes a minimum 7 years (up to 20 years) rigorous imprisonment for acts of sacrilege against Sri Guru Granth Sahib, and 10 years to Life Imprisonment if committed under criminal conspiracy to disrupt communal harmony. It introduced the statutory definition of "Custodian" and made offences cognizable, non-bailable, and triable by Court of Session.

  • Supreme Court Landmark Article 21 Enforcements Article 21 (Right to Life & Personal Liberty)

    Summary:
    • Right to Walk on Footpaths: The Supreme Court recognized pedestrians' right to walk safely on encroached footpaths as an enforceable Fundamental Right under Article 21, taking precedence over motorized carriage space.
    • Safe Highway Transit: Ruled that the State owes a positive constitutional duty to guarantee hazard-free transit on national highways, directing immediate removal of unlicensed commercial establishments from highway rights-of-way.
    • Reproductive Autonomy: Affirmed a minor's constitutional right to reproductive choices by permitting the medical termination of a 31-week pregnancy on mental health grounds, reading Article 21 over statutory caps under the MTP Act.

  • Tenth Schedule & Anti-Defection Jurisprudence 10th Schedule / Articles 102(2) & 191(2)

    Summary: Apex court revisited the 2/3rd merger exemption under paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule (fortified via 91st CAA 2003 which deleted the 1/3rd split rule). Re-emphasized that the Chairman/Speaker functions as a constitutional tribunal whose decisions are subject to judicial review only post-adjudication (Kihoto Hollohan doctrine).

  • Parliamentary & Legislative Assembly Privileges Re-examined Articles 105 & 194

    Summary: Detailed rulings on parliamentary immunity clarified that protection from civil arrest extends 40 days before, during, and 40 days after sessions, but does not extend to criminal proceedings or preventive detention. Confirmed the exclusive institutional privilege of houses to regulate internal proceedings and discipline strangers.

  • Constitutional Sequence in Hung Legislative Assemblies Articles 163, 164 & 174

    Summary: In the wake of regional assembly elections, the judiciary affirmed the discretionary guidelines established by the Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions for Governors appointing a Chief Minister in a fractured mandate: (1) Pre-poll alliance, (2) Single largest party staking claim, (3) Post-poll coalition, requiring a mandatory floor test within 30 days.

  • Statutory Protection Extended to National Song "Vande Mataram" Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971 / Article 51A(a)

    Summary: Parliament introduced an amendment bill incorporating the National Song "Vande Mataram" directly into Section 3 of the 1971 Act alongside the National Flag and National Anthem, prescribing penal sanctions of up to 3 years imprisonment for deliberate disruption or insult.

  • Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026 Ease of Doing Business / Decriminalization

    Summary: Decriminalized 717 statutory provisions across 79 Central Acts, converting minor procedural and administrative defaults from criminal misdemeanors punishable by imprisonment into civil monetary penalties adjudicated by designated administrative officers.

  • Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan (VBSA) Bill Union List Entry 66 / Article 246

    Summary: Introduced to repeal and replace fragmented regulators (University Grants Commission, AICTE, and NCTE) with a single overarching statutory regulatory body—the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan—implementing the Higher Education Commission of India (HECI) mandate under NEP 2020.

  • Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) Amendment Bill, 2026 Concurrent List Entry 9 / Commercial Jurisprudence

    Summary: Overhauled the 2016 Code by introducing Creditor-Initiated Insolvency Resolution Processes (CIIRP), formal cross-border insolvency provisions based on UNCITRAL model law, group insolvency frameworks, and strict 14-day statutory limits for NCLT admission benches.

⭐ 5. January – March 2026: Constitutional & Polity Developments

January – March 2026
  • State Name Alteration: Kerala to ‘Keralam’ Article 3 (Proviso) & Article 4

    Summary: The Union Cabinet cleared the referral of the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 to the State Legislature of Kerala pursuant to the proviso to Article 3. Under the constitutional mechanism, following state assembly recommendations, Parliament may rename the state in the First Schedule by a simple majority vote under Article 4.

  • Menstrual Hygiene Declared a Fundamental Right Articles 21 & 21A (Dr. Jaya Thakur vs Union of India)

    Summary: The Supreme Court delivered a historic judgment ruling that access to menstrual health management facilities, sanitary pads, and clean separate toilets in educational institutions constitutes an intrinsic component of the Right to Dignified Life (Article 21) and the Right to Education (Article 21A). Mandated free sanitary napkins for all adolescent girls in government and aided schools across classes 6 to 12.

  • Withdrawal of Life Support & Passive Euthanasia Protocols Article 21 (Harish Rana Case)

    Summary: In the Harish Rana case, the Supreme Court simplified the implementation guidelines laid down in Aruna Shanbaug (2011) and Common Cause (2018). Reaffirmed that the right to die with dignity is an inseparable dimension of Article 21, approving hospital-level primary and secondary medical board protocols for withdrawing Clinically Assisted Nutrition and Hydration (CANH) in permanently vegetative patients.

  • Resolution for Removal of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha Articles 94(c), 96 & Rule 200

    Summary: A constitutional motion was submitted seeking the removal of Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla under Article 94(c). The procedural prerequisites mandate 14 days prior notice, support of at least 50 MPs on the floor of the House for leave to admit, and passage by an absolute majority (majority of all the then members of the House) with the Speaker prohibited from presiding under Article 96.

  • Inter-State Water Dispute: Pennaiyar River Tribunal Article 262 / Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956

    Summary: The Supreme Court issued a writ directing the Union Government to formally constitute an Inter-State Water Dispute Tribunal under Article 262 to adjudicate the long-pending water sharing and diversion dispute over the Pennaiyar River between Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, barring high court and supreme court jurisdiction under Section 11 of the 1956 Act.

  • Removal Safeguards for Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Article 324(5) & Article 124(4)

    Summary: Judicial proceedings affirmed that under the constitutional guarantee of Article 324(5), the Chief Election Commissioner cannot be removed from office except in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court (requiring an address supported by a 2/3rd special majority in each House of Parliament on proven misbehaviour or incapacity under Article 124(4)).

  • Presidential Address & Union Budget Presentation Articles 87, 112 & 114

    Summary: President Droupadi Murmu addressed the special joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament under Article 87 at the commencement of the Budget Session. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman presented the Annual Financial Statement under Article 112, culminating in the statutory enactment of the Appropriation Act under Article 114 to authorize withdrawals from the Consolidated Fund of India.

  • 16th Finance Commission Devolution Architecture Article 280 (Finance Commission)

    Summary: The 16th Finance Commission, chaired by Dr. Arvind Panagariya, retained the vertical tax devolution share to states at 41%. It introduced an updated horizontal devolution formula incorporating a 10% weightage for Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) fiscal contributions, while reforming Article 275 post-devolution revenue deficit grants.

  • Reservation & Creamy Layer Jurisprudence Articles 15(4), 16(4) & 341

    Summary:
    • Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950: The Supreme Court reaffirmed that Scheduled Caste status is constitutionally restricted to professing Hindu, Sikh, or Buddhist faiths; individuals converting to Christianity or Islam cannot claim protection under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act.
    • Creamy Layer Determinants: Ruled that parental agricultural income and basic government salary cannot be made the exclusive or rigid criteria to classify candidates within the OBC creamy layer without assessing broader socio-economic prosperity.
    • Unreserved Merit Quota: Held that unreserved open seats in public employment must remain completely open to all categories purely based on merit, prohibiting the segregation of reserved category candidates scoring qualifying marks from open seats.

  • Repeal of the Two-Child Disqualification Norm in Local Bodies Part IX & IX-A (Panchayats & Municipalities)

    Summary: The State Legislatures of Telangana and Rajasthan repealed the statutory two-child disqualification norm for contesting Panchayat and Urban Local Body elections, removing barriers preventing parents with more than two children from exercising local democratic franchise.

  • National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) Foundation Article 338A (89th CAA, 2003)

    Summary: The statutory and constitutional watchdog body commemorated its 23rd Foundation Day, reviewing its civil court powers under Clause (8) of Article 338A to inquire into specific complaints regarding the deprivation of rights and safeguards guaranteed to Scheduled Tribes.
