Schedules of the Constitution of India High Yield
  1. First Schedule: Lists the States and Union Territories with territorial extent.
  2. Second Schedule: Salaries, allowances, and emoluments of President, Governors, Chief Justice & Judges of SC/HCs, CAG, etc.
  3. Third Schedule: Forms of Oaths or Affirmations for Union/State Ministers, MPs, MLAs, Judges of SC/HCs, CAG.
  4. Fourth Schedule: Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha to States and Union Territories.
  5. Fifth Schedule: Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes.
  6. Sixth Schedule: Administration of Tribal Areas in 4 Northeastern states: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram (AMTM).
  7. Seventh Schedule: Division of legislative powers via three lists:
    • Union List: 97 original → 100 subjects.
    • State List: 66 original → 61 subjects.
    • Concurrent List: 47 original → 52 subjects.
  8. Eighth Schedule: Official recognized languages. Originally 14, currently 22 languages (Sindhi by 21st Amdt; Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali by 71st Amdt; Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali by 92nd Amdt).
  9. Ninth Schedule: Laws immune from judicial review (primarily land/agrarian reforms). Added by 1st Amendment, 1951. (Subject to review post-1973 per I.R. Coelho).
  10. Tenth Schedule: Anti-Defection Law. Added by 52nd Amendment, 1985. Disqualification on defection grounds.
  11. Eleventh Schedule: Powers, authority, and responsibilities of Panchayats. Contains 29 subjects. Added by 73rd Amendment, 1992.
  12. Twelfth Schedule: Powers, authority, and responsibilities of Municipalities. Contains 18 subjects. Added by 74th Amendment, 1992.
Part I: The Union and its Territory (Articles 1–4) 🦁 Punjab & PEPSU History
Article 1: India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States; specifies names and territories.
Article 2: Admission or establishment of new states (territory not part of India).
Article 3: Formation of new states, alteration of areas, boundaries, or names of existing states.
Article 4: Laws made under Articles 2 & 3 are not considered constitutional amendments under Article 368. ("368 di lod nhi, Schedule 1 and 4 ch change krna sirf").
Key Integration & Historic Facts:
  • Total Princely States: 552 located within geographical boundary of India; 549 joined voluntarily.
  • 3 States initially reluctant:
    1. Jammu & Kashmir: Maharaja Hari Singh signed Instrument of Accession (IOA) on 26 Oct 1947.
    2. Junagadh: Merged via Plebiscite (referendum).
    3. Hyderabad: Mir Osman Ali Khan; merged through military action Operation Polo in September 1948.
  • First to sign IOA: Travancore.
  • Last moment accession: Jodhpur.
  • 1950 4-Fold Classification of States:
    • Part A: Governed by elected British Governor (e.g., Punjab).
    • Part B: Governed by Rajpramukh (e.g., PEPSU – Patiala and East Punjab States Union).
    • Part C: Governed by Chief Commissioner.
    • Part D: Appointed Lieutenant Governor (Andaman & Nicobar Islands).
    • Part ABCD system was completely abolished in 1956 by the 7th Constitutional Amendment.
  • Vishal Andhra Movement: Potti Sreeramulu went on a hunger strike demanding a Telugu-speaking state; died on 56th day (1953).
  • First Linguistic State: Andhra State formed in 1953 (Capital: Kurnool, first CM: Neelam Sanjiva Reddy). Later transformed into Andhra Pradesh in 1956 with capital at Hyderabad.
  • Foreign Enclaves: French enclaves (Pondicherry, Yanam, Karaikal, Mahe); Portuguese enclaves (Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu, Goa). Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu united as one UT on 26 January 2020.
State Reorganization Commissions
  • S.K. Dhar Commission (1948): Recommended reorganization based on administrative convenience, strictly rejected linguistic basis.
  • JVP Committee (1949): Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya. Formally rejected language-based reorganization.
  • Fazal Ali Commission / States Reorganisation Commission (1953): Members: Fazal Ali (Chairman), K.M. Panikkar, Hriday Nath Kunzru. Submitted report in 1955. Rejected "one language, one state" but accepted language as a major criterion. Recommended 16 states & 3 UTs. Resulted in States Reorganisation Act 1956 creating 14 States and 6 UTs.

Union Territories Administration Setup
  • UTs with Administrator: Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Lakshadweep.
  • UTs with Lieutenant Governor (LG): Andaman & Nicobar, Delhi (NCT), Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Puducherry.
Part II: Citizenship (Articles 5–11) PSSSB Core
Article 5: Citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution.
Article 6: Rights of citizenship for persons who migrated to India from Pakistan.
Article 7: Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan who later returned.
Article 8: Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India.
Article 9: Single Citizenship Principle: Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State automatically lose Indian citizenship.
Article 10: Continuance of the rights of citizenship.
Article 11: Parliament's Exclusive Power: Parliament has supreme authority to regulate the right of citizenship by law (Citizenship Act 1955).
Part III: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)
🔥 Top Priority 📰 Active SC Debates
Historical Anchors & Essential Doctrines:
  • Borrowed from USA (Bill of Rights 1791). Termed Magna Carta of India (Original Magna Carta: 1215 by King John of England).
  • 1931 Karachi Session: Presided by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel; Jawaharlal Nehru drafted the resolution on Fundamental Rights.
  • Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan: Termed FRs as "A pledge to our people and a pact with the civilized world."
  • Rule of Law: Propounded by British jurist A.V. Dicey (Absence of arbitrary power, equality before law, predominance of legal spirit).
Article 12: Definition of 'State' (Government, Parliament, State legislatures, local authorities, statutory bodies).
Article 13: Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of the Fundamental Rights shall be void.
  • Article 13(1) - Doctrine of Severability: If an offending clause can be separated from the valid law, only that clause is void. (Applies to pre-constitutional/past laws).
  • Article 13(2) - Doctrine of Eclipse: Post-constitutional laws violating FRs are not dead ab initio; they remain dormant/shadowed and become active if constitutional limitations are removed.
Article 14: Equality before Law (negative concept borrowed from UK) and Equal Protection of the Laws (positive concept borrowed from USA).
Article 15: Prohibition of discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
  • 15(4) & 15(5): Special provisions / reservation for SC/ST/OBC in educational institutions.
  • 15(6): 10% Reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) in educational institutions (103rd Amdt 2019).
Article 16: Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment. (16(6) added for 10% EWS reservation in jobs).
Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability and prohibition of its practice in any form. Absolute right.
Article 18: Abolition of titles (except military and academic distinctions). Bharat Ratna/Padma awards are not titles.
Article 19: Protection of 6 Fundamental Freedoms:
  1. 19(1)(a): Freedom of speech and expression (includes freedom of press, commercial speech, right to know). Electoral Bonds Verdict
  2. 19(1)(b): Freedom to assemble peaceably and without arms.
  3. 19(1)(c): Freedom to form associations, unions, or co-operative societies.
  4. 19(1)(d): Freedom to move freely throughout the territory of India.
  5. 19(1)(e): Freedom to reside and settle in any part of India.
  6. [19(1)(f) Right to acquire/hold/dispose of property — Deleted by 44th Amendment 1978].
  7. 19(1)(g): Freedom to practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade, or business.
Article 20: Protection in respect of conviction for offences:
  • 20(1) No ex-post-facto law: Cannot be punished under a retrospective criminal law.
  • 20(2) No Double Jeopardy: No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.
  • 20(3) Prohibition against Self-Incrimination: No accused shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.
Article 21: Protection of life and personal liberty. Expanded by Maneka Gandhi case (1978) to require "just, fair, and reasonable" procedure; encompasses Right to Privacy (Puttaswamy 2017), Clean Environment, Dignity, Livelihood.
Article 21A: Right to Education: Free and compulsory education to all children aged 6 to 14 years. (Added by 86th Amendment 2002).
Article 22: Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases:
  • 22(1): Right to be informed of the grounds of arrest & consult a legal practitioner.
  • 22(2): Must be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours of arrest (excluding travel time).
  • 22(3): Female arrest rules & safeguards (must be arrested by female police officers with prescribed procedure).
Article 23: Prohibition of traffic in human beings, begar, and other forced labour.
Article 24: Prohibition of employment of children below the age of 14 years in factories, mines, or hazardous employment.
Article 25: Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.
Article 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs.
Article 27: Freedom from payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion.
Article 28: Freedom from attending religious instruction in wholly State-funded educational institutions.
Article 29: Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities.
Article 30: Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions. Recognized national religious minorities: 6 communities (Sikhs, Muslims, Christians, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis).
Article 31: Repealed (Right to Property repealed by 44th Amendment Act 1978 during Morarji Desai / Janata Party Government; shifted to Article 300A).
Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies: Direct approach to Supreme Court for enforcement of FRs. Termed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution".
  • High Court writ jurisdiction lies under Article 226 (broader scope: covers FRs + ordinary legal rights).
  • Prior to 1950, only High Courts of Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras had inherent writ power.
  • Borrowed from English common law (known as Prerogative Writs).
The Five Constitutional Writs (Articles 32 & 226)
Writ Literal Meaning Scope & Application
Habeas Corpus"To have the body of"Protects against unlawful detention. Applicable against both public and private entities.
Mandamus"We Command"Issued to a public official or body commanding them to perform an obligatory public duty. Cannot be issued against President or Governors.
Prohibition"To forbid / stop"Issued by higher courts to prevent an inferior court/tribunal from exceeding its legal jurisdiction. (Preventive only).
Certiorari"To be certified"Issued to quash the order of a lower court/tribunal made without jurisdiction or in violation of natural justice. (Both preventive and curative).
Quo-Warranto"By what authority"Prevents illegal usurpation of a public substantive office. Can be sought by any interested person, not necessarily the aggrieved party.
Article 32A: Validity of State laws not to be considered in Art 32 (Inserted by 42nd Amdt, repealed by 43rd Amdt 1977).
Article 33: Power of Parliament to modify Fundamental Rights for Armed Forces, Police, and Intelligence organizations.
Article 34: Restrictions on FRs while martial law (military rule) is in force in any area.
Article 35: Parliament has the exclusive power to make laws to give effect to certain provisions of Part III (e.g., Art 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34).
Article 35A: Special rights and privileges to permanent residents of Jammu & Kashmir (Issued via Presidential Order 1954; abrogated in August 2019).
Part IV: Directive Principles & Part IV-A: Fundamental Duties
High Yield 📰 UCC Debate
Key Perspectives on DPSP:
  • Borrowed from Ireland (which borrowed from Spain).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Termed DPSP as the "Novel Feature of the Indian Constitution".
  • Prof. K.T. Shah: "DPSP is like a cheque on a bank, payable only when the resources of the bank permit."
  • Justiciability: Non-enforceable in courts of law (Art 37), but fundamental in the governance of the country.
Article 36: Definition of State (same as Article 12).
Article 37: Non-justiciable nature of DPSPs, but fundamental in governance.
Article 38: State to secure a social order for the promotion of welfare of the people.
  • Article 38(2): Minimize inequalities in income, status, facilities, and opportunities (Added by 44th Amendment 1978).
Article 39: Certain principles of policy to be followed by the State (adequate livelihood, distribution of material resources, prevention of concentration of wealth, equal pay for equal work for both men and women).
Article 39A: Equal justice and free legal aid (Added by 42nd Amendment 1976; led to Legal Services Authorities Act / NALSA).
Article 40: Organisation of Village Panchayats (Gandhian Principle). 🔥
Article 41: Right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness, and disablement.
Article 42: Provision for just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.
Article 43: Living wage and decent standard of life for workers; promotion of cottage industries.
Article 43A: Participation of workers in the management of industries (Added by 42nd Amendment 1976).
Article 43B: Promotion of voluntary formation and autonomous functioning of Co-operative Societies (Added by 97th Amendment 2011).
Article 44: Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for all citizens throughout India. 📰 Highly Active News Subject (Uttarakhand UCC implementation)
Article 45: Provision for early childhood care and education to children below the age of six years (Amended by 86th Amdt 2002).
Article 46: Promotion of educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and other weaker sections.
Article 47: Duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and the standard of living and to improve public health (prohibition of intoxicating drinks & drugs).
Article 48: Organisation of agriculture and animal husbandry (prohibiting slaughter of cows, calves, and milch cattle).
Article 48A: Protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wildlife (Added by 42nd Amendment 1976).
Article 49: Protection of monuments, places, and objects of national importance.
Article 50: Separation of Judiciary from the Executive in public services.
Article 51: Promotion of international peace and security.
Part IV-A: Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)

Recommended by Swaran Singh Committee (1976); borrowed from the USSR. Added 10 duties via 42nd Amendment 1976. 11th duty added by 86th Amendment 2002.

  1. Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
  2. Cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom.
  3. Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
  4. Defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.
  5. Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all people, transcending religious, linguistic, and regional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
  6. Value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.
  7. Protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures.
  8. Develop the scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
  9. Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
  10. Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.
  11. (k) added by 86th Amdt (2002): Duty of parent/guardian to provide opportunities for education to child between 6 and 14 years.
Part V: The Union (Articles 52–151) Executive, Parliament & Judiciary
Chapter 1: The Union Executive (President, VP, PM, Council of Ministers, AG)
Article 52: There shall be a President of India.
Article 53: Executive power of the Union vested in the President.
Article 54: Election of the President: Electoral College consists of:
  • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (MPs).
  • Elected members of Legislative Assemblies of States (MLAs).
  • Elected members of Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry (70th Amdt 1992) and J&K UT.
  • Nominated members and State Legislative Councils (MLCs) do not participate.
Article 55: Manner of election of President (System of proportional representation by means of single transferable vote & secret ballot).
Article 56: Term of office of President (5 years from entering office).
Article 57: Eligibility for re-election (No term limits in India).
Article 58: Qualifications for election as President: Citizen of India, minimum 35 years of age, qualified for election as Lok Sabha member, no office of profit.
Article 59: Conditions of President's office.
Article 60: Oath or affirmation by the President (Administered by the Chief Justice of India).
Article 61: Procedure for Impeachment of the President:
  • Sole ground: "Violation of the Constitution".
  • Can initiate in either House of Parliament. Requires 14 days' written notice signed by at least 1/4th members of that House.
  • Passed by a special majority of not less than 2/3rd of the total membership of each House.
Article 62: Time of holding election to fill vacancy in the office of President.
Vice-President of India
Article 63: The Vice-President of India.
Article 64: Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha).
Article 65: VP to act as President or discharge functions during vacancies.
Article 66: Election of Vice-President: Electoral College consists of members of both Houses of Parliament (both elected and nominated; state MLAs do not vote). Min age: 35 years; qualified for Rajya Sabha.
Article 67: Term of office of Vice-President (5 years).
Article 68: Election to fill vacancy.
Article 69: Oath or affirmation by Vice-President (administered by President).
Article 70: Discharge of President's functions in contingencies.
Article 71: All doubts and disputes arising out of election of President or Vice-President are inquired into and decided exclusively by the Supreme Court of India, whose decision is final.
Pardoning Powers & Council of Ministers
Article 72: Pardoning Powers of the President:
  1. Pardon: Completely removes both the sentence and conviction (clean slate).
  2. Reprieve: Temporary stay/suspension on execution of a sentence (especially death penalty) to allow time to seek pardon.
  3. Respite: Awarding a lesser sentence in place of one originally penalised due to special facts (e.g., pregnancy, disability).
  4. Remission: Reducing the amount/period of sentence without altering its character (e.g., 10 yrs rigorous to 5 yrs rigorous).
  5. Commutation: Substituting one form of punishment with a lighter form (e.g., death penalty to life imprisonment).
Article 73: Extent of executive power of the Union.
Article 74: Council of Ministers to aid and advise President.
  • 42nd Amdt made advice binding; 44th Amdt gave President power to send advice back once for reconsideration, but must accept reconsidered advice.
  • 74(2): Nature of advice tendered by Ministers to the President cannot be inquired into by any court.
Article 75: Other provisions as to Ministers:
  • 75(1): PM appointed by President; other ministers appointed by President on PM's advice.
  • 75(1A): Total number of ministers including PM shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of Lok Sabha (Added by 91st Amendment Act 2003).
  • 75(2): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (Individual responsibility).
  • 75(3): The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (House of the People). 🔥 Repeated PSSSB Question
  • 75(4): Oath of office and secrecy administered by President.
  • 75(5): A Minister who is not a member of either House for 6 consecutive months ceases to be a Minister.
Article 76: Attorney-General for India: Appointed by President; must be qualified to be a Judge of the Supreme Court. Holds office during pleasure of President. Chief legal advisor to Union Government.
Article 77: Conduct of business of the Government of India (All executive action in President's name).
Article 78: Duties of Prime Minister in furnishing information to the President.
Chapter 2: Parliament of India (Articles 79–122)
Article 79: Constitution of Parliament: Consists of the President, Council of States (Rajya Sabha), and House of the People (Lok Sabha).
Article 80: Rajya Sabha (Council of States): Max strength 250 (238 elected from States/UTs + 12 nominated by President from Literature, Science, Art, Social Service).
Article 81: Lok Sabha (House of the People): Max statutory strength 550 (after Anglo-Indian abolition).
Article 82: Readjustment after each census (Delimitation Commission). Frozen till first census post-2026.
Article 83: Duration of Houses:
  • 83(1): Rajya Sabha is a permanent body not subject to dissolution; members have 6-year terms; one-third members retire every second year.
  • 83(2): Lok Sabha has a 5-year duration from date appointed for first meeting, unless dissolved earlier.
Article 84: Qualifications for membership: Citizen of India; Min age 30 years for Rajya Sabha, Min age 25 years for Lok Sabha.
Article 85: Sessions of Parliament, Prorogation, and Dissolution. Max gap between two sessions cannot exceed 6 months.
Article 86 & 87: President's address. 87: Special Address at the commencement of first session after each general election and first session of each year.
Article 88: Rights of Ministers and Attorney-General to speak and take part in proceedings of either House or joint sitting, but AG has no right to vote.
Article 89–92: Chairman (VP) and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
Article 93–97: Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha. Pro-tem speaker appointed under Art 95.
Article 98: Secretariat of Parliament.
Article 99: Oath/affirmation of MPs before President or authorized person.
Article 100: Voting and Quorum:
  • Quorum to constitute a meeting is 1/10th (10%) of the total membership of the House.
  • 100(1): Presiding officer does not vote in first instance; exercises a Casting Vote in case of equality of votes (tie).
Article 101 & 102: Vacation of seats and Disqualifications (office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, loss of citizenship, statutory disqualification).
Article 103: Decision on questions as to disqualifications of members (Decided by President after obtaining opinion of Election Commission). [Note: Defection under 10th Schedule is decided by Speaker/Chairman].
Article 105: Powers, privileges, and immunities of Parliament and its members (Freedom of speech in Parliament, immunity from court proceedings for speech/vote).
Article 108: Joint Sitting of Both Houses:
  • Summoned by the President in case of deadlock on an Ordinary Bill or Financial Bill.
  • Presided over by the Speaker of Lok Sabha (in absence: Deputy Speaker; if absent: Deputy Chairman RS. Chairman RS never presides).
  • No joint sitting allowed for Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills.
Article 109 & 110: Money Bills:
  • Article 110: Defines Money Bill (imposition/abolition/alteration of tax, borrowing, custody of Consolidated/Contingency Fund).
  • Speaker's decision certifying a Bill as Money Bill is final.
  • Introduced only in Lok Sabha with prior recommendation of President.
  • Rajya Sabha has restricted powers: must return bill within 14 days with recommendations; Lok Sabha can accept or reject. If not returned in 14 days, deemed passed.
Article 111: Assent to Bills & Presidential Veto Powers:
  1. Absolute Veto: President withholds assent; bill dies. (Generally used for Private Members' Bills or when Cabinet resigns).
  2. Suspensive Veto: President returns bill for reconsideration. If passed again by simple majority, President must give assent. Cannot be used for Money Bills.
  3. Pocket Veto: President neither assents nor returns bill; keeps it pending indefinitely. (No constitutional time limit in India, unlike USA's 10-day limit).
    Historical Precedent: 1986 by President Giani Zail Singh on the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986 regarding postal surveillance.
Article 112: Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget): The term "Budget" is not mentioned in the Constitution; officially termed Annual Financial Statement.
Article 114: Appropriation Bills (No money can be withdrawn from Consolidated Fund without this Act).
Article 115 & 116: Supplementary grants and Vote on Account (interim financial provision).
Article 117: Special provisions as to Financial Bills.
Article 120: Language used in Parliament (Hindi or English; Mother tongue permitted with permission of Speaker/Chairman).
Article 121 & 122: 121: Conduct of SC/HC judges cannot be discussed in Parliament (except during impeachment). 122: Courts cannot inquire into validity of Parliamentary proceedings.
Article 123: Power of President to Promulgate Ordinances: Can issue ordinances when either House is not in session. Has same force as an Act. Must be approved by Parliament within 6 weeks from reassembly. Max lifespan = 6 months + 6 weeks.
Chapter 3: The Union Judiciary – Supreme Court (Articles 124–147)
Article 124: Establishment and constitution of Supreme Court (Inaugurated on 28 January 1950).
  • 124(3) Qualifications: Citizen of India; Judge of High Court for at least 5 years, OR Advocate of High Court for at least 10 years, OR distinguished jurist in President's opinion.
  • 124(4) Removal/Impeachment: By order of President after address supported by special majority of each House on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • Retirement age: 65 years.
Article 125: Salaries and allowances of judges (Charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India).
Article 126 & 127: 126: Appointment of Acting Chief Justice. 127: Ad-hoc Judges (appointed by CJI with previous consent of President).
Article 128: Attendance of retired judges at Supreme Court sittings.
Article 129: Court of Record: Supreme Court is a court of record and possesses power to punish for its contempt.
Article 130: Seat of Supreme Court: Delhi (CJI has power to sit elsewhere with President's approval).
Article 131: Original Jurisdiction: Exclusive jurisdiction to decide federal disputes (Centre vs State, Centre + States vs States, State vs State).
Article 132–134: Appellate jurisdiction (Constitutional, Civil, Criminal matters).
Article 136: Special Leave Petition (SLP): Discretionary power of SC to grant leave to appeal against any judgment/order from any court or tribunal in India (except military tribunals).
Article 137: Review Jurisdiction: SC has power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it.
Article 141: Law declared by Supreme Court to be binding on all courts within India.
Article 142: Enforcement of decrees and orders for "Complete Justice": SC can pass any order necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter. 📰 Prominently invoked in Chandigarh Mayor Election Verdict 2024
Article 143: Advisory Jurisdiction: President can refer any question of law or fact of public importance to SC for advisory opinion. (SC not bound to give opinion on pre-constitutional treaties; opinion is not binding on President).
Article 148–151: Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG):
  • Art 148: CAG appointed by President. Tenure: 6 years or 65 years of age. Removal procedure identical to SC Judge. Guardian of public purse.
  • Art 149: Duties and powers of CAG.
  • Art 150: Prescribes form of accounts of Union and States.
  • Art 151: CAG submits audit reports to President/Governor, who causes them to be laid before Parliament/State Legislature (Examined by Public Accounts Committee - PAC).
Part VI: The States (Articles 152–237)
State Govt Focus 📰 Art 200 Governor Assent Rulings
State Executive (Governor, CM, Advocate General)
Article 153: Governor for each State (7th Amdt 1956 permitted same person as Governor for two or more states).
Article 154: Executive power of State vested in Governor.
Article 155: Governor appointed by warrant under hand and seal of the President.

📰 Landmark SC Ruling (State of Punjab v. Governor, 2023-2024): The Supreme Court settled that the Governor cannot sit indefinitely on bills passed by the legislature. Parts VIII, IX, IX-A, IX-B: Local Self-Government
🔥 100% PSSSB Exam Target Panchayat Focus
Historical Milestones in Local Self-Government:
  • Ancient roots: Chola dynasty (Uttaramerur inscriptions on village administration).
  • Lord Ripon's Resolution (1882): Known as the "Father of Local Self-Government in India".
  • Balwant Rai Mehta: Regarded as the "Father of Panchayati Raj System in India".
  • First Implementation: Inaugurated at Nagaur, Rajasthan on 2 October 1959 by Jawaharlal Nehru. Second state: Andhra Pradesh.
  • First State after 73rd Amendment: Karnataka was the first to adopt Panchayati Raj under constitutional status.
  • National Panchayati Raj Day: Celebrated on 24th April (73rd Amendment Act 1992 came into effect on 24 April 1993).
  • Structural facts: Standard 3-tier system. West Bengal has 4 tiers. Goa has 2 tiers. States having population below 20 lakhs need not constitute intermediate level (Panchayat Samiti).
  • PESA Act 1996: Provisions of Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act for tribal areas.
  • Meetings: Panchayat meetings must be held at least once in 2 months. Sarpanch submits resignation to District Panchayat Officer (DPO).

Article 310: Doctrine of Pleasure: Civil servants hold office during the pleasure of the President or Governor.

  • UPSC originally established on 1 October 1926 (Lee Commission recommendations).
  • Appointment: UPSC Chairman/Members appointed by President. SPSC Chairman/Members appointed by Governor.
  • Tenure: UPSC: 6 years or 65 years of age. SPSC: 6 years or 62 years of age (age raised from 60 to 62 by 41st Amendment 1976).

  • Resignation: UPSC submits to President; SPSC submits to Governor.
  • Joint State Public Service Commission (JSPSC): Created by an Act of Parliament on request of concerned State legislatures (Statutory body, not constitutional). Chairman/members appointed and removed by President; tenure 6 years or 62 years.
Article 323: Reports of Public Service Commissions.

Parts XVIII, XIX, XX, XXI: Emergency, Amendments & Special States Critical Exam Rules
Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions (Articles 352–360)

Provisions borrowed from Government of India Act 1935; suspension of Fundamental Rights borrowed from Weimar Constitution of Germany.

Article 352: National Emergency:
  • Grounds: War, External Aggression, or Armed Rebellion. (The phrase "Armed Rebellion" replaced "Internal Disturbance" via 44th Amdt 1978).
  • Can be proclaimed only on the written recommendation of the Union Cabinet (PM + Cabinet ministers).
  • Must be approved by Parliament within 1 month by a special majority (reduced from 2 months by 44th Amdt). Once approved, operates for 6 months (extended indefinitely in 6-month blocks).
  • Imposed 3 times in India:
    1. October 1962: Indo-China War (revoked Jan 1968).
    2. December 1971: Indo-Pakistan War (Bangladesh Liberation).
    3. June 1975: Declared on grounds of "Internal Disturbance" by Indira Gandhi Govt (revoked March 1977).
Article 353 & 354: Effects of Emergency: Centre acquires power to give executive directions to states; Parliament can legislate on State List.
Article 355: Duty of Union to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance, and ensure governance is carried on in accordance with Constitution.
Article 356: President's Rule (State Emergency / Constitutional Machinery Failure):
  • Imposed by President on receipt of report from Governor or otherwise, if State governance cannot run according to Constitution.
  • Must be approved by Parliament within 2 months by simple majority. Valid for 6 months; max limit is 3 years (subject to EC certification post 1 year).
  • First imposed in Punjab in 1951.
Article 358: Automatic suspension of freedoms guaranteed under Article 19 during National Emergency (Only when proclaimed on grounds of War or External Aggression, NOT Armed Rebellion).
Article 359: Suspension of the right to move courts for enforcement of FRs during emergency by Presidential order. Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended under any circumstance (44th Amendment 1978).
Article 360: Financial Emergency: Proclaimed if financial stability or credit of India is threatened. Approved within 2 months by simple majority. Has NEVER been declared in India so far.
Part XIX: Miscellaneous (Articles 361–367)
Article 361: Protection/Immunity to President and Governors: Not answerable to any court for exercise of duties; no criminal proceedings can be instituted during term of office.
Article 365: Effect of failure to comply with, or to give effect to, directions given by the Union: Lawful to hold that a situation has arisen where state governance cannot be carried on (triggers Art 356).
Part XX: Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)
  • Borrowed from South Africa. Can be initiated only in Parliament (not in state assemblies).
  • No provision for a joint sitting in case of disagreement over a Constitutional Amendment Bill.
  • 24th Amendment Act 1971: Made it mandatory for the President to give assent to a Constitutional Amendment Bill (no veto allowed).
  • Three Types of Amendments:
    1. Simple Majority: (Outside Art 368) e.g., creation of new states, citizenship, quorum, official language, 5th/6th Schedule.
    2. Special Majority of Parliament: (Under Art 368) Majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting (e.g., Fundamental Rights, DPSP).
    3. Special Majority + Ratification by 1/2 of State Legislatures: For federal structures (e.g., election of President, representation of states in Parliament, 7th Schedule lists, SC/HC powers, Art 368 itself).

Champakam Dorairajan v. State of Madras (1951)Held that Fundamental Rights prevail over Directive Principles in case of conflict. Prompted the First Constitutional Amendment (Article 15(4)).
Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967)Supreme Court ruled that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights or abridge them under Article 368.
Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
13-Judge Bench		Propounded the "Basic Structure Doctrine". Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution (including FRs), but cannot alter or destroy the basic structure of the Constitution.
Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975)Applied Basic Structure Doctrine; struck down Article 329A(4) (which took PM's election beyond judicial review); held Judicial Review & Free/Fair Elections are basic structure.
ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla (1976)
(Habeas Corpus Case)		Held that right to life (Art 21) could be suspended during emergency. Infamous ruling, later formally overruled in the Puttaswamy (2017) judgment.
Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)Expanded Article 21. "Procedure established by law" must be just, fair, and reasonable (read American concept of "Due Process of Law" into Art 21). Overruled Gopalan case.
Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)Reinforced Basic Structure. Ruled that Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles. Struck down clauses in Art 368 excluding judicial review.
Shah Bano Begum Case (1985)Upheld divorced Muslim woman's right to maintenance under Section 125 of CrPC; emphasized need for Uniform Civil Code (Article 44).
S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)Established rigorous checks against arbitrary misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule). Held secularism and federalism as basic structure; proclamation is subject to judicial review.
Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)Laid down binding guidelines to protect women from sexual harassment at workplaces (precursor to POSH Act, 2013).
I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007)Ruled that laws placed in the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 (date of Kesavananda Bharati verdict) are open to judicial review if they violate basic structure.
Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017)Unanimously declared that the Right to Privacy is an intrinsic Fundamental Right under Article 21 and Part III of the Constitution.
Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)Decriminalized consensual same-sex relations by reading down Section 377 of IPC; upheld equality and non-discrimination under Art 14, 15, and 21.
Indian Young Lawyers Association (Sabarimala, 2018)Allowed entry of women of all age groups into Sabarimala Temple, upholding right to worship and gender equality under Articles 25 and 14.
