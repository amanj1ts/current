1 What Actually is "Voice"?

The grammatical relationship between the action and the doer/receiver.

Foundation Rule
Active Voice Subject does action

Focus is on the DOER of the action.

[Subject] + [Verb] + [Object]
👉 "Alexander defeated Porus."
Passive Voice Object receives action

Focus is on the RECEIVER or the action itself.

[Object becomes Subject] + [Be-Verb + V3] + by + [Agent]
👉 "Porus was defeated by Alexander."

The Golden 5-Step Conversion Blueprint

Memorize this exact sequential order for 100% accuracy in any conversion question.

Step 1

Swap Positions

Identify Subject & Object in Active sentence. The Active Object becomes the New Subject.

Step 2

Identify Tense

Determine the exact tense. Never change the tense; only change the voice. (Present stays Present!).

Step 3

Insert 'Be' Verb

Add the suitable form of be (is/am/are, was/were, being, been) matching the new subject.

Step 4

Always Use V3

Transform the main verb into its Past Participle (V3). In passive voice, the main verb is ALWAYS V3.

Step 5

Add 'by' + Agent

Attach by + object form of original subject (or specific preposition if non-'by' verb).

Subjective ⇄ Objective Pronoun Shift

Exam Trap Area

When a pronoun shifts from Subject to Object position (or vice versa), its grammatical case changes:

Subject Case (Active) Direction Object Case (Passive) Quick Note
I ➔ me by me
We ➔ us by us
You ➔ you No change!
He ➔ him by him
She ➔ her by her
It ➔ it No change!
They ➔ them by them
Who ➔ By whom Crucial Exam Question
⚠️ Watch out: Proper nouns (like Rahul, London, Apple) do not change their spelling: "Rahul taught me" ➔ "I was taught by Rahul."

The "BE" Verb Family Table

The Engine of Passive

Passive voice is mathematically: [Appropriate form of BE] + V3.

Base / Infinitive be / to be

Used with modals (can be, will be, must be)

Present (V1) is / am / are

Used for Simple Present passive transformations

Past (V2) was / were

Used for Simple Past passive transformations

Continuous / -ing being

Preserves continuous action (is being, was being)

Perfect / Participle (V3) been

Used for all Perfect tenses (has been, have been, had been, will have been)

Golden Rule of Agreement: The be-verb agrees with the new subject, NOT the original subject:
Active: "He eats apples." (Plural object)
Passive: "Apples ARE eaten by him." (Verb changed to 'are')

Absolute Condition: Only Transitive Verbs Can Form Passive Voice!

In exams, examiners frequently give sentences with Intransitive Verbs (verbs with NO object) to trick students into creating illogical passive forms.

❌ No Passive Possible (Intransitive Verbs)

Verbs of motion, state, or existence that answer "where?", "when?", "how?" but NEVER "what?" or "whom?":

• go, come, arrive, sleep, die, laugh, cry, sneeze, fall, run (when without direct object)
Example: "He went to school." (Here 'to school' is a prepositional phrase of place, NOT an object. Passive cannot be made!)
✅ Passive Possible (Transitive Verbs)

Verbs that transfer action directly to a direct object (answers "what?" or "whom?"):

• write (a letter), buy (a car), defeat (an enemy), inform (him)
Example: "He wrote a letter." ➔ "A letter was written by him." (Letter answers what he wrote).

Where 90% of Students Make Errors

Competitions love these 7 advanced structures. Master each pattern thoroughly.

High Exam Yield
Trap 1 • Imperatives Orders / Requests / Advice

Orders, Requests & Suggestions

Imperatives lack a visible subject (implied 'you'). Three conversion models exist:

A. Command / Order with Object: Let + Object + be + V3

Active: "Shut the door."

Passive: "Let the door be shut."

B. Moral Suggestion / Advice: Object + should be + V3

Active: "Help the poor."

Passive: "The poor should be helped."

C. Intransitive or Polite ('Please'): You are requested/ordered/advised to + V1

Active: "Please sit down."

Passive: "You are requested to sit down."

Trap 2 • Interrogatives Who ⇄ By whom

Question Forms (Wh- & Did/Do)

Interrogative structure must stay interrogative (Auxiliary before subject):

A. 'Who' shifts to 'By whom':

Active: "Who wrote the Mahabharata?"

Passive: "By whom was the Mahabharata written?"

(Modern form: "Who was the Mahabharata written by?")

B. 'Whom' shifts to 'Who':

Active: "Whom did you invite?"

Passive: "Who was invited by you?"

C. Do/Does/Did questions:

Active: "Did he break the glass?" (Did = Past)

Passive: "Was the glass broken by him?" (Was + V3)

Trap 3 • Fixed Prepositions Never use 'by' here

Verbs Requiring Specific Prepositions

Many verbs refuse the preposition 'by' in passive voice. Examiners test this relentlessly!

known TO

not 'known by'

married TO

not 'married with'

surprised AT

at behaviour/news

astonished AT

at something

pleased WITH

with a person

satisfied WITH

with your work

contained IN

in the box/bottle

lined WITH

trees lined with

Active: "I know Mr. Sharma." Passive: "Mr. Sharma is known TO me." (NOT 'by me')
Trap 4 • Ditransitive Verbs Two Valid Passives

Two Objects: Indirect (Person) vs Direct (Thing)

Verbs like give, teach, send, offer, tell take two objects. Both can become subjects:

Active sentence:

"The teacher gave me (Indirect) a prize (Direct)."

Option A (Indirect as Subject - More Natural & Preferred):

➔ "I was given a prize by the teacher."

Option B (Direct as Subject - Requires 'to'):

➔ "A prize was given to me by the teacher."

Trap 5 • Modals can, must, should have

Modal Verbs & Modal Perfects

Simple modals take modal + be + V3; modal perfects take modal + have been + V3.

Simple Modal: [Modal + be + V3]

Active: "You must obey the rules."

Passive: "The rules must be obeyed by you."

Modal Perfect: [Modal + have been + V3]

Active: "They should have sent the email."

Passive: "The email should have been sent by them."

Trap 6 • Quasi-Passive Active Form, Passive Sense

Quasi-Passive Verbs (Verbs with Adjectives)

Sentences that look active in structure but convey passive meaning (taste, smell, feel):

Formula: Subject + is/was + Adjective + when it is/was + V3
Active: "Quinine tastes bitter."
Passive: "Quinine is bitter when it is tasted."
Active: "The rose smells sweet."
Passive: "The rose is sweet when it is smelt."
Trap 7 • Agent Omission & 'People say that' Dummy It & Dropped Agents

A. Dropping Vague or Obvious Agents

Do NOT add 'by someone / by people / by police' if the agent is vague, unknown, or inherently understood.

Active: "Someone stole my watch."
Passive: "My watch was stolen." (NOT 'by someone')
Active: "The police arrested the thief."
Passive: "The thief was arrested." (Arrest is obviously done by police)

B. "People say that..." Sentences

When reporting general beliefs: use "It is said that..." or infinitive passive.

Active: "People say that honesty is the best policy."
Passive: "It is said that honesty is the best policy."
Active: "People believe that he is a genius."
Passive: "He is believed to be a genius."
