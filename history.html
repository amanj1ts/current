High-Yield Exam Focus: Strategic Engagements, Dates & Belligerents

Exhaustive chronological inventory of decisive military engagements from the Rigvedic era to post-independence wars, specifically curated for competitive examinations.

War / Battle Date / Period Opponents (Belligerents) Decisive Outcome, Treaty & Exam Signposts
Battle of the Ten Kings (Dasrajan Yudh) c. 14th c. BCE King Sudas (Bharata Trtsu Tribe) vs. Confederation of 10 Aryan/Non-Aryan tribes Rigveda Fought on banks of River Parushni (Ravi). Sudas won under sage Vashistha's guidance, establishing Bharata supremacy.[cite: 1]
Battle of the Hydaspes 326 BCE Alexander the Great (Macedon) vs. King Porus (Paurava Kingdom) Fought on River Jhelum (Vitasta/Hydaspes)[cite: 1]. Alexander won with tactical crossing; impressed by Porus's bravery, restored his kingdom. Soldiers mutinied at River Beas (Hyphasis)[cite: 1].
Kalinga War 261 BCE Emperor Ashoka (Maurya Empire) vs. State of Kalinga (modern Odisha) Massive slaughter recorded in Rock Edict XIII. Prompted Ashoka's transition from Bherighosha (war drum) to Dhammaghosha (moral law) and adoption of Buddhism.
Battle of Koppam 1054 CE Rajendra Chola II vs. Someshvara I (Western Chalukyas) Rajadhiraja Chola was killed on elephant back; brother Rajendra Chola II crowned on battlefield and decisively routed Chalukyas.
First Battle of Tarain 1191 CE Prithviraj Chauhan (Chahamana) vs. Muhammad Ghori (Ghurid) Prithviraj Chauhan decisively defeated and injured Muhammad Ghori at Tarain (near Karnal/Thanesar).
Second Battle of Tarain 1192 CE Muhammad Ghori vs. Prithviraj Chauhan Turning Point Ghori employed mobile horse archers; Chauhan captured and killed. Established Muslim rule in North India; Qutb-ud-din Aibak became governor.
Battle of Chandwar 1194 CE Muhammad Ghori vs. Jaichand (Gahadavala King of Kannauj) Ghori defeated Jaichand; extended Turkish suzerainty over Benaras and the Gangetic plain.
First Battle of Panipat 21 April 1526 Babur (Mughal/Timurid) vs. Ibrahim Lodhi (Delhi Sultanate) Foundation of Mughal Empire. Babur utilized gunpowder, cannons, and Tulghuma (flanking) strategy. Ibrahim Lodhi died on battlefield.
Battle of Khanwa 16 March 1527 Babur vs. Rana Sanga of Mewar Babur declared Jihad, assumed title Ghazi, and secured supremacy in northern India.
Battle of Chausa 26 June 1539 Sher Shah Suri vs. Humayun Humayun defeated and escaped by jumping into Ganges (saved by Nizam water-carrier). Sher Shah took imperial title.
Battle of Kannauj (Bilgram) 17 May 1540 Sher Shah Suri vs. Humayun Humayun routed; driven into exile to Persia for 15 years. Established Second Afghan Empire (Sur Empire).
Battle of Sirhind 22 June 1555 Humayun & Bairam Khan vs. Sikandar Shah Suri Mughals won; Humayun recovered Delhi and Agra thrones.
Second Battle of Panipat 5 Nov 1556 Bairam Khan / Young Akbar vs. Hemu (Hemchandra Vikramaditya) Hemu struck in eye by stray arrow while winning; Mughals captured and executed him, re-establishing Mughal supremacy.
Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi) 23 Jan 1565 Vijayanagara Empire (Aliya Rama Raya) vs. Deccan Sultanates coalition Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar) sacked and ruined the capital city of Vijayanagara (Hampi).
Battle of Haldighati 18 June 1576 Man Singh I & Asaf Khan (Mughals) vs. Maharana Pratap (Mewar) Fierce clash in Aravallis; Maharana's horse Chetak martyred. Mewar maintained fierce guerrilla resistance.
Battle of Samugarh 29 May 1658 Aurangzeb & Murad Baksh vs. Dara Shikoh Decisive Mughal war of succession. Dara Shikoh defeated; Aurangzeb imprisoned Shah Jahan at Agra Fort and seized throne.
Battle of Saraighat 1671 CE Lachit Borphukan (Ahom Kingdom) vs. Ram Singh I (Mughals) River battle fought on Brahmaputra. Ahom forces successfully repulsed massive Mughal imperial expansion into Assam.
Battle of Chappar Chiri 12 May 1710 Baba Banda Singh Bahadur vs. Wazir Khan (Subahdar of Sirhind) Wazir Khan killed by Fateh Singh[cite: 1]; Sikhs captured Sirhind, establishing 1st sovereign Sikh state with capital at Lohgarh[cite: 1].
Battle of Karnal 24 Feb 1739 Nadir Shah (Afsharid Persia) vs. Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela' (Mughal) Nadir Shah defeated imperial Mughal forces in 3 hours; ransacked Delhi, looted Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor.
Third Battle of Panipat 14 Jan 1761 Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani) vs. Sadashivrao Bhau & Vishwasrao (Marathas) Catastrophic Maratha defeat; halted Maratha imperial advance to the north, clearing space for British East India Company.
Battle of Plassey 23 June 1757 Robert Clive (EIC) vs. Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal) Start of British Political Rule. Mir Jafar betrayed the Nawab. British gained revenue control over 24 Parganas.
Battle of Wandiwash 22 Jan 1760 Sir Eyre Coote (British) vs. Comte de Lally (French) 3rd Carnatic War climax. Crushed French territorial ambitions in India; ended with Treaty of Paris (1763).
Battle of Buxar 22 Oct 1764 Major Hector Munro (EIC) vs. Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, Shah Alam II Crucial Milestone Defeated combined tripartite alliance. Resulted in Treaty of Allahabad (1765) granting Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to British.
Battle of Porto Novo 1 July 1781 Sir Eyre Coote (EIC) vs. Hyder Ali (Kingdom of Mysore) 2nd Anglo-Mysore War; Coote checked Mysore's advance. Hyder Ali died in 1782; succeeded by Tipu Sultan.
Battle of Seringapatam 4 May 1799 Lord Wellesley & Gen. Harris vs. Tipu Sultan (Tiger of Mysore) 4th Anglo-Mysore War; Tipu Sultan died defending the breached fort. Mysore placed under Wodeyar dynasty via Subsidiary Alliance.
Battle of Assaye 23 Sept 1803 Arthur Wellesley (later Duke of Wellington) vs. Maratha Empire (Scindia & Bhonsle) 2nd Anglo-Maratha War; bloody British victory breaking Maratha northern army. Led to Treaty of Deogaon and Surji-Anjangaon.
Battle of Khadki (Kirkee) 5 Nov 1817 British EIC vs. Peshwa Baji Rao II 3rd Anglo-Maratha War. Peshwaship abolished; Baji Rao II pensioned off to Bithoor (Kanpur). Maratha empire extinguished.
First Anglo-Sikh War (5 Battles) Dec 1845 – Feb 1846 British EIC (Sir Hugh Gough, Hardinge) vs. Sikh Empire (Sarkar-i-Khalsa) Battles: Mudki, Ferozeshah, Baddowal, Aliwal, Sobraon[cite: 1, 2]. Betrayal of commanders Tej Singh & Lal Singh[cite: 1, 2]. Ended with Treaty of Lahore (9 Mar 1846)[cite: 1, 2]. Kashmir sold to Gulab Singh via Treaty of Amritsar (16 Mar 1846)[cite: 1, 2].
Second Anglo-Sikh War (4 Battles) 1848 – 1849 British EIC (Lord Gough, Dalhousie) vs. Sikh Empire (Diwan Mulraj, Sher Singh) Battles: Ramnagar, Chillianwala, Multan, Gujrat[cite: 1, 2]. Decisive artillery duel at Gujrat ("Battle of the Guns")[cite: 1, 2]. Formal annexation of Punjab on 29 March 1849[cite: 1, 2].
First Indo-Pakistani War 1947 – 1948 Dominion of India vs. Dominion of Pakistan (Tribal Lashkars) Followed Maharaja Hari Singh's signing of Instrument of Accession (26 Oct 1947). Ceasefire on 1 Jan 1949 created Line of Control (LoC).
Sino-Indian War Oct – Nov 1962 Republic of India vs. People's Republic of China Clashes along Aksai Chin and NEFA (Arunachal Pradesh). Unilateral Chinese ceasefire 21 Nov 1962.
Second Indo-Pakistani War Aug – Sept 1965 Republic of India vs. Islamic Republic of Pakistan Operation Gibraltar repulsed; tank battles at Asal Uttar (Punjab). Mediated via Tashkent Declaration (10 Jan 1966) by Lal Bahadur Shastri & Ayub Khan.
Third Indo-Pakistani War 3–16 Dec 1971 India & Mukti Bahini vs. Pakistan Armed Forces Historic surrender of 93,000 Pakistani soldiers under Lt. Gen. Niazi to Lt. Gen. J.S. Aurora. Liberation of Bangladesh. Signed Shimla Agreement (1972).
Kargil Conflict (Operation Vijay) May – July 1999 Republic of India vs. Pakistan Army Infiltrators High-altitude combat in Dras, Batalik, and Tiger Hill sectors. Complete recapture celebrated as Kargil Vijay Diwas (26 July).
1. Early 19th Century & Punjab Annexation (1800 – 1856) Company Hegemony

25 April 1809 Treaty of Amritsar (Ranjit Singh & Metcalfe)

  • Signatories: Maharaja Ranjit Singh & Charles T. Metcalfe (on behalf of Gov-Gen Lord Minto I)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Strategic Frontier: Fixed Sutlej River as the strict boundary between Sikh Empire and British territories[cite: 1, 2]. British placed Cis-Sutlej states (Patiala, Nabha, Jind) under protection[cite: 1, 2].
  • Exam Signpost: Ranjit Singh reoriented conquests westward: Multan (1818), Kashmir (1819), and Peshawar (1834)[cite: 1, 2].
MNEMONIC: Metcalfe Met Maharaja at Amritsar[cite: 2]

TRAP Do not confuse with Treaty of Amritsar (1846) where British sold Kashmir to Gulab Singh Dogra for 75 lakh rupees[cite: 1, 2].

1813 & 1833 Charter Acts & Administrative Consolidation

  • Charter Act 1813: Terminated EIC commercial trade monopoly (retained only tea and China trade)[cite: 2]. Sanctioned ₹1 Lakh annually for Indian education[cite: 2]; allowed Christian missionaries[cite: 2].
  • Charter Act 1833: Completely abolished commercial operations[cite: 2]. Designated Governor-General of Bengal as Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck was first)[cite: 2]. Macaulay appointed 1st Law Member[cite: 2].

TRAP 1st GG of Bengal = Warren Hastings (1773); 1st GG of India = William Bentinck (1833)[cite: 2].

1828 – 1829 Social Reforms & Bengal Regulation XVII

  • Brahmo Sabha (20 Aug 1828): Founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy in Calcutta[cite: 2]; title 'Raja' bestowed by Mughal Emperor Akbar II[cite: 2]. Opposed polytheism, idolatry, and caste abuses[cite: 2].
  • Abolition of Sati (4 Dec 1829): Lord William Bentinck promulgated Regulation XVII declaring Sati culpable homicide[cite: 2]. Extended to Madras & Bombay in 1830[cite: 2].

27 June 1839 Demise of Maharaja Ranjit Singh & Court Instability

  • Rule: Sher-e-Punjab ruled 1799–1839; crowned on Baisakhi 1801 by Baba Sahib Singh Bedi[cite: 1, 2].
  • Destabilisation: Demise triggered palace factionalism between Sandhawalias and Dogra brothers[cite: 2]; rapid succession: Kharak Singh → Nau Nihal Singh → Sher Singh → minor Duleep Singh (1843)[cite: 1, 2].

Dec 1845 – Feb 1846 1st Anglo-Sikh War (Gov-Gen Lord Hardinge)

BATTLE ORDER: M-F-B-A-S (Mudki → Ferozeshah → Baddowal → Aliwal → Sobraon)[cite: 1, 2]
  • Mudki (18 Dec 1845): Lal Singh ordered fire and abandoned Sikh ranks[cite: 1].
  • Ferozeshah (21 Dec 1845): Lord Hardinge's "Night of Terrors"; British saved by Tej Singh's treachery[cite: 1].
  • Baddowal (21 Jan 1846): Ranjodh Singh Majithia captured British baggage trains[cite: 1].
  • Aliwal (28 Jan 1846): Sir Harry Smith defeated Ranjodh Singh Majithia[cite: 1, 2].
  • Sobraon (10 Feb 1846): "Waterloo of Punjab". Sardar Sham Singh Atariwala attained martyrdom[cite: 1].
  • Treaty of Lahore (9 Mar 1846): Jalandhar Doab ceded; Henry Lawrence stationed as Resident[cite: 1, 2].

1848 – 1849 2nd Anglo-Sikh War & Annexation (Lord Dalhousie)

BATTLE ORDER: R-C-M-G (Ramnagar → Chillianwala → Multan → Gujrat)[cite: 1, 2]
  • Origins: Revolts of Diwan Mulraj (Multan) & Chattar Singh Attariwala (Hazara)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Chillianwala (13 Jan 1849): High British casualties on River Jhelum; Lord Gough criticized[cite: 1, 2].
  • Gujrat (21 Feb 1849): "Battle of the Guns". Overwhelming British artillery triumphed[cite: 1, 2].
  • Annexation (29 March 1849): Proclaimed by Lord Dalhousie[cite: 1, 2]. Governed by 3-member Board of Administration (1849–53): Henry Lawrence (Pres.), John Lawrence, Charles Mansel[cite: 1, 2]. Replaced in 1853 by Chief Commissioner John Lawrence[cite: 1, 2].
  • Wood's Despatch (July 1854): "Magna Carta of Indian Education" recommending vernacluar primaries, Anglo-vernacular schools, and collegiate universities[cite: 2].
2. Revolt of 1857 & Crown Rule Transition (1857 – 1884) Revolt to Early Crown

10 May 1857 The Great Revolt of 1857 (Lord Canning)

  • Outbreak: Sparked at Meerut following Mangal Pandey's defiance at Barrackpore (29 March 1857, 34th BNI) over Enfield P-53 greased cartridges[cite: 2].
  • Key Centres & Leaders: Delhi (Bahadur Shah II / Bakht Khan), Kanpur (Nana Saheb / Tantia Tope), Lucknow (Begum Hazrat Mahal), Jhansi (Rani Lakshmibai), Arrah (Kunwar Singh)[cite: 2].
  • Punjab Link: Cis-Sutlej rulers (Patiala, Jind, Nabha, Kapurthala) aided British logistic lines[cite: 2]. Regiments mutinied at Ferozepur, Jalandhar, and Ludhiana[cite: 2]. John Lawrence mobilized the "Punjab Movable Column" to recapture Delhi[cite: 2].
  • Act of 1858 (1 Nov 1858): EIC abolished[cite: 2]; Crown direct rule instituted[cite: 2]. Secretary of State for India established (15-member council)[cite: 2]. Canning proclaimed Queen Victoria's charter at Allahabad Durbar[cite: 2].

1872 & 1873 Socio-Religious Mobilization in Punjab

  • Kuka / Namdhari Outbreak (17–18 Jan 1872): Deputy Comm. L. Cowan blew 66 Kukas from mouths of cannons without trial at Malerkotla[cite: 1, 2]. Baba Ram Singh deported to Rangoon (died 1885)[cite: 2]. PYQ Pioneered non-cooperation and boycott decades before Gandhi[cite: 2].
  • Singh Sabha Movement (1 Oct 1873): Founded at Manji Sahib, Amritsar under Thakur Singh Sandhawalia (Pres.) and Giani Gian Singh (Sec.)[cite: 1, 2]. Countered missionary conversion waves[cite: 1, 2]. Lahore Singh Sabha established 1879 by Prof. Gurmukh Singh and Ditt Singh[cite: 1, 2]. Led to foundation of Khalsa College Amritsar (1892)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Arya Samaj (10 April 1875): Founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in Bombay (slogan: "Go Back to the Vedas")[cite: 2]. Strong mass footing in Punjab after 1877 Lahore visit[cite: 2]. Split 1893: College Party (DAV Lahore 1886: Lala Hansraj, Lala Lajpat Rai) vs. Gurukul Party (Swami Shraddhanand)[cite: 2].

March 1878 Vernacular Press Act & Arms Act (Lord Lytton)

  • Gagging Act: Empowered magistrates to confiscate press equipment of vernacular newspapers publishing seditious contents without right of appeal[cite: 2].
  • Evasion: Amrita Bazar Patrika turned into an English-language weekly overnight to circumvent the statute[cite: 2].
  • Repeal: Repealed in 1882 by the liberal successor Lord Ripon[cite: 2].
MNEMONIC: Lytton Gagged (1878); Ripon Repealed (1882)[cite: 2]

1881 & 1882 Local Self-Government & Census Reforms

  • Lord Ripon's Resolution (1882): Established elected local bodies and district rural boards[cite: 2]. Lord Ripon venerated as the "Father of Local Self-Government in India"[cite: 2].
  • Census of India (1881): First synchronized synchronous decennial census conducted under Lord Ripon[cite: 2]. (Note: 1st non-synchronous census was held under Lord Mayo in 1872)[cite: 2].
  • Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883): Sought to authorize Indian magistrates to try Europeans; sparked ferocious Anglo-Indian backlash, proving necessity of national political association[cite: 2].
3. Early Phase & Rise of Indian National Congress (1885 – 1905) Constitutional Agitation

28–31 Dec 1885 Foundation of Indian National Congress (Lord Dufferin)

  • Inaugural Venue: Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay[cite: 2]. (Shifted from Poona due to cholera outbreak)[cite: 2].
  • Key Architect: Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume, retired civil servant)[cite: 2]. First President: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee)[cite: 2].
  • Attendance: 72 delegates[cite: 2]. Punjab representatives included Lala Muralidhar of Ambala ("Grand Old Man of Punjab") and Jaishi Ram[cite: 2].

1893 & 1900 Punjab Engagements & Agrarian Legislation

  • 9th INC Session at Lahore (27–30 Dec 1893): First INC session held in Punjab[cite: 2]. Presided by Dadabhai Naoroji (recently elected 1st Indian MP to British House of Commons from Finsbury)[cite: 2]. Demanded simultaneous civil service exams[cite: 2].
  • Punjab Alienation of Land Act (1900): Passed by Lord Curzon[cite: 2]. Barred sale/mortgage of agrarian land to "non-agriculturist" moneylender classes without DC sanction[cite: 2]. Divided Punjabi society into statutory agricultural tribes and trading classes[cite: 2].
4. Swadeshi Movement, Revolutionary Agitation & Ghadar Phase (1905 – 1918) Revolutionary Vanguard

16 Oct 1905 Partition of Bengal & The Swadeshi Movement

  • Curzon's Scheme: Bifurcated Bengal into Western Bengal and Eastern Bengal & Assam under pretext of administrative convenience[cite: 2].
  • Reaction: Swadeshi resolution passed at Calcutta Town Hall (7 Aug 1905)[cite: 2]. Effective date (16 Oct 1905) marked as Day of Mourning and Raksha Bandhan[cite: 2]. Emergence of extremist trio Lal-Bal-Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal)[cite: 2].
  • Morley-Minto Reforms (1909): Indian Councils Act 1909 granted separate electorates for Muslims[cite: 2]. Lord Minto dubbed "Father of Communal Electorate"[cite: 2].

March–May 1907 Pagri Sambhal Jatta Agitation (Punjab Agrarian Crisis)

  • Root Cause: Peasant uprising against Punjab Colonisation Bill 1906 and Bari Doab canal water rate hikes[cite: 1, 2].
  • Leaders: Sardar Ajit Singh (founded Bharat Mata Society / Anjuman-i-Muhibban-i-Watan) and Sufi Amba Prasad[cite: 1, 2].
  • Anthem: Famous poem "Pagri Sambhal Jatta" penned by Banke Dayal (Editor, Jhang Syal)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Deportation: Sardar Ajit Singh and Lala Lajpat Rai were deported without trial to Mandalay Jail (Burma) under Regulation III of 1818[cite: 1, 2].

1 Nov 1913 The Ghadar Party & 1915 Armed Uprising

  • Foundation: Set up in San Francisco as Pacific Coast Hindustan Association[cite: 2]. HQ: Yugantar Ashram[cite: 1, 2].
  • Leadership: 1st President: Baba Sohan Singh Bhakna; General Secretary: Lala Har Dayal[cite: 1, 2].
  • Newspaper: Weekly paper Ghadar; maiden issue printed on 1 Nov 1913 in Urdu, followed by Gurmukhi[cite: 1, 2].
  • Martyrdom: Planned pan-India mutiny for 21 Feb 1915; betrayed by Kirpal Singh[cite: 2]. 19-year-old Kartar Singh Sarabha executed at Lahore Central Jail on 16 Nov 1915 (Bhagat Singh's supreme role model)[cite: 1, 2].

TRAP Founder/Gen Sec was Lala Har Dayal, but the 1st President was Baba Sohan Singh Bhakna[cite: 1, 2]!

1914 & 1916 Komagata Maru Incident & Lucknow Accord

  • Komagata Maru (1914): Japanese vessel chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh with 376 passengers to challenge racist Canadian exclusion laws[cite: 1, 2]. Repulsed at Vancouver; docked at Budge Budge (Calcutta) on 29 Sept 1914; British police opened fire, killing 20[cite: 1, 2].
  • Lucknow Pact (Dec 1916): Joint INC-Muslim League session (Tilak & Jinnah)[cite: 2]. Moderates and Extremists reunited[cite: 2]. Congress conceded separate electorates for Muslims in provincial legislatures[cite: 2].
5. Gandhian Mass Movements & Punjab Awakening (1919 – 1934) Mass Satyagraha & Martyrdom

13 April 1919 Rowlatt Satyagraha & Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

  • Rowlatt Act (March 1919): Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act; authorized 2-year imprisonment without judicial trial ("No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal")[cite: 2]. Arrest of Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew & Dr. Satyapal in Amritsar[cite: 1, 2].
  • Massacre: Baisakhi gathering at Jallianwala Bagh fired upon by Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer (1,650 rounds fired)[cite: 1, 2]. Lt. Governor of Punjab was Sir Michael O'Dwyer[cite: 1, 2].
  • Protest: Rabindranath Tagore renounced Knighthood; Mahatma Gandhi relinquished Kaisar-i-Hind medal[cite: 2]. Hunter Commission appointed[cite: 2].
  • Montford Reforms (GOI Act 1919): Enacted Dyarchy in provincial councils[cite: 2]. Extended separate communal electorates to Sikhs[cite: 2].

TRAP Udham Singh assassinated Lt. Gov. Sir Michael O'Dwyer in London (1940), NOT Brigadier Reginald Dyer[cite: 1, 2]!

1920 – 1925 The Akali Movement & Gurdwara Reform Morchas

  • Formations (1920): SGPC constituted 15 Nov 1920 (1st Pres: Sundar Singh Majithia)[cite: 1, 2]; Shiromani Akali Dal formed 14 Dec 1920 (1st Pres: Sarmukh Singh Chubbal)[cite: 1, 2].
  • Nankana Sahib Saka (20 Feb 1921): Mahant Narain Das massacred 130+ Sikhs under Bhai Lachhman Singh Dharowali[cite: 1, 2].
  • Guru Ka Bagh Morcha (Aug–Nov 1922): Non-violent resistance over langar wood[cite: 1, 2]; Rev. C.F. Andrews lauded the triumph of moral fortitude[cite: 1, 2].
  • Jaito Da Morcha (1923–25): Protesting abdication of Maharaja Ripudaman Singh of Nabha[cite: 1, 2]. Jawaharlal Nehru arrested at Jaito in Sept 1923[cite: 1, 2].
  • Sikh Gurdwaras Act (28 July 1925): Vested control of all historic Sikh shrines in the elected SGPC body[cite: 1, 2].

1920 – 1927 Non-Cooperation Movement & Kakori Action

  • Non-Cooperation Movement (1 Aug 1920): Launched on day of Tilak's demise[cite: 2]. Lala Lajpat Rai presided over Calcutta Special Session (Sept 1920)[cite: 2]. Suspended after Chauri Chaura violence (4 Feb 1922)[cite: 2].
  • Kakori Train Action (9 Aug 1925): HRA revolutionaries looted British government treasury from 8-Down train near Lucknow[cite: 2]. Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh, and Rajendra Lahiri hanged in Dec 1927[cite: 2].
  • Naujawan Bharat Sabha (March 1926): Founded at Lahore by Sardar Bhagat Singh, Chhabil Das, and Yashpal to spread socialist ideals[cite: 2].

1928 – 1931 HSRA, Simon Boycott & Lahore Conspiracies

  • Simon Commission Boycott (30 Oct 1928): Lala Lajpat Rai assaulted by SP Scott / ASP Saunders during protest at Lahore Railway Station[cite: 2]. Succumbed on 17 Nov 1928 ("Every blow struck on my head...")[cite: 2].
  • Saunders Assassination (17 Dec 1928): Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev eliminated ASP John P. Saunders in Lahore to avenge Lalaji[cite: 1, 2].
  • Central Assembly Bombing (8 April 1929): Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt threw smoke canisters against Public Safety & Trade Disputes bills[cite: 1, 2].
  • Lahore Session & Purna Swaraj (Dec 1929): Jawaharlal Nehru presided on banks of River Ravi; unfurled Tricolour on midnight of 31 Dec 1929; pledged 26 Jan 1930 as Independence Day[cite: 1, 2].
  • Shaheedi Diwas (23 March 1931): Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev hanged at 7:33 PM in Lahore Central Jail[cite: 1, 2]; cremated at Hussainiwala on Sutlej banks[cite: 1, 2].
6. Final Push Towards Independence & Partition of India (1935 – 1947) Independence & Division

1935 & 1937 GOI Act 1935 & Unionist Party in Punjab

  • GOI Act 1935: Longest imperial enactment; abolished provincial dyarchy and instituted Provincial Autonomy[cite: 2]. Established Federal Court (1937) and RBI (1935)[cite: 2].
  • 1937 Elections & Unionist Rule: In Punjab, the secular cross-communal Unionist Party (founded by Sir Fazl-i-Hussain, Sir Chhotu Ram, Sikandar Hayat Khan) won 95 of 175 seats[cite: 2]. Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan became Premier of Punjab (followed by Khizr Hayat Tiwana in 1942)[cite: 2].

March 1940 Lahore Resolution & Udham Singh's Vengeance

  • Udham Singh at Caxton Hall (13 March 1940): Shot dead former Lt. Gov. Sir Michael O'Dwyer in London[cite: 1, 2]. Used court pseudonym "Ram Mohammad Singh Azad"; executed at Pentonville Prison on 31 July 1940[cite: 1, 2].
  • Lahore / Pakistan Resolution (23 March 1940): Muslim League session at Minto Park, Lahore drafted by Sikandar Hayat Khan, moved by Fazlul Huq demanding sovereign Muslim states[cite: 2]. Master Tara Singh strongly protested[cite: 2].

1942 – 1945 Cripps Mission, Quit India & Simla Conference

  • Cripps Mission (March 1942): Stafford Cripps offered post-war Dominion status and provincial secession option[cite: 2]. Gandhi called it a "Post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"[cite: 2].
  • Quit India Movement (8 Aug 1942): Passed at Gowalia Tank, Bombay (call: "Do or Die")[cite: 2]. Top leaders arrested in Operation Zero Hour[cite: 2]. Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted flag[cite: 2]. Boycotted by Unionist Party, Muslim League, and Hindu Mahasabha[cite: 2].
  • Simla Conference (June–July 1945): Lord Wavell convened talks to reconstitute council; broke down as Jinnah claimed sole right to nominate Muslims[cite: 2].

1946 – 1947 Cabinet Mission, Radcliffe Award & Freedom

  • Cabinet Mission Plan (March–May 1946): Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander[cite: 2]. Rejected outright Pakistan; created 3-tier grouping (Section B included Punjab)[cite: 2]. Constituent Assembly formed on this framework[cite: 2].
  • Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947): Partition of British India into India and Pakistan; advanced date to 15 August 1947[cite: 2]. Sir Cyril Radcliffe chaired Punjab & Bengal Boundary Commission[cite: 2].
  • 15 August 1947: India achieved Independence[cite: 2]. Punjab partitioned into East Punjab (India) and West Punjab (Pakistan)[cite: 2]. 1st CM of East Punjab: Dr. Gopi Chand Bhargava; 1st Governor: Sir Chandulal Trivedi[cite: 1, 2].

Key Governor-Generals & Viceroys for Punjab Exams

  • Lord Canning (1856–1862): 1857 Revolt[cite: 2]; Queen's Proclamation at Allahabad (1 Nov 1858)[cite: 2]; Indian Penal Code (1860)[cite: 2].
  • Lord Curzon (1899–1905): Punjab Land Alienation Act (1900)[cite: 2]; Partition of Bengal (1905)[cite: 2]; Archaeological Survey restoration[cite: 2].
  • Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921): Montford Reforms (1919)[cite: 2]; Rowlatt Act[cite: 2]; Jallianwala Bagh firing[cite: 2]; Non-Cooperation start[cite: 2].
  • Lord Irwin (1926–1931): Simon Commission[cite: 2]; Martyrdom of Bhagat Singh (1931)[cite: 2]; Dandi Salt March (1930)[cite: 2]; Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)[cite: 2].
  • Lord Mountbatten (1947): June 3rd Partition Plan[cite: 2]; Radcliffe Boundary Commission[cite: 2]; 1st Governor-General of free India[cite: 2].

Essential Exam Traps & Memory Mnemonics

  • TRAP 1 Dyer vs O'Dwyer: Reginald Dyer ordered firing at Jallianwala Bagh (1919)[cite: 1, 2]. Michael O'Dwyer was the Lt. Governor of Punjab assassinated by Udham Singh in London (1940)[cite: 1, 2].
  • TRAP 2 Ghadar Leadership: Founder/Gen Sec was Lala Har Dayal, but the 1st President was Baba Sohan Singh Bhakna[cite: 1, 2].
  • TRAP 3 Awadh Annexation: Awadh was annexed by Dalhousie in 1856 on charges of misgovernance (Sleeman/Outram report), NOT under Doctrine of Lapse[cite: 2]!
  • TRAP 4 Communal Electorates: Muslims given separate electorates in 1909[cite: 2]; Sikhs, Anglo-Indians, and Christians given separate electorates in 1919[cite: 2].
MNEMONIC: Sequence of 1920-30s Pacts (S-N-L-C-G-P)[cite: 2]

Simon (1927/28) → Nehru Report (1928) → Lahore Purna Swaraj (1929) → CDM/Salt March (1930) → Gandhi-Irwin (1931) → Poona Pact (1932)[cite: 2].
