1.1 The Supreme Court of India (Part V, Articles 124–147)

Establishment, Composition & Appointments:

  • Inauguration: 28th January, 1950 (succeeded the Federal Court established under the GOI Act, 1935 in 1937).
  • Strength: Maximum possible strength is 34 Judges (Chief Justice of India + 33 other Judges).
  • Article 124(2) — Appointment: Every Judge of the SC is appointed by the President of India after consultation with such judges of the SC and High Courts as the President deems necessary. For appointing other judges, consulting the CJI is obligatory.
  • Collegium System: Evolved through the Judges Cases (1982–1998); the CJI recommends names in consultation with a collegium of the 4 senior-most judges of the SC. (Convention of seniority was bypassed in 1973 for Justice A.N. Ray and in 1975 for Justice M.H. Beg).
  • Article 124(3) — Qualifications: Citizen of India AND (a) Judge of a High Court (or two/more in succession) for at least 5 years, OR (b) Advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years, OR (c) a distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President.
  • Oath, Tenure & Salary: Oath administered by the President. Holds office until 65 years of age. Resigns in writing to the President. Salaries determined by Parliament by law (charged on Consolidated Fund of India; CJI: ₹2.80 Lakh/month, Judges: ₹2.50 Lakh/month; 50% pension). Cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment except during a Financial Emergency (Art. 360).
  • Article 124(4) — Removal: Removed by an order of the President only after an address is passed by each House of Parliament in the same session on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity, supported by a special majority (majority of total membership of that House + not less than 2/3rds of members present and voting). (Unsuccessful motions: Justice V. Ramaswami in 1991; Justice Soumitra Sen in 2011 resigned before Lok Sabha vote).
  • Bar on Practice (Rule of Plead): A retired SC judge cannot plead or act in any court or before any authority within the territory of India.
  • Ad-hoc & Retired Judges: Under Art. 127 (lack of quorum) and Art. 128 (retired judges), the CJI can request a judge to sit with the prior consent of the President.

Jurisdictions & Constitutional Powers (Exam Hotspots):

  • Article 129 — Court of Record: SC judgements are recorded for perpetual memory and testimony, carry evidentiary value, and empower the SC to punish for contempt of court.
  • Article 131 — Exclusive Original Jurisdiction (Repeated in Shifts 11, 19, 22): The SC hears directly (without going to lower courts first) any federal dispute:
    1. Between the Government of India (Union) and one or more States;
    2. Between the Union and any State(s) on one side and one or more States on the other;
    3. Between two or more States.
    Exclusions: Pre-constitutional treaties/covenants with princely states, inter-state water disputes (Art. 262—e.g., SYL canal disputes), and private citizen suits against the Union/State.
  • Article 32 — Writ Jurisdiction: Original (but not exclusive) jurisdiction to issue 5 writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto) strictly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
  • Appellate Jurisdiction (Shift 8): Hears appeals from High Courts in Constitutional (Art. 132), Civil (Art. 133), and Criminal (Art. 134) matters, plus Special Leave Petition (SLP) under Art. 136.
  • Article 137 — Review of Judgements: Power of SC to review its own judgements or orders.
  • Article 141 — Law Declared by SC Binding (Shift 16): The law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within the territory of India (not merely High Courts or executive bodies; SC itself is not bound by its own prior rulings and can overrule them).
  • Article 143 — Advisory Jurisdiction: President can refer any question of law or fact of public importance to the SC. Neither is the SC bound to give advice (in general matters), nor is the President bound to accept such advice.
  • Exam Trap (Shift 4): Settling federal disputes, protecting FRs, and interpreting the Constitution are core SC functions; supervising state legislature sessions is NOT a function of the SC.

1.2 Judicial Review vs. Judicial Activism & Public Interest Litigation (PIL) (Shifts 5, 11, 12, 15, 17, 18, 20)

Dimension Judicial Review (Arts. 13, 32, 136, 226) Judicial Activism & PIL
Core Definition & Distinction (Shift 11 & 12) The constitutional power of the SC and High Courts to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive actions. If found violative of the Constitution (ultra vires), the court strikes them down as null and void. Proactive role played by the judiciary where courts go beyond traditional dispute resolution to proactively interpret the Constitution to address societal issues, fill legislative vacuums, and direct executive policy implementation.
Trigger Scenario (Shifts 15 & 17) Exercised when a law is challenged as violating a Fundamental Right, being repugnant to constitutional provisions, or outside the competence of the framing authority. Illustrated when a court directs the state government to implement new environmental regulations or social welfare measures to protect citizens' rights.
Constitutional Restoration (Shift 18) Curtailed during the Emergency by the 42nd Amendment Act (1976); restored to the SC and High Courts by the 43rd Amendment Act (1977). Part of the Basic Structure. Expanded rapidly in the late 1970s and 1980s through the relaxation of the rule of Locus Standi via Public Interest Litigation (PIL) (also called Social Action Litigation - SAL).
Criticism & Pioneers (Shifts 5 & 20) Borrowed from the US Constitution. Balances Parliamentary Sovereignty with Judicial Supremacy. Common Criticism (Shift 5): It may encroach upon the legislative and executive domains (violating separation of powers / judicial overreach).
PIL Pioneers: Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer (Mumbai Kamgar Sabha vs. Abdulbhai, 1976) & Justice P.N. Bhagwati; Hussainara Khatoon vs. State of Bihar (1979) on undertrial prisoners. Filed under Art. 32 (SC), Art. 226 (HC), or Sec. 133 CrPC (Magistrate).

1.3 High Courts (Part VI, Articles 214–231)

  • Origin & Total HCs: Originated in 1862 (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras). Currently 25 High Courts in India. The 7th Amendment Act, 1956 authorized Parliament to establish a common High Court for two or more states/UTs (e.g., Punjab & Haryana High Court at Chandigarh, originally established on 8th Nov 1947, having jurisdiction over Punjab, Haryana, and UT Chandigarh).
  • Article 214: There shall be a High Court for each State.
  • Article 215 — Court of Record (Shift 3): Every High Court shall be a Court of Record and shall have all the powers of such a court including the power to punish for contempt of itself.
  • Article 217 — Appointment & Tenure (Shift 13): Appointed by the President after consultation with the Chief Justice of India (CJI) and the Governor of the State (plus the Chief Justice of the concerned HC for puisne judges). Holds office until 62 years of age. Resigns to the President; removed by the President in the same manner as an SC Judge.
  • Qualifications: Citizen of India + held a judicial office in India for 10 years OR been an advocate of a High Court for 10 years. (Note: Unlike the SC, there is NO "distinguished jurist" clause for HC appointment).
  • Article 226 — Writ Jurisdiction (Shift 6): Authorizes High Courts to issue writs not only for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights but also for "any other purpose" (ordinary legal rights). Thus, HC's writ jurisdiction is wider in scope than the SC's under Art. 32.
  • Article 227 — Power of Superintendence (Shifts 8 & 19): Every High Court has superintendence over all courts and tribunals throughout its territorial jurisdiction (except military courts/tribunals). This power covers both judicial and administrative matters (e.g., directing subordinate courts to follow a revised procedure for filing evidence, calling for returns, prescribing forms, and settling fee tables).
  • Article 228: Transfer of certain cases to HC if they involve a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution.

1.4 Subordinate Courts & Family Courts (Arts. 233–237)

  • Primary Function (Shift 9): To resolve civil and criminal cases at the district level.
  • Article 233 — Appointment of District Judges (Shifts 5, 6, 16): Appointments, postings, and promotions of District Judges in any State are made by the Governor of the State in consultation with the High Court exercising jurisdiction in relation to such State.
    • Eligibility: (1) Must not already be in the service of the Union or State; (2) Must have been an advocate or pleader for at least 7 years; (3) Must be recommended by the High Court.
  • Article 234 — Recruitment of Other Judicial Officers: Made by the Governor in accordance with rules made by him after consultation with the State Public Service Commission (SPSC) and the High Court.
  • Article 235 — Control over Subordinate Courts (Shifts 17 & 18): Control over district courts and courts subordinate thereto (including posting, promotion, and grant of leave to persons belonging to the judicial service holding posts inferior to District Judge) is vested squarely in the High Court.
  • Article 236 (Interpretation): "District Judge" includes Judge of a City Civil Court, Additional/Joint/Assistant District Judge, Chief Judge of a Small Cause Court, Chief Presidency Magistrate, Sessions Judge, Additional/Assistant Sessions Judge.
  • Death Sentence Confirmation (Shift 18): While a Sessions Judge can award capital punishment (death sentence) in heinous criminal cases, the High Court is the final judicial authority required to confirm a death sentence awarded by the Sessions Court before it can be executed (Sec. 366 CrPC / Sec. 407 BNSS).
  • Family Courts Act, 1984 (Shift 9): Enacted to promote conciliation and speedy settlement of marriage and family disputes. State Governments must set up Family Courts (mandatory in cities/towns with population exceeding 10 lakh) in consultation with the HC. Primary Jurisdiction: Suits for dissolution of marriage (divorce), restitution of conjugal rights, nullity of marriage, judicial separation, maintenance, and custody/guardianship of minors.
  • Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008: Came into force on 2nd October, 2009. Presided over by a Nyayadhikari (First Class Judicial Magistrate level) appointed by the State Govt in consultation with the HC. Tries mobile/village-level civil and criminal disputes; not bound by strict rules of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, but guided by principles of natural justice.

1.5 Lok Adalats & NALSA (Shifts 1, 4, 7, 10, 11, 15, 17, 20, 21)

  • Constitutional Foundation (Shifts 4, 11, 15): Article 39A (inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976) directs the State to secure equal justice and free legal aid by suitable legislation or schemes to ensure that opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other disabilities.
  • Statutory Authority (Shift 7): Lok Adalats and NALSA are constituted under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (enforced on 9th November, 1995; amended in 1994 & 2002).
  • National Supervision (Shift 1): The National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) supervises and monitors the organization of Lok Adalats and legal aid programmes at the national level.
    • Patron-in-Chief of NALSA: Chief Justice of India (CJI).
    • Executive Chairman of NALSA: Senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court.
    • Hierarchy: SCLSC (Supreme Court), HCLSC (High Court), SLSA (State—headed by CJ of HC as Patron-in-Chief), DLSA (District—headed by District Judge), and Taluka Legal Services Committee.
  • Historical Milestones (Shift 17):
    • First Lok Adalat camp in post-independence India was held on 14th March, 1982 at Una (Junagadh), Gujarat.
    • First modern formal Lok Adalat organized in 1986 was held in Chennai (Madras).
  • Composition: Presided over by a sitting or retired judicial officer as Chairman, with two other members (usually a lawyer and a social worker).
  • Function & Jurisdiction (Shifts 10 & 20): An Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) forum ("People's Court") that settles both pending court cases and pre-litigation disputes through amicable compromise and conciliation without formal court procedures or court fees.
    Crucial Bar: Lok Adalats have NO jurisdiction over criminal cases involving non-compoundable offences.
  • Legal Status of Award (Shift 21): Every award of a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a decree of a Civil Court and is final and binding on all the parties to the dispute. NO appeal lies to any court against the award of a Lok Adalat.
  • Permanent Lok Adalats (2002 Amendment): Set up for Public Utility Services (transport, postal, power, water, sanitation, insurance, banking) with pecuniary jurisdiction up to ₹1 Crore; can decide disputes on merit if parties fail to reach a compromise.

1.6 Tribunals (Part XIV-A, Arts. 323A & 323B) (Shifts 2, 5, 13, 14, 15, 17, 22)

  • Constitutional Insertion (Shifts 5 & 13): Original Constitution had no provisions for tribunals. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (on Swaran Singh Committee recommendations) inserted a new Part XIV-A titled "Tribunals" consisting of two articles: Article 323A and Article 323B.
  • Core Purpose & Distinction from Regular Courts (Shifts 14, 15, 22): Tribunals are quasi-judicial bodies constituted for specialised branches of law to provide specialized, speedy, and technical adjudication on specific legal/administrative disputes, reducing the burden on regular courts. They follow principles of natural justice rather than rigid CPC/Evidence Act rules.
  • Article 323A — Administrative Tribunals (Shift 2):
    • Empowers ONLY Parliament (not state legislatures) to establish administrative tribunals.
    • Subject Matter: Adjudication of disputes and complaints regarding recruitment and conditions of service of persons appointed to public services and posts in connection with the affairs of the Union, States, or local/other authorities.
    • Statutory Act: Parliament enacted the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985, establishing the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) and State Administrative Tribunals (SATs).
  • Article 323B — Tribunals for Other Matters (Shift 17):
    • Empowers both Parliament and State Legislatures (within their legislative competence) to establish tribunals for other specified matters.
    • Subjects Covered: (a) Levy, assessment, collection, and enforcement of any tax; (b) Foreign exchange, import and export; (c) Industrial and labour disputes (Shift 17); (d) Land reforms under Art. 31A; (e) Ceiling on urban property; (f) Elections to Parliament and State Legislatures; (g) Foodstuffs; (h) Rent and tenancy rights.
  • Judicial Review over Tribunals (L. Chandra Kumar Case, 1997): The SC struck down clauses of Arts. 323A(2)(d) and 323B(3)(d) that excluded the jurisdiction of High Courts. Appeals against orders of CAT/tribunals lie before the Division Bench of the concerned High Court under Art. 226/227.

MODULE 2: UNION & STATE EXECUTIVE (PART V & PART VI)

2–3 Qs Per Shift

2.1 Pardoning Powers: President (Art. 72) vs. Governor (Art. 161) (Shifts 1, 2, 3, 4, 9)

Nature of Power (Shift 2): When the President (under Art. 72) or Governor (under Art. 161) suspends, remits, or commutes a sentence, they are exercising a Judicial Power (or quasi-judicial function conferred on the executive to correct judicial errors). They do not sit as a court of appeal or conduct retrials personally.

Category of Sentence President of India (Article 72) Governor of a State (Article 161)
1. Union vs. State Laws (Shift 9) Can pardon sentences for offences against Union Laws (matters to which Union executive power extends).
Exam Trap (Shift 9): President DOES NOT have power to grant pardon for offences under State laws EXCEPT in cases of a Death Sentence.		 Can grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions for offences against State Laws (matters to which the executive power of the State extends).
2. Court Martial / Military Courts (Shift 1) YES. Empowered to grant pardon, reprieve, respite, suspension, remission, or commutation in respect of sentences by a Court Martial (military court). NO (Beyond Authority — Shift 1). The Governor has NO power to pardon or commute sentences inflicted by a Court Martial.
3. Sentence of Death (Shift 3) Exclusive Authority to Pardon Death Sentence: Can grant pardon, remission, or commute a death sentence passed by a High Court/Sessions Court under both Union and State laws. Under Art. 161, the Governor can suspend, remit, or commute a death sentence under state law, but cannot grant a complete Pardon for a death sentence (Note: In 2021, SC in Pyare Lal case clarified Sec. 433A CrPC does not fetter Art. 161 remission, though complete pardon of death remains Presidential).
5 Key Terms Defined: (1) Pardon: Completely absolves offender from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications; (2) Commutation: Substitutes one form of punishment for a lighter form (e.g., Death to Rigorous Imprisonment); (3) Remission: Reduces the period/duration of sentence without changing its character (e.g., 7 yrs RI to 3 yrs RI); (4) Respite: Awards a lesser sentence due to a special fact (e.g., pregnancy or physical disability); (5) Reprieve: Temporary stay on the execution of a sentence (especially death) pending a mercy petition.

2.2 Conduct of Business, Council of Ministers & Executive Articles (Shifts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 16, 18, 19, 20, 22)

Constitutional Provision Union Executive (Part V: Arts. 52–78) State Executive (Part VI: Arts. 153–167)
Head of State & Executive Power (Shift 2) Art. 52: There shall be a President of India.
Art. 53: Executive power of the Union is vested in the President (Supreme Commander of Armed Forces under Art. 53(2)).		 Art. 153 (Shift 2): There shall be a Governor for each State (7th Amendment, 1956 allows the same person to be appointed Governor for two or more States).
Art. 154: Executive power of State vested in Governor.
Art. 155: Appointed by President.
Aid & Advice of Council of Ministers (Shifts 9, 19, 22) Art. 74(1) (Shift 22): CoM headed by the PM to aid and advise the President, who shall act in accordance with such advice (made binding by 42nd Amendment; 44th Amendment allowed President to return advice once for reconsideration, after which it is binding).
Art. 74(2) (Shift 9): Whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall NOT be inquired into in any court.		 Art. 163(1) (Shifts 7 & 19): CoM with CM at the head to aid and advise the Governor, except in so far as he is required to exercise his functions in his discretion.
Policy Disagreement (Shift 19): Governor can return advice once for reconsideration, or exercise constitutional discretion when no party has a clear majority (Shift 7).
Appointment, 15% Cap & Collective Responsibility (Shifts 3, 6, 7, 8, 11, 16, 18, 19, 20) Art. 75(1) (Shifts 6 & 8): PM is appointed by the President; other Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the PM.
Art. 75(1A) (Shift 18 — 91st Amendment, 2003): Total Ministers, including PM, shall not exceed 15% of total strength of the Lok Sabha (House of the People).
Art. 75(3) (Shift 16): CoM is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. If a Minister publicly disagrees with a Cabinet decision, he must resign.		 Art. 164(1) (Shifts 7 & 11): CM is appointed by the Governor; other Ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the CM.
Art. 164(1A) (Shift 3 — 91st Amendment, 2003): Total Ministers, including CM, shall not exceed 15% of total strength of the State Legislative Assembly (minimum 12 Ministers in smaller states).
Art. 164(2) & (3) (Shifts 19 & 20): Collectively responsible to the Assembly. If CM loses assembly confidence and refuses to resign, Governor must dismiss the CoM. Oath (Art. 164(3)): Administered by the Governor.
Chief Law Officer (Shift 8) Art. 76 — Attorney General of India (Shift 8): Appointed by President; must be qualified to be an SC Judge; holds office during pleasure of President. Has right of audience in all courts and right to speak/participate in Parliament (and committees) without the right to vote (Art. 88). Art. 165 — Advocate General for the State: Highest law officer in the state; appointed by the Governor; must be qualified to be appointed a High Court Judge; holds office during pleasure of the Governor.
Conduct of Business (Shifts 1, 4, 7) Art. 77 (Shift 1): Deals with the conduct of business of the Government of India.
Art. 77(1) (Shifts 4 & 7): All executive actions of the Government of India shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the President of India.		 Art. 166: Conduct of business of the Government of a State. All executive actions of the State Government shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the Governor.
Duties of PM & CM as Channel of Communication (Shifts 7, 10, 14) Art. 78 (Shift 14): Duty of the Prime Minister to: (a) communicate to the President all decisions of the CoM relating to administration and proposals for legislation; (b) furnish such information as the President may call for; (c) if the President so requires, submit for the consideration of the CoM any matter on which a decision has been taken by a Minister but which has not been considered by the Council. Art. 167 (Shifts 7 & 10): CM acts as the chief channel of communication between the State CoM and the Governor.
Exam Trap (Shift 10): CM must submit an individual Minister's decision to the CoM only if the Governor so requires—it is NOT a duty to submit every individual Minister's decision to the CoM before implementation.

2.3 Administration of Union Territories (Shifts 4, 9, 13, 15, 21)

  • Part VIII (Articles 239–241): Deals with Union Territories (currently 8 UTs).
  • Article 239 (Shift 4): Every UT is administered by the President through an Administrator. Under Art. 239(2), the President may appoint the Governor of a State as the Administrator of an adjoining Union Territory, and where a Governor is so appointed, he exercises his functions as Administrator independently of his State Council of Ministers (Direct Punjab-Chandigarh relevance: The Governor of Punjab acts as the Administrator of UT Chandigarh).
  • Executive Action in UTs (Shift 9): All executive actions relating to the administration of a Union Territory are expressed to be taken in the name of the President of India.
  • UTs with Elected Assemblies & CMs (Shift 15): Only three UTs currently have their own elected Legislative Assemblies and Chief Ministers: Delhi (Art. 239AA — 69th Amendment, 1991), Puducherry (Art. 239A — 14th Amendment, 1962), and Jammu & Kashmir. (CMs of Delhi and Puducherry are appointed by the President, not the Lt. Governor).
  • Legislative Status of UTs vs. States (Shift 21): Unlike States, Parliament retains plenary legislative authority over all Union Territories (on all three lists, including State List subjects), even in UTs with elected legislative assemblies.
  • J&K & Ladakh (Shift 13): The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 (effective 31 Oct 2019) bifurcated the erstwhile state into two UTs: UT of J&K (with legislature) and UT of Ladakh (without legislature).

2.4 Vice-President, Ordinance Power & Emergency Triggers

  • Vice-President (Arts. 63–71): Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art. 64). Elected by an Electoral College consisting of all members (both elected and nominated) of both Houses of Parliament (State MLAs do NOT participate). Removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority (majority of all the then members) and agreed to by the Lok Sabha by a simple majority (Art. 67(b)).
  • Ordinance Making Power:
    • President (Art. 123): Promulgated when either or both Houses of Parliament are not in session. Must be approved within 6 weeks of reassembly (maximum life if not approved: 6 months + 6 weeks).
    • Governor (Art. 213): Promulgated when the State Legislature is not in session (also 6 weeks approval window after reassembly).
  • National Emergency Written Decision (Shift 21 — Art. 352): Inserted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978: The President can issue a Proclamation of National Emergency ONLY after the written decision of the Union Cabinet (PM + Cabinet Ministers—the only place in the Constitution where the term "Cabinet" is explicitly used) is communicated to him.
  • State Consolidated Fund (Shift 22 — Art. 266): Includes tax revenues collected by the state, loans raised by the state government, and grants-in-aid from the Centre. Voluntary public donations to the Chief Minister's Relief Fund are NOT part of the State Consolidated Fund.

MODULE 3: PARLIAMENT & STATE LEGISLATURE (PARTS V & VI)

2–3 Qs Per Shift

3.1 State Legislature: Articles 168, 169, 170 & 171 (Shifts 1, 6, 8, 10, 11, 14, 16, 18, 20)

Constitution, Creation/Abolition & Vidhan Sabha:

  • Article 168 — Constitution of Legislatures in States (Shift 1): For every State there shall be a Legislature which shall consist of the Governor, and (a) in 6 bicameral states (Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana), two Houses (Legislative Council & Legislative Assembly), and (b) in other states (including Punjab), one House (Legislative Assembly).
  • Article 169 — Abolition or Creation of Legislative Councils (Shifts 16 & 20):
    1. The State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) must pass a resolution to that effect by a Special Majority (majority of total membership of the Assembly + not less than 2/3rds of members present and voting).
    2. Parliament then passes an Act by a Simple Majority (under Art. 169(3), such a law is NOT deemed a constitutional amendment under Art. 368).
    3. Punjab Fact: The Punjab Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) was abolished in 1970 via the Punjab Legislative Council (Abolition) Act, 1969.
  • Article 170 — Composition of Vidhan Sabha (Shifts 6, 8, 18):
    • Consists of between 60 and 500 members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the State (Exceptions via Art. 371: Sikkim 32, Goa 40, Mizoram 40, Puducherry 30; Punjab has 117 seats).
    • Eligibility (Art. 173 — Shift 6): Must be a citizen of India, at least 25 years of age (30 years for Legislative Council), and registered as a voter.
    • Article 174 (Shift 14): The Governor is empowered to issue formal orders for summoning, proroguing, or dissolving the State Legislative Assembly.

Article 171 — Composition of Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) (Shifts 10, 11, 18):

Total strength: Maximum 1/3rd of the total strength of the State Legislative Assembly, and minimum 40 members. It is a permanent body (1/3rd members retire every 2 years; 6-year term). Members are chosen via a mix of indirect election (proportional representation by single transferable vote) and nomination:

Fraction Electorate / Mode of Selection (Art. 171(3))
1/3rd Elected by electorates consisting of members of local bodies (Municipalities, District Boards, etc.) in the State.
1/3rd Elected by the members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) of the State from amongst persons who are not members of the Assembly.
1/12th Elected by graduates of 3 years' standing residing in the State.
1/12th Elected by teachers of at least 3 years' standing in educational institutions not lower than secondary schools in the State.
1/6th Nominated by the Governor (NOT 1/4th — Shift 18 trap!) from persons having special knowledge/practical experience in Literature, Science, Art, Cooperative Movement, and Social Service. (Note: Cooperative Movement is extra compared to President's Rajya Sabha nominations).

3.2 Legislative Process, Money Bills vs. Ordinary Bills & Lapsing of Bills (Shifts 5, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16)

Ordinary Bills, Money Bills & Financial Proposals:

  • Parliament Composition (Art. 79): President + Council of States (Rajya Sabha, Art. 80) + House of the People (Lok Sabha, Art. 81—directly elected by the people, Shift 19). (Punjab Parliamentary Seats: 13 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha).
  • Freedom of Speech in Parliament (Art. 105(1) — Shift 14): Expressly made subject to the provisions of the Constitution and to the rules and standing orders regulating the procedure of Parliament.
  • Stages of an Ordinary Bill (Shifts 5, 8, 13, 16):
    • Following introduction (First Reading), during the Second Reading, the Member in-charge may move a procedurally valid motion that the bill be: (a) taken into consideration immediately, (b) referred to a Select Committee of the House (Shift 5), (c) referred to a Joint Committee of both Houses, or (d) circulated for eliciting public opinion.
    • Before voting on passage, discussion and debate among members takes place (Shift 16).
    • Amendments by Other House (Shift 8): If the second House passes an ordinary bill with amendments, the originating House must approve the amendments passed by the other House before the bill can proceed for Presidential assent (Art. 111 — Shift 13). In case of deadlock (6 months), the President can summon a Joint Sitting under Art. 108 (presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker). (No Joint Sitting in State Legislatures; Assembly can override Council after 3 months + 1 month under Art. 197).
  • Money Bills (Art. 109 & Art. 110 — Shift 10): Introduced only in Lok Sabha on the prior recommendation of the President. Speaker's certification is final. Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill; it must return it within 14 days. If Rajya Sabha does not return a Money Bill within 14 days, the bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form originally passed by Lok Sabha and sent to the President.
  • Demand for a Grant (Art. 113(3) — Shift 15): A financial proposal for a demand for a grant can be considered by Parliament only on the recommendation of the President (and voted upon exclusively by the Lok Sabha).

Article 107(4) & (5) — Lapsing of Bills on Dissolution of Lok Sabha (Shift 10):

This is a classic high-yield trap (analogous to Art. 196 for State Legislatures). When the Lok Sabha is dissolved:

Status of the Bill at Dissolution Fate
1. Bill pending in the Lok Sabha (whether originating in LS or transmitted to it by RS). LAPSES
2. Bill passed by the Lok Sabha but pending in the Rajya Sabha. LAPSES
3. Article 107(4) (Shift 10): Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) which has NOT been passed by the Lok Sabha (House of the People). DOES NOT LAPSE
4. Bill not passed by the two Houses due to disagreement, and the President has notified the holding of a Joint Sitting (Art. 108) before the dissolution of LS. DOES NOT LAPSE
5. Bill passed by both Houses and pending assent of the President (or returned by the President for reconsideration). DOES NOT LAPSE

3.3 Parliamentary Committees (Shifts 7, 9, 12, 17)

Primary Role of Parliamentary Standing Committees (Shifts 7, 9, 12): They scrutinize and examine bills, budgets, legislative proposals, and government policies in detail before they are discussed and voted on in the full House. (They do not pass laws independently, enforce judicial rulings, or approve the budget directly).

1. Estimates Committee (Shift 17)

  • Rule of Procedure (Shift 17): Established under Rule 310 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.
  • Origin: First constituted in 1950 on the recommendation of Finance Minister John Mathai.
  • Strength: 30 Members — ALL from Lok Sabha only (Rajya Sabha has no representation).
  • Chairman: Appointed by the Speaker invariably from the ruling party.
  • Function: Examines budget estimates and suggests 'economies' in public expenditure ("Continuous Economy Committee").

2. Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

  • Rule of Procedure: Governed by Rule 308 of Lok Sabha Rules.
  • Origin: Oldest committee; set up in 1921 under the GOI Act, 1919.
  • Strength: 22 Members (15 from Lok Sabha + 7 from Rajya Sabha), elected annually by proportional representation.
  • Chairman: Appointed by the Speaker; since 1967, by convention, always from the Opposition.
  • Function: Examines annual audit reports of the CAG (CAG acts as the "friend, philosopher and guide" of the PAC).

3. Committee on Public Undertakings

  • Rule of Procedure: Governed by Rule 312A of Lok Sabha Rules.
  • Origin: Created in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee.
  • Strength: 22 Members (15 from Lok Sabha + 7 from Rajya Sabha).
  • Chairman: Appointed by the Speaker from amongst the Lok Sabha members only.
  • Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs): 24 Committees (16 under LS, 8 under RS), each having 31 members (21 LS + 10 RS).

MODULE 5: CITIZENSHIP, FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS, DPSP & FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES

2–3 Qs Per Shift

5.1 Citizenship: Part II (Articles 5–11) & Citizenship Act, 1955 (Shifts 2, 5, 10, 15, 17)

Constitutional Provisions (Part II — Shift 2):

  • Single Citizenship: Borrowed from the UK (British) Constitution.
  • Article 5: Citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution (26 Jan 1950) for persons domiciled in India (born in India, or either parent born in India, or ordinarily resident for 5 years before commencement).
  • Article 6: Citizenship rights of persons who migrated to India from Pakistan (cutoff date: 19th July, 1948).
  • Article 7: Citizenship rights of certain migrants to Pakistan after 1st March, 1947 who later returned to India on resettlement permits.
  • Article 8 (Shift 10): Grants citizenship to persons of Indian origin residing outside India (registered by diplomatic/consular representatives).
  • Article 9 (Shift 15): Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State cease to be citizens of India.
  • Article 10 & Article 11 (Shift 10): Art. 11 empowers Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law (including acquisition and termination of citizenship).

Citizenship Act, 1955 (Shifts 5, 15, 17):

  • Nodal Authority (Shift 17): The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) is the nodal authority responsible for processing applications and granting Indian citizenship.
  • 5 Modes of Acquisition:
    1. By Birth (Sec. 3): Born in India.
    2. By Descent (Sec. 4): Born outside India to Indian parent(s).
    3. By Registration (Sec. 5 — Shift 5): A person of Indian origin (or married to an Indian citizen) who is ordinarily resident in India for at least SEVEN (7) YEARS before making the application for registration.
    4. By Naturalisation (Sec. 6): Ordinarily resident in India for 12 years (11 years aggregate + 12 months immediately preceding application; reduced to 5 years for persecuted minorities from Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and Pakistan under CAA 2019).
    5. By Incorporation of Territory (Sec. 7).
  • 3 Modes of Loss/Termination (Shifts 10 & 15): (1) Renunciation (Sec. 8): Voluntary declaration; (2) Termination (Sec. 9 — Shift 15): Automatic cessation upon voluntarily acquiring the citizenship of another country; (3) Deprivation (Sec. 10): Compulsory termination by Central Govt for fraud, disloyalty, or 2 years' imprisonment abroad within 5 years of registration.

5.2 Fundamental Rights: Part III (Articles 12–35) (Shifts 2, 4, 6, 7, 14, 16, 19, 21, 22)

Borrowed from the USA (Bill of Rights). Called the Magna Carta of India. Meaning of 'Justiciable' (Shift 14): They are enforceable through courts of law (an aggrieved person can directly approach the SC under Art. 32 or HC under Art. 226). Exclusively for Indian Citizens (not foreigners): Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, and 30. Never Suspended during Emergency (Art. 359): Articles 20 and 21.

1. Right to Equality (Articles 14–18):

  • Art. 14: Equality before Law (British—negative concept) & Equal Protection of Laws (US—positive concept).
  • Art. 15 (Shift 19): Prohibits discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. Art. 15(4) & 15(5) (Shift 19) empower the State to make special provisions for the advancement of Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs), SCs, and STs (and women/children under Art. 15(3); EWS under Art. 15(6)).
  • Art. 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment.
  • Art. 17: Abolition of Untouchability.
  • Art. 18 — Abolition of Titles (Shift 4): Abolishes titles (except military or academic distinctions). Art. 18(4) (Shift 4): "No person holding any office of profit or trust under the State shall, without the consent of the President, accept any present, emolument, or office of any kind from or under any foreign State."

2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22):

  • Art. 19 (Shifts 2 & 21): Guarantees SIX (6) Fundamental Freedoms: (a) Speech & Expression (includes Freedom of the Press — Shift 21: banning a newspaper solely for criticizing government policies violates Art. 19(1)(a)), (b) Peaceful assembly without arms, (c) Form associations/unions/cooperatives, (d) Move freely throughout India, (e) Reside and settle in any part of India, (g) Practise any profession/trade. (Art. 19(1)(f) Property deleted by 44th CAA).
  • Art. 20: Protection in respect of conviction for offences: (1) No ex-post-facto criminal law, (2) No Double Jeopardy, (3) No Self-Incrimination.
  • Art. 21 & Art. 21A (Shift 16): Protection of Life & Personal Liberty (Art. 21); Right to Free and Compulsory Education for children aged 6–14 years (Art. 21A — 86th CAA, 2002).
  • Art. 22: Protection against arrest and detention (produce before magistrate within 24 hours; preventive detention safeguards).

3. Exploitation (23–24) & 4. Religion (25–28):

  • Art. 23: Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labour (begar).
  • Art. 24: Prohibition of employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines, or hazardous jobs.
  • Arts. 25–28: Freedom of conscience & free profession, practice, and propagation of religion (25); Manage religious affairs (26); Freedom from taxes for promotion of any religion (27); Freedom from religious instruction in state-funded educational institutions (28).

5. Cultural/Educational (29–30) & 6. Remedies (32):

  • Arts. 29 & 30 (Shift 21): Provide cultural and educational rights (including the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice under Art. 30) to both Religious AND Linguistic Minorities.
  • Art. 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies (Shift 21): Described by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the 'very heart and soul of the Constitution'. Empowers SC to issue 5 writs.

5.3 Directive Principles (Part IV, Arts. 36–51) (Shifts 3, 4, 6, 11, 12, 13, 15, 20, 22)

  • Source & Nature (Shifts 3 & 6): Enshrined in Part IV (Articles 36 to 51). Borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Ireland). Aim to establish a Welfare State (social and economic democracy). Non-justiciable in nature (Art. 37).
  • High-Frequency Articles Tested Across Shifts:
    • Article 39A (Shifts 4, 11, 15 — Socialist): Equal justice and free legal aid to ensure justice is not denied due to economic or other disabilities (42nd CAA; basis of Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987).
    • Article 40 (Shift 4 — Gandhian): Directs the State to take steps to organise Village Panchayats as units of self-government.
    • Article 41 (Shift 20 — Socialist): Right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness, and disablement.
    • Article 42 & 43: Just/humane work conditions & maternity relief (42); Living wage & cottage industries (43); Workers' participation in industry (43A—42nd CAA); Cooperative societies (43B—97th CAA).
    • Article 44 (Liberal-Intellectual): Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens.
    • Article 47 (Shift 13 — Gandhian): Duty of State to raise nutrition/standard of living and prohibit intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health (classified under Gandhian Principles).
    • Article 48 (Shift 22 — Liberal/Gandhian): Organize agriculture and animal husbandry on modern and scientific lines, and prohibit slaughter of cows, calves, and other milch and draught cattle.
    • Article 48A (Shifts 3 & 12 — 42nd CAA): Protect and improve the environment and safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country.
    • Article 50 & 51: Separation of Judiciary from Executive (Art. 50); Promotion of international peace and security (Art. 51).

5.4 Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Art. 51A) (Shifts 5, 11, 20, 22)

  • Part, Article & Source (Shifts 11 & 20): Enshrined in Part IV-A under Article 51A. Borrowed from the USSR (Soviet Union) Constitution. Applicable only to citizens (not foreigners) and are non-justiciable.
  • Committee & Prime Minister (Shift 22): Added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 on the recommendations of the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee, which was constituted during the tenure of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi. (Swaran Singh Committee recommended 8 duties; 42nd CAA added 10 duties).
  • 11th Fundamental Duty (Shift 5): Added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 under Article 51A(k)—duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of 6 and 14 years.
  • Key Sub-Clauses to Remember (Shift 11):
    • Art. 51A(a): Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
    • Art. 51A(c) (Shift 11): Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
    • Art. 51A(e): Promote harmony and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
    • Art. 51A(g): Protect and improve the natural environment (forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife).
    • Art. 51A(h): Develop scientific temper, humanism, and spirit of inquiry and reform.
    • Art. 51A(i): Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
  • Verma Committee (1999): Identified legal provisions for implementation of Fundamental Duties (e.g., Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971; Wildlife Protection Act, 1972).

6.3 Targeted Punjab Module: Constitutional Evolution, Judiciary & Folk Culture (2% State Weightage)

A. Punjab Constitutional, Administrative & Judicial Facts:

  • PEPSU & Merger: Patiala and East Punjab States Union (PEPSU) was formed on 15 July 1948 as a Part-B State (8 princely states: Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Kapurthala, Faridkot, Malerkotla, Kalsia, Nalagarh; Rajpramukh: Maharaja Yadavindra Singh). Merged into Punjab on 1st November, 1956 via the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
  • Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966: Enacted on the recommendation of the Justice J.C. Shah Commission (1966) (following the Parliamentary Committee headed by Hukam Singh). On 1st November, 1966, linguistic reorganisation created modern Punjab (Punjabi-speaking), Haryana (17th state), transferred hilly areas to Himachal Pradesh, and constituted Chandigarh as a Union Territory and common capital.
  • Legislature & Representation: Unicameral Legislature (Vidhan Parishad abolished in Jan 1970). Vidhan Sabha: 117 Seats (34 reserved for SCs, 0 for STs); Lok Sabha: 13 Seats (4 reserved for SCs); Rajya Sabha: 7 Seats.
  • First Office Bearers of Punjab (1947): First Governor: Sir Chandulal Madhavlal Trivedi; First Chief Minister: Dr. Gopi Chand Bhargava; First Speaker: Sardar Kapur Singh. First Woman CM of Punjab: Rajinder Kaur Bhattal (1996).
  • President's Rule (Art. 356) in Punjab: Punjab was the first state in India where President's Rule was imposed (20th June, 1951, after Dr. Gopi Chand Bhargava resigned). Imposed 8 times in total (longest continuous spell: 1987–1992).
  • Punjab & Haryana High Court: Originally established as the High Court of Judicature at Lahore (1919); post-partition established as the East Punjab High Court at Shimla on 15th August / 8th November, 1947 (First Chief Justice: Justice Ram Lal). Shifted to Chandigarh in 1955 (building designed by Le Corbusier) and renamed the High Court of Punjab and Haryana on 1st November, 1966.
  • Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973): Demanded limiting Central jurisdiction strictly to Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications, and Currency, vesting residuary powers in the states.

B. Punjab Folk Theatre, Dances, Music & Cultural Heritage (Direct Replacement for Haryana 'Saang' Qs):

  • Naqqal & Swang (Folk Theatre): Naqqal is Punjab's signature traditional folk theatre based on satire, wit, and improvisation performed by Naqqals / Bhand troupes (Mirasi community). Bhand Pather / Swang and Ramlila / Raslila form the semi-operatic rural theatre tradition.
  • Dhadi & Kavishri (Ballad Singing): Dhadi Jatha (institucionalized by the 6th Sikh Guru, Guru Hargobind Ji, outside Akal Takht) sings heroic ballads (Vaars) using the Dhadd (hourglass drum) and Sarangi. Kavishri is energetic a cappella heroic poetry recitation originating in the Malwa region.
  • Qissa Tradition (Sufi & Romantic Epics): Heer Ranjha by Waris Shah (Shakespeare of Punjab), Sohni Mahiwal by Fazal Shah, Mirza Sahiban by Peelu, and Sassi Punnu by Hashim Shah. Father of Punjabi Sufi Poetry: Baba Sheikh Farid (verses included in Guru Granth Sahib); Bulleh Shah famous for Kafis.
  • Folk Dances of Punjab:
    • Men's Dances: Bhangra (harvest dance), Jhumar (graceful ecstasy dance from Sandal Bar), Luddi (victory dance), Malwai Giddha (performed by men of Malwa using Chimta, Kato, Bugchu), Dankara / Gatka (martial dance enacted by Guru Hargobind Ji & Guru Gobind Singh Ji).
    • Women's Dances: Giddha (accompanied by Boliyan—couplets), Sammi (ancient tribal dance of Sandal Bar), Kikli (whirling dance in pairs), and Jaago (performed on the night before a wedding carrying a decorated pot with lit oil lamps).
  • Folk Instruments & Crafts: Algoza (paired woodwind flutes), Tumbi (single-stringed instrument popularized by Lal Chand Yamla Jatt), Bugchu, Chimta, Dhol. Phulkari ("flower work" embroidery on Khaddar; Bagh is when the entire base cloth is covered with embroidery; holds GI Tag).
